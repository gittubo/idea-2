How to turn any PDF into a slide deck with Claude

the honest version

What Claude can and cannot do here

Worth setting expectations before you upload anything, because this is where people get frustrated.

It genuinely builds files Claude creates .pptx, .xlsx, .docx and .pdf files you download. Confirmed in Anthropic's own docs, on every plan including Free.
It is a first draft, not a finished deck Structure, content and hierarchy come out strong. Visual polish is yours. Budget 20 minutes of tidying, against the two hours you would have spent building it.
Long PDFs are fine, with a catch Up to 1,000 pages upload. Claude reads text and visuals up to 100 pages; past that it reads the text only, so charts in a 400-page report need calling out separately.
Images do not carry across Claude writes the slides, it does not lift the charts out of your PDF. Ask it to leave a placeholder note on any slide that needs the original figure pasted in.
setup

Turn on file creation, once

This takes about 30 seconds and you only ever do it one time. Without it Claude gives you slide text in the chat instead of a file you can download.

  1. 🔓 Switch the setting on. In Claude on web or desktop, open Settings, then Capabilities, and switch on "Code execution and file creation". On mobile it is your name in the sidebar, then Settings, then Capabilities. On Team and Enterprise plans an owner turns it on for the whole organisation under Organization settings, then Capabilities. Anthropic's page on it is here.
  2. 💳 Check nothing else is needed. There is no upgrade gate on this one. File creation is available on Free, Pro, Max, Team and Enterprise. If you are on Free, expect to hit usage limits on a very long document sooner, so do the big reports in one focused sitting.
  3. 📎 Have your PDF ready. Limits are 500MB per file, up to 20 files in one chat, and 1,000 pages per PDF, per the upload docs. Messy scans, annual reports, research papers, meeting minutes, all fine.

The exact prompt

Upload the PDF first, wait for it to finish processing, then paste this. Fill in the four bracketed bits, they are the difference between a generic deck and one you can actually present.

📊 turn this PDF into a deck 
Read this entire document and build me a slide deck from it as a downloadable .pptx file.

My audience is [who is in the room, for example "our leadership team, who have not read the report and have 20 minutes"], and the one thing I need them to walk away with is [the single decision, action or belief you want]. Target [12] slides, plus a title slide and a closing slide. Anything that does not serve that one takeaway gets cut, even if the report spends pages on it.

Structure it properly: a title slide, a one-slide executive summary that could stand alone if someone only read that, then the body organised by argument rather than by the document's own chapter order, then a closing slide with the specific next steps. Each body slide gets a headline that states the point as a full sentence, not a topic label, and a maximum of four supporting lines underneath. Where the document gives a number that matters, put the number on the slide and cite the page it came from.

In the speaker notes for every slide, write what I should actually say out loud, in plain spoken English, about 60 to 90 seconds' worth. Where the document had a chart or table I cannot recreate, add a clearly marked placeholder line telling me which page to pull the original figure from. When you are done, tell me the three weakest slides and what would make them stronger.
The two lines doing the heavy lifting are the audience and the single takeaway. A deck built for "our leadership team who have 20 minutes and need to approve the budget" comes out sharp and ruthless. A deck built with no audience named comes out as a polite summary of the PDF, which is the thing you were trying to avoid.
make it yours

Brand it in the same chat

Once the first version exists, do not start over. Reply in the same chat and Claude rebuilds the file with your styling applied to every slide.

🎨 apply my brand 
Rebuild that deck with my brand styling applied consistently to every slide.

My colours are [#HEX for the background], [#HEX for headings], and [#HEX for the accent or highlight]. My heading font is [font name] and my body font is [font name], and if those exact fonts are not available, pick the closest standard equivalents and tell me which you used so I can swap them later.

Apply it properly rather than only to the title slide: background colour on every slide, headings in the heading colour and font, body text in the body font at a size that stays readable when projected, and the accent colour used only for emphasis, never for whole paragraphs. Keep the margins generous and consistent, left-align the body text, and make sure nothing overflows the slide. Then give me the new .pptx to download.
Do not have your hex codes to hand? Ask Claude to pull them for you first: paste in your logo or a screenshot of your website and ask it to name the three or four hex codes and suggest which is the background, which is the heading and which is the accent. Then feed those into the prompt above.
the fixes

Four follow-ups that fix most decks

The first draft is a starting point. These four replies are the ones I use almost every time, in this order.

  1. ✂️ Cut it down. "This is two slides too long for a 20 minute slot. Merge or cut the weakest two and tell me what you removed and why." Length discipline is the fastest quality win in any deck.
  2. 🗣️ Fix the headlines. "Rewrite every slide headline as a full sentence that states the point, not a topic label. 'Q3 revenue' becomes 'Q3 revenue grew 18 percent, driven entirely by renewals.'" This single pass makes a deck read as an argument instead of a filing cabinet.
  3. 🔢 Pressure-test the numbers. "For every figure on these slides, quote the exact sentence from the source PDF and the page number, so I can check nothing was rounded, mixed up or inferred." Do this before you present anything to anyone who matters.
  4. ❓ Prep the hard questions. "Add an appendix of five slides answering the toughest questions this audience will ask, including the two the report itself does not answer well." The appendix is where you stop being caught out in the room.
get the file

Download it and finish it off

  1. ⬇️ Download the .pptx. Claude posts the file in the chat when it finishes. Click to download, then open it in PowerPoint, Keynote or Google Slides. It is a normal editable file, nothing locked or proprietary.
  2. 👀 Read it once, out loud. Read the speaker notes aloud against the slides. Anything you stumble on is a slide that needs rewriting, and you will catch it in five minutes instead of live in the meeting.
  3. 🖼️ Paste in the real figures. Work through the placeholder lines and drop in the actual charts from the source PDF. Screenshot straight from the document so the data stays exactly as published.
  4. 📐 Fix spacing last. Nudge text boxes, even out the margins and check it on the screen or projector you will actually present on. Cosmetic tidying is the fastest part and the last part.

Other things worth doing with the same file

You have already uploaded the document, so keep the chat open. These take seconds each.

A one-page brief "Turn the same content into a one-page summary as a .docx, written for someone who will never open the deck."
The numbers, in a spreadsheet "Pull every figure and date from the document into an .xlsx with columns for metric, value, period and source page."
An email version "Write the 200-word email I send with this deck, so the recipient knows what to look at and what I need from them."
The devil's advocate pass "Argue against the conclusions in this document. Where is the reasoning thin, and what would change my mind?"
