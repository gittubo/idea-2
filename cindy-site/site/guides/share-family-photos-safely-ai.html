the stakes

Why this actually matters

This is not paranoia, the risk is documented. Barclays projected that "sharenting" (parents oversharing kids online) could account for two-thirds of identity fraud facing young people by 2030, because a name, birthday, and school are enough to start cracking passwords and accounts. Source: APA, June 2026.

The leaks are rarely the photo itself. They are the details around it: a school logo in the background, a house number on the mailbox, a geotag in the file, or a caption that quietly reveals a full name and age. The Australian eSafety Commissioner flags these same background and location details as the real exposure. Source: eSafety Commissioner.

clearing that up

One myth to clear up first

You might have heard "Instagram keeps your photo's GPS location." Here is the honest version: Instagram strips GPS, camera model, and timestamp from the file other people can download from your public post. Source: privacystrip, 2026.

The catch: the ORIGINAL file on your phone still has the location baked in. So if you ever send that original by email, AirDrop, a shared drive, or a DM at full quality, the GPS goes with it. That is why the fix is to strip location at the source, not to trust the platform.
copy this

The prompt that builds your checklist

Paste this into Claude and it will generate a reusable "before I post" checklist you can run through every single time. This is the exact thing SHIELD unlocks.

✂️ build my checklist 
You are my personal privacy filter for social media. Build me a short, repeatable "before I post" checklist I can run through before sharing any photo of my kids or family online. Cover four things: (1) what to physically strip or crop before uploading (location data, street signs, school logos, house numbers, my front door, uniforms, number plates), (2) what personal details a caption or comment can accidentally reveal (full names, birthdays, exact age, school, suburb, routine, home address), (3) the safest account and audience settings to post to, and (4) a quick 5-second sanity check I can do on the spot. Keep it plain, tick-box style, and written so I can screenshot it and reuse it forever.
check the caption

Have Claude review your actual caption

Claude is strongest as a reasoning partner on the TEXT you feed it, so use it to sanity-check your caption before it goes out.

🔍 the caption review 
Here is the caption I am about to post with a family photo: "[paste your caption]". Act as a privacy reviewer. What personal details am I accidentally revealing here (full names, exact age, birthday, school, suburb, routine, or anything that helps someone locate or identify us)? Rewrite it two ways: one warmer and one more private, both keeping the same feeling but removing the risky details.
One important honesty note 🌱: do NOT upload your original, un-stripped photo to Claude (or any AI) just to "check the metadata." AI vision tools do not reliably read GPS out of an image, and uploading the raw original is the exact thing you are trying to avoid. Strip the file first (below), then use Claude for the thinking: the checklist, the caption review, and the settings walk-through.
the 5-second fix

Strip the location before you share it

On iPhone this takes 5 seconds and is the single highest-impact step. When you share, tap the photo, tap Options at the top, and turn Location off before you send, you will see "Location Not Included." To scrub it permanently, open the photo, swipe up or tap the info button, tap Adjust under the map, then No Location. Source: Apple Support.

To stop new photos from ever storing GPS, go to Settings, Privacy & Security, Location Services, Camera and set it to Never. On Android, most gallery apps have a "Remove location" toggle in the share sheet, and you can turn off location tags inside the Camera app settings.

the audience

Lock down who actually sees it

If you want tighter privacy without going private everywhere, Instagram's Close Friends list lets you share Stories to a hand-picked group only. Nobody can see the list, the member count, or who else is on it, and people are not notified when you add or remove them. Set it up in Profile, menu, Close Friends. Source: Instagram Help.

Ask Claude to talk you through the trade-offs:

🔒 compare the settings 
Walk me through the difference between a public account, a private account, and posting to a Close Friends list on Instagram for sharing family photos. For each option, tell me who can see and re-share my posts, what it does and does not protect against, and which one you would recommend if my main goal is keeping my kids' photos out of strangers' and scrapers' hands while still sharing with people I actually know.
go further

3 bonus prompts to run next

1 · the family agreement 
Build me a one-page "family photo rules" agreement I can share with grandparents, aunts, uncles, and friends, covering what they can and cannot post about my kids, so I am not the only one protecting their privacy.
2 · safe birthday captions 
I want to post a birthday photo of my child. Give me 5 caption ideas that celebrate the day without revealing their full name, exact age, birthday date, school, or our suburb.
3 · the footprint audit 
Act as a digital footprint auditor. Ask me 8 questions about what I currently share about my family online, then tell me my top 3 exposure risks and the single easiest fix for each.
how to run it

How to get the most out of it

If you are new to this, run the first prompt once, screenshot the checklist, and keep it in your Notes app so you tick through it before every post. If you already think about privacy, level it up: save the checklist as a reusable Claude Project or a saved prompt so every future caption gets auto-reviewed, and set your Camera to never store location so the strip step is handled before you even open Claude.

the honest bit

When you do not need all of this

Not every photo is high-risk. A landscape, a plate of food, a heavily cropped hand-holding shot, none of that needs the full process. The checklist is for anything that shows a child's face, a recognisable place, a uniform, or a caption with identifying details. The goal is to post on purpose, not to never post again.
