the shape of it

The whole stack on one page

One chef and a few sharp knives beats ten gadgets you used once. The stack has four layers, and only the first one costs me anything. Everything else is either free or already included, and the layers matter more than the logos: if you understand what each layer is for, you can swap any tool inside it later without rebuilding your whole setup.

🧠 The brain Claude does the thinking, writing, planning and analysis. One place, one context, one bill. This is the only layer I deliberately pay for.Free plan to try it, around $20 a month for Pro
👩‍💻 The hands Claude Code turns instructions into real files on your machine: a working site, a script, a renamed folder of 400 photos. Same subscription, different surface.Included with a paid Claude plan
🌱 The memory Claude Projects hold your context, your docs and your rules so you stop re-explaining yourself in every new chat.Free plan included, capped at 5 projects
💡 The renderers Free image and video generators that execute the prompts and shot lists Claude writes. Claude directs, they render. I pay nothing at this layer.$0, on daily free credits
🌱 Why this holds up. The expensive tools in most people's stacks are wrappers: a nice interface around a model, plus a prompt someone else wrote. Once you can write the prompt yourself, you are paying for the interface. Most of this guide is me handing you the prompts.
the five swaps

What you are actually cancelling

These are ordered fastest swap first, deepest change last. Swap one takes you ten minutes and you feel it immediately. Swap five takes an afternoon and quietly changes how every other swap performs, because it is the one that gives Claude your context. Do them in order and do not skip to five.

  1. 1️⃣ Meeting assistants Paste a transcript, get decisions, actions, owners and deadlines back. Ten minutes to swap.
  2. 2️⃣ Email writers Feed Claude three emails you actually wrote, it learns your voice and drafts in it from then on.
  3. 3️⃣ Website builders Claude Code writes the site and opens a live preview while you watch, no templates, no monthly hosting tier.
  4. 4️⃣ Course platforms A 30 day plan with a daily drill, so you practise instead of watching someone else practise.
  5. 5️⃣ Productivity apps A Claude Project that remembers your context, so you stop re-explaining your life every single chat.
swap 1 of 5

Meeting assistants

The paid version of this is a bot that joins your call, records it, and emails you a summary nobody reads. The part you actually wanted was the list of who owes what by when. You can get that from any transcript you already have: Zoom, Teams, Google Meet and most phone recorders will export one, and your phone's voice memo app plus Claude will do it for an in-person meeting.

The trick is asking for the gaps, not just the summary. Most meetings end with two or three tasks that never got an owner or a date, and that is exactly where things quietly die. This prompt makes those visible instead of smoothing over them.

📝 turn a transcript into owners and deadlines 
Here is the raw transcript of a meeting I just had. Pull out every decision that was made, every action item, who owns it, and the deadline if one was said out loud.

If an owner or a date was never actually stated, do not guess: put that item in a short "needs chasing" list at the bottom instead, so I can see exactly what we left vague. Then write me a five line summary I can paste straight into the group chat, and underneath that, a separate two line message for each owner telling them only their own part. Keep the language plain, and never add anything that was not actually said in the room.

[paste the transcript here]
🌱 Make it permanent. Once the output is right, save this as a Claude skill or drop it into a Project's instructions and it runs the same way every time. Anthropic's create custom skills guide walks through it, and it works on the free plan.
swap 2 of 5

Email writers

Every AI email tool sells you the same thing: a draft that sounds like a press release. The reason it sounds wrong is that it has never read anything you wrote. Give Claude three real examples first and the drafts stop sounding like a stranger doing an impression of you.

Pick three emails that are genuinely yours, ideally different types: one you sent to a client, one to a colleague you like, one where you had to say no. Two minutes of copy-pasting buys you every draft after that.

💌 train Claude on your email voice 
I am pasting three emails I have actually written below. Study them and write me a short profile of my email voice: how I open, how long my sentences run, how formal I am, the words and phrases I reach for, and how I sign off.

Then tell me the three habits that make my writing recognisably mine, and the two habits I should probably drop. From now on, whenever I ask you to draft an email, write it in that voice and never fall back on a formal template tone unless I explicitly ask for one. Keep my actual sentence length, do not smooth me out.

Start by drafting this one: [what the email needs to do, who it is going to, and any deadline]. Give me two versions, one warmer and one shorter.

swap 3 of 5

Website builders

This is the swap people do not believe until they watch it happen. Claude Code writes the actual files for your site and opens a live preview in your browser, so you are editing a real page instead of fighting a template editor and a monthly plan. Here is the honest gate first: Claude Code needs a paid Claude plan (Pro, Max, Team or Enterprise) or a Console account, per Anthropic's quickstart. If you are on the free plan, do this swap in normal Claude instead and ask for the full HTML file, then save it yourself.

  1. 👩‍💻 Install it. Open your terminal (on Mac, press Cmd and space, type Terminal, hit enter). On Mac, Linux or WSL, paste curl -fsSL https://claude.ai/install.sh | bash. On Windows PowerShell it is irm https://claude.ai/install.ps1 | iex. Both commands are straight off the official quickstart.
  2. 📁 Make a folder for the site. Type mkdir my-site then cd my-site. That means "make a folder called my-site, then go into it". Everything Claude builds lands here, which means nothing it does can touch the rest of your computer.
  3. ▶️ Start Claude in that folder. Type claude and press enter. The first run asks you to log in with your Claude account in the browser. You now have Claude sitting inside that folder, able to read and write the files in it.
  4. 🎨 Give it the build prompt. Paste the prompt below, filled in. Claude writes the files, then starts a preview and tells you an address like http://localhost:8000 to open in your browser.
  5. 🔁 Talk to it like a designer. Leave the preview open, say what is wrong in plain English ("the hero is too tall, the button colour is muddy, make the spacing calmer") and refresh. Three or four rounds gets you somewhere you would have paid for.
  6. 🚀 Put it online free. Ask Claude to deploy it to Netlify or Cloudflare Pages and follow what it tells you. Both have free tiers that cover a personal site, so the hosting subscription goes too.
🌐 build my one page site 
I want a one page website for [what it is for, for example my freelance design services]. Build it as a single index.html with the CSS inline, no frameworks and no build step, so I can open the file in a browser and it works with nothing else installed.

The sections I need, in order: [a hero with a headline and one button, a short about, three services with prices, and a contact section with my email]. Make it look like this: [describe the feel, for example warm cream background, one soft orange accent, generous spacing, a serif for headings, nothing corporate]. It has to look right on a phone as well as a laptop.

Build it, then start a local preview server and tell me the exact address to open. Do not change anything else until I have looked at it and given you feedback.
🌱 One honest limit. A first build gets you a genuinely good looking single page, not a nine page site with a blog and a booking system. Keep the first ask small, get it live, then grow it. Trying to one-shot an entire business site is where people conclude "AI can't do this" when the real problem was the size of the ask.
swap 4 of 5

Course platforms

The reason a course subscription feels like progress is that watching is comfortable. You finish 40 lessons and cannot do the thing, because you never did the thing. What actually moves you is a short daily drill with a bit of friction in it, which is the one thing a video library will never give you.

So ask for the drill, not the syllabus. Note the last line of this prompt: making Claude ask you questions before it commits to a plan is what turns a generic curriculum into one that fits your actual week.

🎓 build my 30 day plan with daily drills 
I want to learn [skill] in 30 days. I can give it [X minutes] a day, and I am starting from [what you already know, honestly].

Build me a 30 day plan where every single day has one specific drill I actually do with my hands, not a video I watch, and where each drill genuinely fits in the time I have. Group the days into four weekly themes that build on each other, and end each week with one small finished thing I could show someone. For every drill, tell me what "done" looks like so I am not guessing.

Also tell me which days in a plan like this are the ones people usually quit on, and what to do on those days instead of quitting. Before you write the final plan, ask me the three questions you most need answered about my situation, then rewrite it once using my answers.
💡 Do this in a Project. Put the plan in a Claude Project (swap 5) and paste your drill output back in each day. Claude can see what you actually did, spot where you are stuck, and adjust week three instead of handing you a plan that stops matching you by day nine.
swap 5 of 5

Productivity apps

This is the one that makes the other four better. A Claude Project is a workspace with its own chat history, its own uploaded documents and its own standing instructions, so Claude starts every conversation already knowing who you are and how you like things done. Projects are available on every plan, including free, capped at five projects on free (Anthropic's own help doc), and paid plans add retrieval so you can load far more into one.

Most people set one up, upload nothing useful, and conclude it does not do much. What to load is genuinely the whole game, so this prompt makes Claude interview you and then write the instructions for you.

  1. ➕ Create the project. In Claude, open Projects in the sidebar and create one per area of your life, not one giant one. Mine are split by business area, which is why a content question never gets answered with finance context.
  2. 🗂️ Load the boring documents. The stuff you retype constantly: your brand or style rules, your pricing, your CV, your product descriptions, a doc of past work you are proud of. Boring beats clever here.
  3. 📜 Write the standing instructions. This is the "always do this, never do that" box. Use the prompt below to have Claude draft it from your own answers rather than guessing.
  4. 🔁 Update it when you correct Claude. Every time you find yourself giving the same correction twice, that correction belongs in the instructions. That habit is what turns a project from a folder into something that compounds.
🧠 set up my Claude Project properly 
I am setting up a Claude Project for [area of my life or work, for example my freelance business]. Ask me the eight questions you would need answered to never make me re-explain my context to you again: who I am, what I am working on, who my clients or audience are, how I like things written, what my constraints are, and what I want you to never do.

When I answer, turn my answers into two things. First, a set of project instructions I can paste straight into the project's instruction box, written as direct rules and kept under 400 words. Second, a list of the specific files or documents I should upload as project knowledge, each with a one line reason for why it earns its place.

Then show me one example of how a normal request to you would be answered differently with those instructions in place versus without them, so I can see whether it is actually working.
the renderers

Images and video, without the creative suite

Here is the honest version of this swap: Claude does not generate images or video. What it does is the part you were actually paying a creative suite for, the direction. It writes the detailed prompt, holds your style consistent across a whole set, and turns an idea into a numbered shot list. Then a free generator executes it. One director, free hands.

🎨 turn my idea into prompts and a shot list 
I need visuals for [what you are making, for example a reel about my new offer, or three images for a blog post]. The style I want: [describe it, for example warm, natural light, film grain, no glossy corporate feel].

First, write me one detailed image prompt per visual, each specifying subject, composition, lighting, mood and any text that must appear, written so a text-to-image tool can follow it without guessing. Then, if this is for video, break the idea into a numbered shot list of 4 to 6 shots, each with its own prompt, a duration in seconds, and one line on how it cuts to the next shot.

Keep every prompt under 120 words, keep the style words identical across all of them so the set looks like one person made it, and flag any shot you think a free generator will struggle with so I can swap it for something achievable.

Where I render images

Three real ones, all genuinely free to use today, each good at a different thing.

  1. 🌱 Reve The one I reach for first. Its whole reputation is prompt adherence: it follows long, specific prompts (exactly what Claude writes) more faithfully than almost anything else, and it handles text inside images unusually well. Free credits when you sign up, no card needed, and the free tier is enough to render a Claude-written set and iterate.
  2. 💡 Recraft The design specialist. Where others make photos, Recraft makes design assets: vector graphics, logos, icons and mockups with clean, sharp text. Free plan with daily credits, and the honest limits: free images are public in Recraft's community gallery and are not licensed for commercial use, so it is for practice and personal projects until you upgrade.
  3. 🧠 Krea The multi-model workbench. One free account gives you 100 compute units a day, reset daily, no credit card, with access to a rotating bench of image models plus upscaling, so you can test the same Claude prompt across different models and keep the winner. Free tier has no commercial licence, and heavier models drain the daily units faster.

Where I render video

Video free tiers die fast, so I checked every one of these against its own pricing page this week. Several big names people still recommend (Kling, Hailuo, Luma, Pika) have quietly killed or gutted their free tiers, so the real free three right now are these.

  1. 🌱 Dreamina The most generous free video tier that exists right now: 225 free credits every single day, reset daily, no card, from CapCut's parent company. It fronts the Seedance model family, which is genuinely strong at multi-shot generation, so a Claude shot list maps onto it naturally. Free output is watermarked and lower resolution.
  2. 💡 PixVerse Daily credits reset at midnight UTC and expire the same day (the exact amount shows in-app, they do not publish it). Strong library of templates and effect presets when you want a stylised clip fast. Treat the output as personal use only: their terms limit free outputs to non-commercial purposes unless you buy a commercial licence, and the same is true of Dreamina and Vidu, so nothing here is safe to drop straight into client work.
  3. 🧠 Vidu The consistency specialist. Its reference-to-video feature keeps the same character or product recognisable across shots, and even the free plan includes 10 reference images a month, which is rare. Signup credits plus small daily login credits, 720p cap, watermarked, and free output is non-commercial.
💡 The honest limits, all in one place. Free video means watermarks (Dreamina and Vidu for sure), 480p to 720p output, slow queues at peak hours, and daily caps, so a 6 shot sequence takes a couple of days on one tool or one day spread across all three. That is completely fine for drafts, tests and content that sits under heavy overlays, and not fine for a polished hero shot. When you genuinely outgrow the caps, that is rung three of the ladder below: add ONE paid renderer, not a suite. And free tiers change without notice, so if one of these has moved by the time you read this, the model stays the same: Claude directs, the current free tool renders.
the money bit

The free, cheap, power ladder

You do not need to land on the top rung. Most people reading this belong on rung two forever, and the honest answer to "which rung am I on" is whichever one your current output actually needs, not the one that sounds most serious.

🌱 Rung 1, free Claude's free plan plus free renderers. You get the five swaps in a lighter form: no Claude Code, up to five Projects, and daily caps on the generators. Genuinely enough to decide whether any of this is for you.$0 a month
💡 Rung 2, one subscription One paid Claude plan, everything else free. This unlocks Claude Code, bigger project knowledge and far more headroom in a day. This is the rung the whole guide is written for.Around $20 a month, see Claude's pricing
👩‍💻 Rung 3, one paid renderer Only if you hit a free generator's daily cap regularly, or you need commercial rights and no watermark. Add ONE paid image or video tool, not a suite.Usually $10 to $30 a month on top
🧠 Rung 4, the higher Claude tier Worth it only when you are running Claude Code most days and genuinely hitting limits. This is a capacity decision, not an ambition decision.$100+ a month, and most people do not need it
🌱 The renewal rule I use. Before anything renews, ask one question: did I open this in the last 30 days, and did it produce something I actually shipped? Not "could it be useful". Did it. Anything that fails both goes, and you can always resubscribe for one month later if you were wrong, which is much cheaper than a year of quietly paying for it.
be honest

What this does not replace

A guide that says one tool replaces everything is selling you something. Here is where the stack genuinely stops, so you do not cancel something you needed.

Live transcription in the room Claude works from a transcript, it does not sit in your call and record it. If you need the recording itself, keep whatever captures it, or use your phone's voice memo app and hand Claude the file.
Your actual design taste Claude will build the site and write the prompts. Deciding it looks cheap and knowing why is still you, and that judgement is the part that is getting more valuable, not less.
Scheduling and sending Drafting an email is not sending it, and a content plan is not a posting queue. Keep the tool that actually pushes the button, and let Claude do everything up to that point.
Anything with your money in it Accounting, invoicing, payroll. Claude can explain, draft and sanity check, but the system of record stays the system of record. Do not cancel your books to save $20.
