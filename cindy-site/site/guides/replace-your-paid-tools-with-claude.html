The 3-step move

  1. 🗂️ Find the free twins. Browse OpenAlternative, a directory of free and open-source alternatives to popular paid tools (Notion, Canva, Firebase and more). It shows each tool's activity so you can see it's actually maintained, not an abandoned repo.
  2. 🧠 Let Claude match your list. Paste your real subscriptions into Claude and have it match each one to a free alternative with the honest trade-off, and tell you which to switch and which to keep.
  3. 🚚 Switch one this week. Pick a single swap and have Claude walk you through the setup AND the migration (export from the old, import into the new) before you cancel anything.

The 2 Claude prompts

First, the audit. Paste your subscriptions and let Claude do the matching:

🧠 match my subscriptions 
Here is my full list of paid subscriptions and what I pay each month:
[paste your tools + monthly price, e.g. "Notion $10, Canva Pro $15, Grammarly $12, Zapier $20..."]

For each one, give me: a genuinely good free or open-source alternative, the honest trade-off (what I'd actually lose), and a verdict: switch or keep (some tools earn their price, say so). Put it in a table with columns: paid tool, monthly cost, free swap, trade-off, verdict. Total up what I'd save if I switched the "switch" ones.

Then, the part most people miss, the migration. Do this before you cancel anything:

🚚 set it up + migrate 
I want to switch from [paid tool] to [free alternative]. Walk me through it as a non-technical person:
1. The exact steps to export all my data out of [paid tool].
2. How to set up [free alternative] and import everything into it.
3. A checklist to confirm nothing is missing, so I can safely cancel [paid tool].
Keep every step plain and numbered.
The trap is cancelling first and migrating later. Always export, import, and confirm everything moved over before you hit cancel. Claude writing the export/import checklist is what makes switching feel safe instead of scary.

the honest bit

Keep what earns its price

This isn't about cancelling everything. The swaps worth making are the ones you barely open but keep renewing out of inertia. Some tools genuinely save you hours and deserve their spot, keep those with a clear conscience. Run the audit, switch one this week, and let the savings prove the point before you do more.

Free tiers change. Before you rely on any swap, double-check its current free limits, they move around. And always migrate before you cancel.
