The Claude council: your 5-advisor AI decision system

Hey, it's Cindy 🌱 This is the Claude Council, a free skill that runs any decision past 5 independent AI advisors and hands you one clear verdict.

You know that thing where you ask one AI one question, get one confident answer, and have absolutely no way to tell if it is brilliant or quietly wrong? That is the trap. A single AI gives you a single take, often delivered with total confidence even when it is missing something obvious. And for the decisions that actually matter (your pricing, your positioning, whether to take the deal), one confident answer is not enough.

This fixes that. It is a free skill called the LLM Council, built by Ole Lehmann and adapted from Andrej Karpathy's "LLM Council" methodology. I call it the Claude Council in my reel because it runs entirely inside Claude, but its real name is the LLM Council, so do not get confused when you go to install it. Instead of one answer, it runs your question through 5 independent advisors, has them anonymously review each other's answers, then a chairman pulls it all into one clear verdict. It is like having a board of advisors on call, for free, any time you are stuck.

how it works

What the Council actually does

Here is the whole thing in plain language. Four steps, all happening inside one Claude chat.

  1. 🎯 It frames your question and quietly pulls in context. Claude takes what you asked, sharpens it into the real decision, and quietly reads relevant context from your workspace so the advisors are not flying blind.
  2. 👥 It spawns all 5 advisors at once. Five different thinkers answer your question in parallel, and each one leans all the way into its own angle with zero hedging. No watered-down committee answer.
  3. 🕵️ It runs an anonymized peer review. Each advisor's answer gets relabeled Response A through E, then all 5 review the set blind. They each call out which answer is strongest, which has the biggest blind spot, and what every single one of them missed. Because it is anonymous, they are judging the thinking, not the source.
  4. ⚖️ A chairman synthesizes one verdict. Finally, a chairman reads everything (the answers and the peer reviews) and gives you one clear decision, not a wishy-washy "it depends."
the cast

Meet the 5 advisors

These are thinking lenses, not job titles. Each one is deliberately one-sided so that together they cover your blind spots.

  1. 🔪 The Contrarian. Actively hunts for what is wrong, missing, or about to fail. Assumes your idea has a fatal flaw and goes looking for it. The friend who saves you from a bad deal by asking the question you have been avoiding.
  2. 🧱 The First Principles Thinker. Ignores your surface question and asks "what are we actually trying to solve here?" Strips away assumptions and rebuilds from the ground up. Will sometimes tell you that you are asking the wrong question entirely.
  3. 🚀 The Expansionist. Looks for the upside everyone else is missing. What could be bigger, what adjacent opportunity is hiding, what you are undervaluing. Cares about what happens if this works even better than you expect.
  4. 👽 The Outsider. Has zero context about you or your field and responds purely to what is in front of it. Catches the curse of knowledge: the stuff that is obvious to you but confusing to everyone else. Honestly the most underrated of the five.
  5. 🔨 The Executor. Only cares whether this can actually get done and the fastest path to doing it. Ignores theory completely. Looks at everything through "OK, but what do you do Monday morning?" If your idea has no clear first step, the Executor says so.
what to expect

What you get back: the verdict

The chairman's verdict comes back in five clean parts, so you can read it like a map:

  1. 🤝 Where the council agrees. The high-confidence signals, the parts everyone landed on independently.
  2. ⚔️ Where the council clashes. The genuine disagreements, with both sides laid out so you can see the real tension.
  3. 🔦 Blind spots the council caught. The things that only surfaced once the advisors reviewed each other.
  4. ✅ The recommendation. One clear answer. Not "it depends."
  5. 👣 The one thing to do first. A single concrete next step, so you walk away with momentum instead of a to-do list.

the craft

How to get the most out of the council

This is the part most people skip, and it is the difference between generic advice and a real war room.

  1. 📄 Feed it real context, not a one-line question. The Council reads your workspace, so the more it knows, the sharper every advisor gets. Drop in your real numbers, your constraints, what you have already tried, and what is actually at stake. "Should I raise my rates?" gets you generic advice. "I charge $1,100 per reel, my last sponsor pushed back on price, here is the email" gets you a war room.
  2. 🍴 Frame a real decision, not a yes or no. The Council is built for forks in the road: this option versus that one, raise or hold, hire or automate, pivot or push. If your question has one factually correct answer, you do not need 5 advisors, you need a quick lookup. Save the Council for the calls where being wrong is expensive.
  3. ⏰ Run it BEFORE the move, not after. The whole point is to catch the fatal flaw while you can still change course. Council the pricing email before you send it, the positioning before you build the landing page, the hire before you post the job. After you have committed, it is just expensive hindsight.
  4. 🗺️ Read the verdict like a map, not a grade. Where the council agrees is your high-confidence signal: that part of the plan is probably solid, so stop second-guessing it and move. Where the council clashes is the gift: that is the exact spot where smart people genuinely disagree, which means that is where you should slow down and dig.
  5. ✂️ Paste your actual draft for copy review, do not describe it. If you want the Council to pressure-test a caption, a landing page, a sales email, or a sponsorship pitch, give it the real words. Describing your copy hides the exact thing the Outsider is built to catch: where your writing is confusing or flat to someone who is not already in your head.
  6. 😬 Go in wanting the answers you do not want to hear. If you only run the Council to get a yes, you wasted it. The real value is the Contrarian finding the hole you have been avoiding and the First Principles Thinker telling you that you are solving the wrong problem entirely. Walk in ready to be wrong, that is where the money is.
  7. 👣 Act on "the one thing to do first," not the whole list. The chairman ends with a single concrete next step on purpose. Most decisions die in a 12-item to-do list. Do the one thing, then come back and council your next fork.
  8. 🔁 Council the council. You are allowed to push back. If the recommendation feels off, reply with "pressure-test that recommendation" and you get a second pass that argues against the first. You can also zoom in on a single advisor: "say more about what the Contrarian flagged" pulls the thread on the one angle that worried you.
  9. 🎬 Use it on content ideas too, not just business decisions. Before you batch-film a whole series or commit to a new angle, council the concept: "war room whether this hook actually lands or just sounds clever." The Outsider will tell you if it only makes sense to people already in the niche.

Every way you can use the council

Marketing, content and growth

Picking your one lead platform when you are spread thin.

📣 pick one platform 
council this: I'm a solo creator posting AI content. Right now I split my time across Instagram reels, LinkedIn carousels, and Threads, and I'm doing all three badly because I'm exhausted. My goal this year is to build an audience I can eventually sell a $47/mo community to. Should I go all in on one platform for the next 90 days, or keep a lighter presence on all three? If one, which one, given the community-sale goal?

Repositioning when your hook audience is not your buyer audience.

🎯 reposition the hook 
council this: My most viral reels are fear-based hooks that pull huge reach but mostly attract scared employees who never buy anything. The people who actually message me about working together are small business owners who want AI to run their ads. Do I lean harder into the fear hooks for reach and accept low conversion, pivot my whole positioning toward business owners and lose the easy reach, or try to run both and risk a confused brand?

Whether to take a sponsorship that does not fit your niche.

🍱 the off-niche sponsorship 
council this: A meal-kit brand offered me $1,400 for two reels. I make AI and marketing content, so meal kits are off-topic. The money is real, but my last three posts have been tightly on-niche and my engagement is finally climbing. Do I take the deal and risk diluting the audience I just tightened, negotiate for an AI angle they probably won't accept, or turn it down? This is only my second paid offer ever.

Pricing a sponsored reel package without a benchmark.

💵 price a reel package 
council this: A SaaS brand wants a 3-reel package plus 30 days of usage rights to run my videos as their own ads. My only prior paid deal was $1,900 for two reels with no ad usage. They opened by asking for "your package rate." I'm tempted to quote $3,500 but I'm scared of pricing myself out of my first package deal. What number do I send, how do I structure the usage rights, and how do I justify it in the email?

Killing or doubling down on a content series.

📉 kill or double down 
council this: I'm 8 episodes into a numbered series. Episodes 1 to 3 did great, then 4 through 8 flattened to half the views. My rule was to re-evaluate at 10 episodes. Do I push to 10 as planned and then decide, kill it now and free up the slots for fresh ideas, or refresh the format and treat the next two as a test?

Choosing your call-to-action mix across reels.

🔁 the CTA mix 
council this: Most of my reels end with a hard CTA: "comment KEYWORD and I'll DM you the resource." My reach has dipped and I'm wondering if the constant keyword-bait comments are training the algorithm to see my engagement as low-quality. Do I keep the hard CTA because lead capture is the point, cut back to half and let the rest be pure value to protect reach, or test a soft "follow for more" close on my highest-reach posts only?

Pricing, offers and monetization

A tripwire price point that is killing conversions.

🏷️ the tripwire price 
council this: I sell an AI prompt pack as a low-ticket tripwire at $27 and conversion from my email list is about 2%. Some people in my niche price these at $9 to $17 and claim higher take rates. Do I drop to $17 to get more buyers into my world so I can upsell my $497 offer later, or hold $27 because the higher price filters for serious buyers? Am I even thinking about this the right way?

Productize a service into a flat-price offer.

📦 productize the service 
council this: I keep getting DMs asking me to set up their AI content system, and I quote it custom every time, usually $400 to $900. The scoping conversations are exhausting. I'm considering one fixed productized offer at a flat $397, same deliverables every time. The risk is some setups are more work than others and I'll lose money on the hard ones. Should I flatten to one price, do tiers, or keep quoting custom?

Launch model versus evergreen.

🚪 launch vs evergreen 
council this: I'm deciding how to sell my paid community. Option A is a launch model: doors open for one week a few times a year with urgency. Option B is evergreen: doors always open, sell continuously with no urgency. I post daily so I have steady reach, but I hate the dead months a launch model creates, and I worry evergreen signups will trickle and never spike. Which fits a daily-posting creator with my reach?

Free tier versus paid-only entry.

🆓 free tier vs paid-only 
council this: I'm launching a community for marketers learning AI. Option A: open a free tier for social proof and nurture, then upsell paid later. Option B: skip free and go paid-only at a low launch price so the member count is all real buyers. Free risks a ghost-town of non-buyers; paid-only risks a tiny visible count at launch that kills momentum. Which structure should I open with?

Anchor a high-ticket offer you have never sold.

⚓ anchor the high ticket 
council this: I want to add a 1:1 AI strategy call as my highest-priced offer, capped at 4 a month. I've never sold a call at any price. Part of me wants to anchor at $750 to position it as premium, part of me wants to start at $297 to get the first bookings and build testimonials. The calls also become content and case-study fuel, which matters more to me right now than the cash. Where should I set the price, and is launching this even the right move before I have proof?

All three matter for a September launch, but I can't do all three well at once. What do I sequence first, second, third, and what do I consciously delay?

🎬 sponsorship admin

Option B: hire a managed VA at a fixed monthly rate to handle admin only, while I keep all negotiation judgment. Option C: sign with a talent manager who takes 15 to 20% of deal revenue but owns the whole pipeline. Which structure do I commit to at my current deal volume?
skip it when

When NOT to use it

The Council is powerful, but it is not for everything. Skip it when:

  1. ❓ Your question has one factually correct answer. That needs a quick lookup, not a debate.
  2. ✍️ You just want something created. "Write me three hook options" is a creation task. Ask Claude normally and keep moving.
  3. 📋 You want a summary. Recapping a doc or a thread does not need 5 advisors.
  4. 😌 You are not actually torn and nothing expensive rides on it. If the stakes are low and you already know your answer, go. The Council is for the hard, costly, genuinely uncertain calls.
