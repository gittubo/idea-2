Still image, platform, you, motion, story

Each one only makes sense once the last one has landed. Prompting a still image teaches you the language. The platform tour teaches you the map. A consistent version of yourself gives you a character. Motion gives the character life. The short film puts all four together.

one

Better ChatGPT images

ChatGPT Image Generator Tutorial (Exact Prompts I Use) · Jake Dawson · 13 minutes 48 · free to watch, free to follow

The prompting fundamentals, with his actual prompts on screen rather than described. The most useful part is the editing conversation: uploading a real photo and changing it by talking, rather than writing one perfect prompt and hoping. That habit transfers to every tool in the rest of this list.

You walk out with: the structure of a prompt that produces what you pictured, and the ability to fix an image by talking to it.

One note. It was recorded before the most recent ChatGPT image updates, so the interface on screen looks a little older than yours. The prompting itself has not changed. Image generation and editing an uploaded photo are documented across ChatGPT plans in OpenAI's own help page.

Learn 98% of Higgsfield in 18 minutes

The full platform in one pass. Watch this before you pay for anything, because eighteen minutes tells you whether the feature set matches what you actually want to make. It covers the image side, the video side and how the pieces connect, including running it from a chat rather than the web interface.

three

Make an AI version of yourself

How to Make Your AI Clone With Higgsfield (Full Guide) · Thomas Creates · 8 minutes 20 · free to watch

The shortest video on the list and the one with the biggest payoff. It builds a Soul ID character, which is a trained likeness of you that stays consistent across every image and video you generate afterwards. This is the step that separates "a nice AI picture" from "a photo of me that I can actually use".

You walk out with: a character of yourself you can shoot with forever, rather than a one-off image.

Get the training photos right first. This is the step everything downstream depends on, and it is almost entirely about the photos you feed it. There is a checklist below.

This is a craft video rather than a tool video, which is why it is the one worth watching twice. It is about why AI video reads as AI, and the specific things that fix it: imperfection, real dialogue, camera movement, sound design and editing. Almost none of that is about which model you use.

do this first

The training photo checklist

Video three is the video everything else depends on, and it lives or dies on the photos you upload. Higgsfield's own help centre says a Soul ID character needs 20 or more photos of the same person, up to 80 supported. What it also says, and what people skip, is what those photos have to look like (source).

  1. 📷 20 photos minimum. More is better up to 80. Twelve good photos produces a worse character than thirty average ones.
  2. 🔆 Well lit, different angles, different expressions. Front on, three quarters, profile, smiling, neutral. A set of twenty near-identical selfies teaches it one pose.
  3. 🚫 No sunglasses, no heavy shadows, no cropped faces. Anything that hides part of your face teaches it to guess that part.
  4. 🧍 At least one full-height photo. Required, and the reason generated full-body shots either work or come out strange.
  5. 🙅 Only your own face. Or someone who has actually agreed to it. That is their rule and it is a good one.
Spend the twenty minutes. Gathering the right thirty photos is genuinely the difference between a character that looks like you and one that looks like your cousin. Everything after this step inherits whatever you feed it here.
night one

Make something tonight

Before you watch anything, make one image, so the tutorials land on something you have already tried. Open ChatGPT, attach a photo of yourself, and paste this.

🎨 my first proper image prompt 
Using the photo I have attached as the reference for my face, generate one photorealistic portrait of me, 4:5, in a warm-lit indoor setting with a large window just out of frame to the left.

Keep my face, features, skin tone, skin texture and proportions exactly as they are in the photo. Do not slim, reshape, lighten or smooth anything. Real skin texture, not retouched.

Lighting: soft directional daylight from the left, gentle fill on the right so the shadows stay open, warm neutral background with enough depth that I am clearly separated from it. Shot on an 85mm lens at eye level, shallow depth of field, focus on the eyes.

Then tell me, in three short lines, which parts of this prompt did the most work, so I know what to change first next time.
The last paragraph is the point. Asking it which parts of the prompt mattered turns one generation into a lesson. Do that on every early attempt and you will stop guessing within a week.
five nights

A schedule that finishes

  1. Night one, 14 minutes. Video one, then run the prompt above and change one thing about it three times.
  2. Night two, 18 minutes. Video two. Decide whether you are paying for the platform before you go further.
  3. Night three, 8 minutes plus prep. Gather your training photos against the checklist, then video three, then train the character.
  4. Night four, 23 minutes. Video four, then generate one short clip and deliberately break the "too clean" rule to see the difference.
  5. Night five, 40 minutes. Video five. Then write one scene of your own, even a bad one, while it is fresh.
You can stop at night three. Videos one to three give you a complete outcome on their own: consistent photos of yourself that actually look like you. Nights four and five are for when you want them to move.
