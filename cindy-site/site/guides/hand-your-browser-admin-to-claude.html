the switch

Chatting versus delegating

Most people only ever use the chat box, so their mental model of AI is "it tells me things". Claude in Chrome is a different shape. It is a browser extension that reads, clicks and navigates websites alongside you, inside your real browser, in your real logged-in tabs (official getting-started article). That last detail is the whole point: it works inside the accounts you are already signed into, so it never hits a login wall on the tools you actually use.

The admin that has been on your list for a month usually survives because it is thirty small decisions, none of them hard, spread across six tabs. That is exactly the shape of task worth handing over.

⚠️ The plan and browser gate, upfront. Claude in Chrome is available on all paid plans (Pro, Max, Team and Enterprise), and the Chrome side panel is in beta. It is not supported on other Chromium-based browsers or on mobile, so it is Google Chrome on a desktop or laptop. On Max and Team plans the side panel runs as a Claude Cowork session, and that is rolling out to Pro; on Enterprise an admin has to enable it first. Current plan details: claude.com/pricing.

Install it in five minutes

  1. 🌐 Open Google Chrome. Not Edge, not Brave, not Arc, and not your phone. This one is Chrome on desktop only, and trying it elsewhere is the most common reason people think it does not exist.
  2. ➕ Add the extension. Go to the Claude in Chrome listing on the Chrome Web Store and click Add to Chrome.
  3. 🔐 Sign in with your Claude account. Use the same account you use on claude.ai so your skills, connectors and history follow you.
  4. 📌 Pin it. Click the puzzle-piece icon in the Chrome toolbar, then the pin next to Claude. You want it one click away or you will forget it is there.
  5. ✅ Grant the permissions. The extension asks for a specific list, and the one that matters is debugger, which is what actually lets Claude click buttons, type text and take screenshots. Every permission and the reason for it is documented in the getting-started article, and it is worth reading once rather than clicking through.
  6. 🖱️ Open the side panel on the page you care about. Navigate to the tab first, then click the Claude icon. The side panel reads the tab you are on with no extra setup, so you never need to paste a screenshot or describe what is on screen.
🌱 The side panel stays open while you browse, and it can also be driven from Claude Cowork or from Claude Code if you would rather start the task somewhere else. For everything in this guide, the side panel on its own is enough.

The three permission modes

There is a drop-down on the chat input with three modes, and picking the right one is most of the safety story. From Anthropic's Claude in Chrome permissions guide:

  • Manually approve (formerly "ask before acting"). Claude pauses and asks before each action and you choose allow or deny. Slow, and the right choice for your first three runs and for anything touching money, clients or anything you cannot undo.
  • Automatically approve. Claude keeps working and reviews each action for safety itself, blocking anything it judges unsafe and pausing to ask when it needs you. If it keeps hitting blocks it falls back to asking per step. This is the default in the Cowork side panel and it is the sensible everyday setting once you trust the task.
  • Skip all approvals. Claude does not pause and nothing checks its actions automatically. Anthropic's own guidance is to use it only when you completely trust every action, connector, file and app involved. For admin work, there is very little reason to reach for this.
💡 The side panel remembers whichever mode you pick for future sessions, so if you switch to automatic for one quick job, that is where it stays next time. Check the drop-down before a run that matters.

What to hand over, and what to keep

The first task decides whether you ever use this again. A good candidate is boring, repetitive, has a clear finished state, and does not need your taste in the middle of it.

👩‍💻 Hand over: gathering Pulling the same fields off twenty pages into one table. Comparing what is open across your tabs. Reading a long settings screen and telling you what is switched on.
🌱 Hand over: filling Retyping the same details into a profile, a directory listing, a form or a media kit. Anything where the information already exists and you are moving it from one box to another.
🧠 Keep: judgement What to charge, what to say yes to, what tone a reply needs, which of two options is better. Ask Claude to draft it, then decide yourself.
💡 Keep: one-offs If you will never do it again, writing a good instruction takes longer than doing the thing. This pays off on the task that repeats.

Prompt 1: refresh a profile or media kit

The classic month-old task. Describe the outcome, not the clicks, and give it the source of truth to work from.

👩‍💻 update the profile 
I have [the page, for example "my profile on this marketplace" or "my media kit page"] open in this tab and it is out of date. Bring it up to date for me. Work through the page section by section rather than fixing one field and stopping.

Here is the current, correct information to use, and do not use anything else, do not round numbers up, and do not invent a credential, a client, a follower count or a date that is not in this list: [paste your real current stats, bio, links, services, rates, locations and anything else the page asks for].

Go through every editable field on the page, including the ones collapsed behind "edit" or "show more" or a second tab, and update anything that no longer matches the list above. Where a field asks for free text like a bio or description, rewrite it in the same voice as the text I gave you rather than making it more formal. If a field asks for something that is not in my list, stop and ask me instead of guessing.

Before you save anything, show me a plain list of every change you are about to make, in the format "field: old value becomes new value", and wait for me to confirm. Do not delete an existing section to make room for a new one, do not change my username or my email, and do not touch anything to do with payments or account settings.

Prompt 2: collapse ten tabs into one table

Drag the relevant tabs into Claude's tab group first. Claude can view and interact with every tab in that group at once, so you stop hopping between them.

🧠 the multi-tab collation run 
I have grouped several tabs for you. Go through every tab in your tab group and pull them into one comparison table for me. Do not change, submit, send or delete anything on any of these pages: this is a read-only job.

For each tab, record these fields: [list exactly what you need, for example the name, the price, the plan or tier, the renewal date, the cancellation link, and the account the subscription is billed to]. Where a field genuinely is not on the page, write "not shown" rather than leaving it blank or guessing a plausible value, because a blank cell and a missing value look identical later and I will not be able to tell them apart.

Give me the result as a table I can paste straight into a spreadsheet, one row per tab, sorted by [the field that matters, for example cost, highest first]. Under the table, tell me how many tabs you covered so I know none were skipped, and flag anything that looked unusual, for example a plan I appear to be paying for twice or a renewal inside the next seven days.

If a page will not load, sits behind a login, or shows something different from what I described, stop and tell me which tab and what you saw instead of working around it.

Prompt 3: fill the long form, stop before submit

Long application forms, directory listings, supplier onboarding, insurance renewals. The instruction that makes this safe is the hard stop at the end.

🌱 fill it, do not send it 
Fill in the form on this page for me using the details below, and stop before submitting it. Do not click submit, send, apply, pay or confirm under any circumstances, even if the form warns me that progress will be lost.

My details: [paste name, business name, ABN or registration number, address, email, phone, website, and anything else the form is asking for]. For the longer free-text questions, here is what I want to say and the tone I want: [paste your notes, or a previous answer you were happy with].

Work through the form in order, including any steps hidden behind "next" or "continue", and expand every conditional section that applies to me. For any question you cannot answer from my details, leave it empty and add it to a list rather than filling it with something plausible. For any question where two of my answers could both be correct, pick the more conservative one and tell me you did.

When you reach the final review or submit screen, stop there and give me two things: a list of every field you filled with the value you entered, and a list of every field you left for me. Then wait. I will read it and press submit myself.

Prompt 4: reconcile records across two systems

Matching invoices to transactions, receipts to a spreadsheet, orders to bookings. Claude has built-in knowledge of how to navigate common platforms including Gmail, Google Calendar, Google Docs, Slack and GitHub, so this kind of cross-checking is one of the things it handles most reliably.

💡 the reconciliation run 
I need two systems reconciled and I want a report, not action. Read-only: do not send, reply, forward, delete, archive, mark as read, upload or attach anything anywhere.

System one is [for example my email inbox, searched for invoices and receipts between these dates]. System two is [for example the transactions or expenses list in this accounting tool, same date range]. Go through system one item by item and try to find the matching entry in system two, matching on amount first, then date within a few days, then the supplier or sender name.

Give me four clearly separated lists. Matched: the items you found in both, with the amount, the date and the supplier. In system one only: things I have a receipt for that are not recorded, which is usually what I am missing. In system two only: recorded items with no receipt attached. Uncertain: anything where you found a near-match but the amount or the date does not line up, with both values shown so I can judge it myself.

Do not merge an uncertain match into the matched list to make the numbers tidier, and give me a count for each list at the top. If you run out of items to check or hit a limit on how far back you can search, say exactly where you stopped.
🌱 That fourth list is the one that earns its keep. An assistant that says "these three do not quite line up, here are both numbers" is far more useful than one that quietly rounds a mismatch into a match and hands you a clean report that is wrong.
make it stick

Save it once, then let it repeat

The prompts above are long on purpose. The good news is that you write them once. When one works, keep it.

  1. 💾 Save it as a shortcut. Once a prompt produces the result you wanted, save it as a shortcut in the extension. You then recall it by typing / in the chat and picking it, instead of rewriting the instruction each time.
  2. ✏️ Tune it after the first real run. The gap between what you meant and what you wrote only shows up on a live task. Edit the shortcut rather than correcting Claude mid-run, so the fix is permanent.
  3. ⏰ Put it on a schedule. Shortcuts can be scheduled to run automatically using the clock icon in the upper right of the extension panel, daily, weekly, monthly or annually. Good candidates: a weekly stock or price check, a monthly subscription sweep, a Monday morning pull of the numbers you always look up.
  4. 🧾 Make it report somewhere you look. End any scheduled prompt with an instruction to write its output into a note, a doc or a draft email. A scheduled run that finished perfectly into a void is the same as one that never ran.
  5. 🔁 Record the fiddly ones instead. If a task is more clicking than reasoning, the classic side panel lets you record yourself doing the steps once and saves it as a repeatable shortcut. Recording is not available when the side panel is running as a Cowork session.
the honest bit

What Claude will not do, whatever you ask

These are blocked regardless of your permission mode. Making purchases or financial transactions, creating accounts, handling sensitive credit card or ID data, downloading files from untrusted sources, permanent deletions (emptying trash, deleting emails, files or messages), giving investment or financial advice, executing trades, modifying system files, and completing instructions found inside emails or web content. Separately, Claude always needs your explicit permission to change permission settings, grant authorisations, or type sensitive information into a website. Full list: permissions guide.

Two more things worth knowing before you build a week around this. Agentic browsing is a multi-minute activity, not a two-second one, so it buys back your attention rather than raw clock time: start it and go do something else. And Claude cannot tell that the underlying information was wrong, so a run that executes perfectly on bad source data gives you confident, wrong output. The review step at the end is the part you keep.

Anthropic is explicit that this is a genuinely risky capability and worth reading up on before you use it heavily. Their guidance is in use Claude in Chrome safely, and if the extension misbehaves there is a troubleshooting article.
