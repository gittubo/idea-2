Editable carousels with Claude and Canva

Hey, it's Cindy 🌱 This is the exact two-tool build I use now: Claude designs the whole carousel from a real brief, then one Canva feature turns those flat slides back into a fully editable template.

the setup

What this actually does

This pairs two tools so neither one has to be perfect on its own.

Claude

Does the thinking and the design: the hook, the slide order, the copy, and the visual layout of every slide.

Canva

Does the touch-ups: it lifts the text and objects back out of Claude's flat slide images so you can move, recolour, and reuse them.

The honest bit up front: Claude does not paint raster art like a photo generator. It designs each slide as a real HTML/CSS layout and renders it, which you then export as a PNG at Instagram size. That is why the Canva step matters, because a rendered PNG is flat until you free the layers.

step one

Step 1: give Claude a real brief, not "make me a carousel"

The whole thing lives or dies on the brief. Vague in, generic out. Paste this into Claude and fill the brackets:

✍️ the carousel brief 
You are my carousel designer. Build me a 7 slide Instagram carousel (1080x1350, 4:5).

Audience: [who they are, what they struggle with, e.g. "marketers newish to AI who feel behind"].
Topic: [the one idea this carousel teaches].
Goal of the post: [save / share / follow / comment a keyword].
My brand: [2-3 colours as hex, 1 heading font, 1 body font, the vibe in 3 words].

Structure: slide 1 = a scroll-stopping hook, slides 2-6 = one idea each with a short headline and 1-2 supporting lines, slide 7 = a clear call to action.

First give me the slide-by-slide copy as an outline and wait for my approval. Do not design yet.
💡 Why the pause matters: approving the words first means Claude designs around locked copy, so you are not redesigning slides because the message shifted. Fix the writing while it is still cheap text.
step two

Step 2: feed it proof it can copy from

Before Claude designs, give it 2-3 carousels you already love as reference. Describe them or paste the text, and tell Claude what specifically works (the hook style, the one-idea-per-slide rhythm, the amount of white space).

📎 the style reference 
Here are 2-3 carousels whose style I want to echo (not copy): [paste the slide text or describe them]. What I like: [e.g. "big bold headline, tiny supporting line, lots of breathing room"]. Match that energy for my brand, keep my colours and fonts.
🔍 Heads up: echo the structure and rhythm, never clone someone's exact wording or layout. The goal is a proven shape in your own voice, not a copy that gets you flagged.
step three

Step 3: let Claude design and export the slides

Once you approve the copy, tell Claude to build it:

🖼️ the design command 
Approved. Now design all 7 slides as one carousel, each 1080x1350, consistent fonts, colours and spacing across every slide. Render them so I can export each slide as a PNG.

Claude builds each slide as a rendered design (an HTML/CSS layout), which you export as individual PNG images at 1080x1350.

⚙️ The clean way to do this at scale: an open-source Claude Code skill called Open Carrusel is built exactly for this. You chat with Claude to design the slides, and it screenshots each one to a PNG at exact Instagram dimensions and zips them for you. It is free on GitHub: github.com/Hainrixz/open-carrusel.
📐 No-skill version: if you are not in Claude Code, ask Claude to render the slides as a design you can view, then export or screenshot each slide at 1080x1350 (4:5). Instagram's carousel spec is 1080x1350 for portrait, confirmed here: help.instagram.com.
the real trick

Step 4: make them editable again in Canva

Here is the part most people miss. Those exported PNGs are flat, the text is baked in like a screenshot. Canva has two Magic Studio features that lift the layers back out so you get a real editable template.

  • 🔤 Grab Text is the one for the words. It lifts the text off a flat image and turns it into editable, restyleable text, so you can fix a typo, swap a line, or change the font without touching the rest of the slide. This is the correct feature for headlines and copy. Source: canva.com/features/edit-text-in-image.
  • 🧩 Magic Grab is the one for objects. It selects a shape, person, or graphic and releases it from the background so you can move or resize it. Use this for icons and visual elements, not text. Source: Canva Design School.

How to reach both: upload your PNG into a Canva design, select the image, click Edit (or Edit photo), open Magic Studio, then pick Grab Text or Magic Grab and click or brush the element you want to free.

💳 The honest catch: Grab Text and Magic Grab are both Canva Pro features, not on the free plan. Canva Pro is around $12.99 USD a month (check current AUD pricing on Canva's plans page): canva.com/pricing. If you would rather stay free, keep your carousel fully editable inside Claude and just re-export when you tweak, instead of grabbing layers out of a PNG.
📸 Grab Text works best on flat text. If a headline looks skewed or angled, it grabs cleaner when the source slide has straight, front-on text, which Claude's layouts already give you.
make it reusable

Step 5: turn it into a weekly template

Once the layers are free in Canva, you have a reusable template, not a one-off post.

  1. 🎨 Recolour the whole thing to a new palette in a couple of clicks.
  2. 🔁 Swap the copy slide by slide for next week's topic while the layout stays locked.
  3. 💾 Save it as a Canva template so every future carousel starts 90 percent done.
The design was never the hard part. The blank page was, and now you never start from one.

the craft

How to get the most out of it

🌱 If you are just starting: do steps 1-3 only for your first few. Get comfortable briefing Claude and exporting clean PNGs before you touch the Canva layer. A great carousel with baked-in text still performs; editability is an upgrade, not a requirement.

🚀 If you want leverage: build one master style guide (bonus prompt 2), save it, and paste it into every brief so every carousel is instantly on-brand. Then batch: brief 4 topics in one Claude session, export all the slides, and Grab Text them in Canva in one sitting. That is a month of carousels in an afternoon.

skip it when

When to skip the Canva step

If a carousel is truly one-and-done (a time-sensitive announcement you will never reuse), don't bother freeing the layers. Export the PNGs from Claude and post them. The Grab Text and Magic Grab step only earns its keep when you plan to reuse or restyle the template later.
