One small thing to get right. Frank Rosenblatt introduced the perceptron in a 1957 report, and the physical Mark I Perceptron machine was built and demoed in 1958 (Cornell Chronicle). 70 years, five leaps

You do not need any maths to follow this. Each leap did one thing: it fixed the problem the leap before it could not solve. Read it in order and the whole of modern AI clicks into place.

  1. 🧠 1957, the perceptron. Frank Rosenblatt built the first machine that could learn from examples instead of being hand-programmed. Show it a picture, ask "is this a cat", and it drew a single straight line to decide yes or no. One artificial neuron, one yes-or-no answer. Powerful for its time, but a straight line can only separate the simplest things.
  2. 🕸️ 1980s, neural networks. One neuron was not enough, so researchers wired thousands together in layers. The breakthrough was backpropagation: when the network got something wrong, this algorithm nudged every connection a little so it did better next time. Rumelhart, Hinton and Williams popularised it in a 1986 Nature paper, and suddenly networks could read handwriting and tell faces apart.
  3. 🏔️ 2000s to 2012, deep learning. Stack many layers and each one learns something richer than the last: edges, then shapes, then whole objects. In 2012 a deep network called AlexNet crushed the ImageNet image contest and kicked off the modern AI boom. LeCun, Bengio and Hinton later laid out the whole idea in a 2015 Nature review.
  4. ⚡ 2017, the transformer. A paper called "Attention Is All You Need" let models read a whole sentence at once and weigh how every word relates to every other, instead of crawling left to right word by word. This is the "T" in GPT and the single idea that made today's fluent AI possible.
  5. 🤖 Today, generative AI. Train a giant transformer on huge amounts of text and you get a large language model like Claude that can write, reason and code. Same family tree as that 1957 perceptron, just vastly bigger, better trained, and now able to generate instead of only classify.

the vocabulary

The 4 words that unlock every AI conversation

Neuron One tiny unit that takes inputs, weighs them, and passes on a signal. Stack enough of them and you get behaviour that looks like intelligence.
Weights The dials the model tunes while it learns. "Training" a model is just the search for better weights.
Backpropagation The feedback step that tells every weight how to change after a mistake. This is literally how a network learns from being wrong.
Attention The 2017 trick that lets a model focus on the words that matter in context. It is why modern AI reads like it actually understands you.
You do not need to code or do any maths to get value from this. Knowing the arc, one neuron to networks to attention, is the AI literacy that makes every headline and every new tool make sense. That alone puts you ahead of most people talking about AI.
