The reframe: you don't need a better AI, you need a better setup

Most people open Claude, type a question, get an okay answer, and quietly decide AI is overhyped. The problem was never the model. It is that Claude knows nothing about you yet. Not your voice, not your audience, not your work. So it gives you the average of the internet, because that is all it has to go on.

Spend one weekend fixing that, and the same Claude suddenly feels twice as smart. Same model, completely different output. That is the whole game, and almost nobody actually sets it up.

the four moves

The C.A.S.T method, start to finish

Four moves, in order. Skip one and you are back to typing prompts into a stranger forever.

🔵 C, configure. Turn on Claude's settings and connect your apps, so it can actually do things instead of just talking.
🟡 A, architect. Build a Project and load your knowledge into it, so Claude remembers who you are across every chat.
🟢 S, specialize. Save your repeated tasks as Skills, so you stop re-explaining the same process every week.
🌱 T, target. Train it on your niche, your voice, and your audience, so everything it writes sounds like you, not like a robot.
the map

First, the 3 places Claude actually works

Before the steps, you need this, because most people only ever use the first one. Claude has three surfaces, and they each do a different job.

  1. ☕ Chats. Fast and throwaway. Great for quick questions and brainstorms. Forgets everything tomorrow. Think of a chat as a colleague over coffee: helpful in the moment, no long-term memory of you.
  2. 🗄️ Projects. A workspace with memory. You upload your docs once, set your instructions once, and every chat you start inside that Project already knows you. Think of a Project as a senior hire who actually read your onboarding docs.
  3. 🔖 Skills. A saved task. You teach Claude a process one time, and it reuses that exact process forever. Think of a Skill as a saved playbook your team runs the same way every time.

The one most people skip is Projects, and it is the one that changes everything. That is step A.

C is for configure (turn it on)

This is the boring-but-critical part: switch on Claude's capabilities so it can do more than chat. You do this once, in your account settings.

  1. ⚙️ Open claude.ai and click your name in the bottom left, then Settings.
  2. 🧩 Find the Capabilities area (this is where things like web search and file creation live). Turn on the ones you want available. Skills have their own home in the Customize section, under Skills (more on those in step S).
  3. 🔌 Look for Connectors, also under Customize (sometimes called integrations). This is how Claude plugs into your other tools, like Google Drive, Gmail, Notion, or Canva, so it can read and act on your real stuff. Connect the few you use most. You authorize each one once, like logging into any app.
✅ A connector just means a secure bridge between Claude and another app. You are not coding anything. You click connect, you log in, you approve, done.

Do not over-connect on day one. Start with the two or three tools you live in. You can always add more later.

the big one

A is for architect (build your Project)

This is the magic step. A Project is Claude's long-term memory of you. Set it up once, save hours every single week. Here is the build in four moves.

  1. 🏷️ Name it. Pick a clear focus, like "content engine" or "client X" or "my brand." One Project per job.
  2. 📋 Instruct it. Set the rules: Claude's role, your tone, the output format you want, and what to avoid. This is the standing brief every chat in the Project will follow.
  3. 📤 Upload your knowledge. This is the step that does the magic. Feed the brain: your brand voice doc, your audience and ideal customer, your content pillars, your best past posts, transcripts, style guides. The more real context you give it, the more it sounds like you.
  4. 🧪 Test it. Run one real task inside the Project, see what comes back, tweak the instructions, then ship. Thirty minutes of setup, hours saved every month after.

Here is a copy-paste Project instruction template to start from. Paste it into your Project's custom instructions and swap in your details.

🧠 The Project instructions template 
You are my content and marketing partner. You know my brand inside out.

ABOUT ME: [one line on who you are and what you do]
MY AUDIENCE: [who you make content for, their main pain point]
MY VOICE: [3 to 5 words, e.g. warm, plain-spoken, no jargon, a little funny]
MY CONTENT PILLARS: [the 3 to 5 topics you post about]

RULES:
- Write in my voice using the docs I have uploaded as the source of truth.
- Keep it clear and direct. Explain anything technical the way a sharp friend would.
- No long dashes. Use commas, periods, or brackets instead.
- When you are missing context, ask me one sharp question instead of guessing.
- Always give me the actual draft, not a description of what you would write.

S is for specialize (save your repeated tasks as Skills)

If you find yourself re-explaining the same process to Claude every week, that is a Skill waiting to happen. A Skill is a saved set of instructions Claude loads automatically whenever the task comes up, so you never re-teach it.

The easiest way to make one is to just talk it through. Open a chat and describe the process out loud, and Claude formats it into a proper Skill for you.

Here is a worked example. Say you write Instagram captions the same way every time. You would open a chat and paste this.

🔖 Turn a repeated task into a saved Skill 
Help me build a Skill for writing my Instagram captions.

When I give you a topic or a finished reel, write a caption that:
- Opens with a scroll-stopping first line, all lowercase
- Stays casual and friendly, like texting a friend
- Uses 2 to 3 emojis, never more
- Ends with one clear call to action
- Never uses long dashes

Ask me for the topic, then give me the caption. Save this as a Skill
called "caption writer" so I can reuse it.

Now every time you say "write me a caption," Claude already knows your rules. No re-explaining. You can turn Skills on and off anytime under Customize, then Skills.

sound like you

T is for target (train it on your niche and voice)

This is the polish pass that makes the difference between "good AI writing" and "that sounds exactly like me." Targeting means feeding Claude the specifics of your world until it stops sounding generic.

  1. 📝 Feed it your voice, not a description of it. Do not tell Claude "I'm casual and warm." Show it. Upload five of your best-performing posts and say "this is my voice, match it." Examples beat adjectives every time.
  2. 👥 Give it your real audience. Drop in who you serve, their exact pain points, the words they actually use. The more specific, the less generic the output.
  3. 🔁 Correct it like a new hire. When it gets the tone wrong, do not just sigh and rewrite it yourself. Tell it what was off ("too formal, more like how I'd text a friend") and ask it to save that note. Each correction makes the next draft sharper.
  4. 🎯 Lock your format. If your reels always follow a structure, or your carousels always have 7 slides, tell it once and add it to the Project instructions so it never forgets.

Targeting is not a one-time thing, it compounds. Every doc you add and every correction you make stacks, and after a couple of weeks Claude genuinely writes like you.

hour by hour

Your one-weekend blueprint

Here is the whole thing mapped hour by hour. You do not have to do it in one sitting, but it genuinely fits in a weekend. Tick each block as you go.

🔵 Hours 1 to 4, configure. Turn on Claude's capabilities and connect the two or three apps you live in.
🟡 Hours 5 to 10, architect. Build one Project, write your instructions, upload your knowledge, run one test task.
🟢 Hours 11 to 16, specialize. Save 2 Skills for your most repeated tasks (captions, scripts, repurposing, whatever you do weekly).
🌱 Hours 17 to 22, target. Feed it your niche, your voice examples, your audience, and lock your formats.
🟠 Hours 23 to 24, ship. Stress-test it with one piece of real work and smooth the rough edges.

One weekend. One setup. It pays you back every single week after that.

before you start

A few things I wish someone told me first

  1. 💡 Upload more than you think you should. Most people give Claude one thin doc and wonder why it sounds flat. Your brand voice lives in your actual posts, transcripts, and old work. Feed it the real stuff.
  2. 🧱 One Project per job, not one giant Project for everything. A "content engine" Project and a "client X" Project will both be sharper than one messy catch-all.
  3. 🪄 Skills are for the tasks you are sick of re-explaining. If you have typed roughly the same instructions twice, that is your signal to make it a Skill.
  4. ✍️ Show, never describe. "Match this voice" plus five real examples beats three paragraphs describing your voice. Every time.
  5. 🔧 Treat it like onboarding a teammate, not a magic box. The first few outputs will be 80 percent right. Correct it, save the note, and it gets better fast. That is the work, and it is worth it.
