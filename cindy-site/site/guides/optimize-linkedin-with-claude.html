how it works

How recruiters actually find you

Recruiters almost never scroll a feed hoping to spot you. They open LinkedIn Recruiter (the paid hiring tool) and keyword-search by target job title, hard skills, and tools, the same way you'd Google something. So your profile is not a page they read top to bottom. It's a search-results row that either matches their query or doesn't. Optimizing means making sure the exact words they type live in the fields LinkedIn weights most, then giving them a reason to click. Two goals fall straight out of that: rank in the search to get found, then win the click to get the callback.

the levers

The 6 levers, in priority order

Fix them top to bottom. The ones near the top move the search needle the hardest, so if you only have an hour, start there.

  1. 🥇 Headline, your single most searchable field LinkedIn weights the headline more heavily than almost anything else in search, so this is where you win or lose the ranking. Use the structure TARGET JOB TITLE | KEYWORD | KEYWORD | KEYWORD | short human value line, not vague buzzwords like "results-driven guru". Put the exact title you want next, plus the real hard skills and tools from job posts. Prompt A below writes this for you.
  2. 📄 About section, second most searchable LinkedIn weights the opening of your About most heavily, and only the first ~3 lines show before "see more". So your target title and 2 to 3 core skills have to live in the first two sentences, doing double duty as the hook. Then use close to the full 2,600 characters, working real keywords across the section instead of cramming them once. Prompt B writes it.
  3. 📸 Photo, the cheapest win on the whole profile LinkedIn's own data: a profile with a photo gets up to 21x more profile views and 9x more connection requests than one without. A warm, real, well-lit photo beats a stiff corporate one every time. This is the single fastest thing you can fix today. (source: linkedin.com business blog)
  4. 🖼 Banner, free real estate most people leave blank Most profiles keep the default blue box. The spec is 1584 x 396 px (a 4:1 ratio), and mobile crops the edges, so keep every word inside the central 1260 x 300 px safe zone. A clean banner with your role and a subtle "open to opportunities" cue signals intent before anyone reads a line. Prompt C briefs it so you can build it in Canva.
  5. 🎯 Skills, recruiters filter by them LinkedIn shows your top 3 pinned skills first and weights them most in matching, so these three carry real search value. Set them to the exact skills named in the job descriptions you're targeting, not generic ones. Pin the three that matter most and let the rest sit below.
  6. 🔎 Open to Work, flips you into the recruiter filter Turning this on feeds LinkedIn's "open to opportunities" filter that recruiters search by, so you show up in searches you'd otherwise miss. Members who use it are 37% more likely to receive recruiter InMails. Choose "recruiters only" mode and the status stays private and invisible to your current employer. (source: LinkedIn talent blog via socialmediatoday.com)

The 3 Claude prompts

Open Claude, have your resume and 1 to 2 real job posts for the role you want ready to paste, then run these one at a time. Everything they write is pulled from your actual resume, so you can back up every word in an interview.

✍️ the headline writer, paste your resume + target role 
You are a LinkedIn strategist who understands exactly how recruiters search. Recruiters use LinkedIn Recruiter to keyword-search by target job title, hard skills, and tools, and the headline is the single most heavily weighted searchable field. Your job is to rewrite mine so I actually show up. Here's my resume: [paste resume]. Here's the role I'm targeting next (title + 1-2 real job posts if I have them): [paste target title + job descriptions]. Do this: 1. Pull the 8-10 keywords recruiters would ACTUALLY type to find someone for that role: real job titles, hard skills, tools, certifications, ignore fluff like "results-oriented" or "team player". 2. Write me 3 headline options using this structure: TARGET JOB TITLE | KEYWORD | KEYWORD | KEYWORD | short human value line, keep each under 220 characters. 3. Make one keyword-forward, one balanced, one personality-forward, and tell me the tradeoff of each. 4. Only use keywords that are actually TRUE for me based on my resume, never invent a skill or title I can't back up in an interview, if a must-have keyword for this role is missing from my background flag it as a real gap instead of faking it. 5. Warn me if any version looks keyword-stuffed, since LinkedIn can suppress that.
📝 the about section writer 
You are writing my LinkedIn About section to do two jobs at once: rank in recruiter keyword search AND make a human want to reply. LinkedIn weights the opening most heavily, and only the first ~3 lines show before "see more", so the first two sentences have to carry my target title and core skills. My resume: [paste resume]. Target role + the language real postings use: [paste target role + 1-2 job descriptions]. The tone I want: [warm and human / straight professional / founder energy]. Write my About section so that: 1. The first 2 sentences state what I do + my target title + 2-3 core skills, in plain human language, so I get found AND the preview line hooks. 2. The body naturally works in the industry keywords and tools from those job posts, spread across the section not crammed once, short skimmable paragraphs. 3. It uses real proof from my resume: numbers, outcomes, named tools, no vague adjectives. 4. It ends with a soft line on what I'm looking for next. 5. Length close to the full 2,600 characters if I have the substance, never padded. Rules: everything must be true to my resume, no invented metrics, no buzzwords a recruiter would never search. Give me the final copy in a clean block plus a 2-line note on which keywords you placed and why.
🎨 the banner brief 
You are a brand designer briefing my LinkedIn banner. It's free real estate most people leave as default blue. Spec: 1584 x 396 px, 4:1, and all text must sit inside the central 1260 x 300 safe zone because mobile crops the edges. About me + the role I'm targeting: [paste short bio or resume + target role]. Give me a complete banner brief I can build in Canva: 1. THE ONE LINE: a short role descriptor or tagline, plus an optional subtle "open to opportunities" cue. 2. LAYOUT: where each element sits inside the safe zone, left to right so nothing gets cropped. 3. COLOR + VIBE: 2-3 palette options that read professional but human, with hex codes. 4. WHAT TO LEAVE OUT: the clutter to avoid (busy stock photos, tiny text, anything near the edges or behind the profile-photo circle bottom-left). 5. A 1-sentence more-personality vs more-corporate alt. Keep it clean and mobile-first, give me the banner text in a copy block.
before you post

Three honest notes

🔒 Open to Work, quietly. If you're job-hunting while still employed, pick the "recruiters only" visibility. Recruiters see you're open, your boss and colleagues don't. The public green "#OpenToWork" ring around your photo is a separate, optional choice, so you stay in control of who knows.
😊 A warm photo beats a stiff one. You don't need a paid studio headshot. Good natural light, a plain background, real eye contact, and a genuine expression outperform a rigid corporate shot. Stand near a window, shoot it on your phone, pick the one where you look like someone people want to talk to.
✅ Keep every keyword truthful. Only claim titles and skills you can defend in an interview. Keyword-stuffing, repeating a term unnaturally to game the search, can get your profile suppressed by LinkedIn, and a claim you can't back up ends the call fast. The prompts are built to flag real gaps instead of faking them, so use that.
