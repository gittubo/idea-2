The styling plan ChatGPT builds from one selfie

Hey, it's Cindy 🌱 Here is the full prompt from the reel, plus the four follow-ups that turn the plan into something you can actually walk into a barber or a shop with.

what this is

A styling plan, not a verdict

Everything here is about what suits you. Not what is wrong with you, not a score, not a before and after. The prompt is written that way on purpose, and there are two reasons for it.

The first is that a score is useless. Knowing a number tells you nothing you can do on Saturday. Knowing that a side part balances your face shape, or where your beard's neck line should sit, is something you can act on this week.

The second is practical: ChatGPT gets cagey when you ask it to rate or rank a face, and gives you hedged, watered-down answers. Ask it for styling recommendations with reasons and it will give you pages. Same model, completely different result, purely because of how the question is framed.

Any plan works. ChatGPT Images is available on all tiers, and reading an uploaded photo is a standard thing it does. You can run all of this on the free plan.
step one

Take the photo it needs

Most weak answers come from a weak photo. Two minutes here changes the whole output.

  1. Daylight, facing a window. No overhead light, no ring light, no bathroom mirror. Overhead light invents shadows, and it will read those shadows as your bone structure.
  2. Hair fully off your face. It needs your hairline and your jaw. A fringe hides both, which is ironic given the first thing it recommends is your haircut.
  3. Straight on, neutral expression. No tilt, no angle, no smile. Smiling changes your cheek and jaw shape and it will style for the version that only exists while you are laughing.
  4. Shoulders in frame. The clothing section needs your shoulder line and your neck length. A tight face crop gets you a worse answer.
  5. Add a second photo if you can. A full-length one, standing straight, arms relaxed. It makes the fit and silhouette section far more specific.
the main prompt

The styling plan

Attach the photo, then paste this. It comes back in three parts: the read, the six-section plan, and a shortlist of the three changes worth doing first.

🪞 the full styling plan 
Act as a personal stylist and image consultant. From this photo, build me a practical styling plan based on what suits me.

Step 1, the read. In a few short lines, tell me my face shape, my brow and eye spacing, my jawline shape, my general proportions, and my colouring (undertone, contrast level, hair and eye depth). For each one, say what in the photo told you.

Step 2, the plan. Give me these six sections, and for every recommendation include one short sentence explaining why it suits me specifically:
1. Hair: the cut that suits my face shape, the parting that balances it, the length worth growing out or keeping, and the texture or styling direction that works with my hair type.
2. Brows: the shape, thickness and arch position that fit my eye spacing and brow bone.
3. Facial hair, if I have any or could grow it: whether clean shaven, stubble or a fuller beard suits my jaw, and exactly where to set the cheek line and the neck line.
4. Clothing: the necklines that work on me, the fits and silhouettes that suit my frame, the sleeve and hem lengths that read best on my proportions, and the two things to avoid because of my shape rather than because of trend.
5. Colour: the colours to keep near my face, my best neutrals, my best accent colours, and whether gold or silver metals suit me.
6. Grooming or makeup: the tones that match my undertone, described in plain words rather than brand names.

Step 3, the shortlist. Give me the three changes from all of the above that would make the biggest difference, ordered by how quickly I could do them.

Rules: this is a styling plan, not an assessment of how I look. Do not comment on my weight, my skin condition, my teeth, or anything that is not styling. Do not rate, score or rank my appearance. Do not tell me what is wrong with me. Frame everything as what suits me and why.
Keep that last paragraph. The rules block is what stops it drifting into commentary you did not ask for, and it is the part people delete to save space. Leave it in.
what comes back

The six sections, and what to do with each

Hair The most valuable section, because a cut lasts months. The parting recommendation is the sleeper: a side part versus a middle part changes how wide your face reads, and it costs nothing to test.
Brows Thickness, arch position and where the tail ends, set against your eye spacing. Screenshot this one and take it to whoever does them, because describing a brow in words never goes well.
Facial hair The cheek line and neck line advice is the part barbers actually need. A beard that looks untidy is usually a neck line set too high or too low rather than a length problem.
Clothing Necklines, fits, sleeve and hem lengths. The two "avoid" items are framed around your shape rather than trend, which is why they stay true next season.
Colour Which colours belong near your face, your neutrals, your accents, and gold versus silver. This is the cheapest section to act on, because it mostly changes what you reach for in a wardrobe you already own.
Grooming or makeup Tones matched to your undertone, in plain words rather than brand names, so you can match them at any counter or in any aisle.
follow-up one

Turn it into the words to say out loud

A plan you cannot communicate is a plan you will not use. This one is the single most useful follow-up in the whole guide.

💬 the script for my barber or hairdresser 
Turn the hair and facial hair sections of my plan into the exact words I should say at the appointment, written the way a normal person talks rather than as a list of technical terms.

Give me one short paragraph I can read out or show on my phone, covering the cut, the parting, the length, and for facial hair the cheek line and neck line. Use the words hairdressers and barbers actually use, and where a technical term is the clearest option, give it to me with a plain explanation in brackets so I know what I am asking for.

Then give me two things to say if they suggest something different, phrased politely, so I can hold my ground without it being awkward. Finish with the one question I should ask them before they start, the one that will tell me whether they have understood what I want.
follow-up two

See it before you commit

This is the part people want most, and it is worth being clear about what it is: a preview of a styling direction on your own face, not a photograph of your future.

👀 show me the recommended look 
Create one image of me with the hair and facial hair you recommended, so I can see the direction before I commit to it. Use the photo I gave you as the base.

Keep my face, features, bone structure, skin tone, skin texture, proportions and expression exactly as they are. Do not slim, reshape, lighten, smooth or retouch anything. The only things that change are the hair and the facial hair.

Render it in the same lighting as my original photo rather than studio lighting, because I want to see how it looks in real life rather than in a photoshoot. Then make a second version showing the same hair grown out by about two months, so I know what I am committing to between appointments.
Read the output for what it is. Image models flatter by default, and that is exactly why the "do not smooth or retouch" line is in there. If the image comes back looking airbrushed, say "you retouched my skin, redo it keeping my real skin texture and lighting" and it will correct. Use the result as a direction to show your stylist, not as a target to measure yourself against.
follow-up three

Shop from the wardrobe you already have

🧺 audit what I already own 
Here are photos of the clothes I wear most often. Using the clothing and colour sections of my plan, sort them into three groups: pieces that already suit my shape and colouring and should be in heavier rotation, pieces that suit me but I am probably wearing in the wrong combination, and pieces that are fighting my proportions or my colouring.

For anything in the middle group, tell me what to wear it with instead and why that pairing works better on my frame. Be specific about the combination rather than giving me a general principle.

Then give me a short list of what is genuinely missing, no more than five items, described by neckline, fit, fabric weight and colour rather than by brand or trend, and ordered by how much daily use each one would get.
follow-up four

Make it a plan with dates on it

🗓 the three month version 
Take the three-change shortlist and turn it into a realistic three month plan, assuming I have a normal budget and a normal amount of free time.

For each change, tell me what to do in month one, month two and month three, what it roughly costs, and how long it takes before the result actually shows. Be honest where something takes longer than people expect, for example growing a fringe out or growing a beard to a length where it can be shaped properly.

Then tell me the one change on this list that is reversible and cheap enough to test this weekend, so I can start with something low risk rather than committing to the big one first.
when it hedges

The three things that go wrong

  1. 🫤 It gives you a vague, careful non-answer. Almost always because the request drifted into rating territory. Reset with "I am not asking you to judge how I look, I am asking for styling recommendations with reasons" and it opens up again.
  2. 📋 The advice is generic. If it reads like a magazine listicle, make it justify itself: "for each recommendation, tell me what specifically in my photo led you to it, and change anything you cannot justify". The second pass is usually much sharper.
  3. 💇 The hair advice ignores your actual hair. It cannot always tell texture from one photo. Tell it: your hair type, whether it is fine or thick, how it behaves when you let it dry, and how much time you will realistically spend on it in the morning. That last one changes the recommendation more than anything else.
the honest bit

What it is good at, and what it is not

It is genuinely good at geometry. Face shape, proportions, where lines fall, what balances what. That is readable from a photo and it is most of what a stylist is doing in the first half hour.

It is weaker on the things a photo cannot show: how your hair actually behaves, what you are comfortable wearing, what your life needs you to dress for, and the fact that fit is decided by a tailor and not by a description. Treat the plan as a well-reasoned starting opinion rather than a ruling, and keep the parts that sound like you.

Save the step 1 read. Your face shape, proportions and colouring do not change. That paragraph is the reusable asset, and every future question you ask, about an outfit, a haircut, a pair of glasses, can start by pasting it back in.
