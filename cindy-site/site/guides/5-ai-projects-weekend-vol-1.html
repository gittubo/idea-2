💡 One weekend, one project. The title says five because you should pick from five, not build five. One finished project beats five half-started ones, and each of these is scoped for a genuine two-day build with time left to make it look good.
project one

An AI clone of you

Beginner friendly, about 4 to 6 hours. A web page where visitors talk to "you": your cloned voice answering questions from your own writing. The most shareable thing you can build, and it quietly proves voice AI, a knowledge base, and app deployment in one project.

  1. 🎙️ Clone your voice. ElevenLabs' instant voice clone needs about a minute of clean audio. Record yourself reading naturally, no music, no echo. Starter plan is around $5 a month and includes cloning.
  2. 🧠 Give it your brain. Export your own writing (posts, notes, a bio, FAQs about you) and load it into a Chatbase bot, free tier. That becomes the knowledge base your clone answers from.
  3. 📄 Put it on a page. Ask Lovable to build a simple one-page site with your photo and the chat embedded. Wire the bot's replies through the ElevenLabs API so answers come back in your voice.

visitors ask questions and my cloned ElevenLabs voice answers from a
knowledge base of my own writing (Chatbase free tier).

My stack: ElevenLabs Starter, Chatbase, Lovable for the page.

project two

Your AI chief of staff

Intermediate, about 6 to 8 hours. An n8n agent that reads your Gmail and Calendar overnight, triages what matters, drafts replies to the important things, and sends you one prioritized morning brief. The project you'll still be using in month three, which is exactly what impresses people.

  1. ⚙️ Set up n8n. Free if you self-host, or start a cloud trial. Connect the Gmail and Google Calendar nodes with your account.
  2. 🕐 Build the nightly run. A scheduled workflow pulls unread email plus tomorrow's calendar, sends both to Claude with a triage prompt, and gets back: urgent items, drafts worth sending, and a ranked to-do list.
  3. 📬 Ship the brief. Format Claude's output into one email to yourself at 7am. Keep drafts as Gmail drafts, never auto-send.
📌 Never let it send. The agent drafts, you send. That single rule keeps this safe enough to run on your real inbox from day one.
paste into Claude 
I'm building a morning-brief agent in n8n this weekend. It reads my
unread Gmail and tomorrow's Google Calendar every night and emails me
a prioritized brief at 7am, with reply drafts saved as Gmail drafts.

1. Design the n8n workflow node by node, in order, with what each
   node does and its key settings.
2. Write the triage prompt that turns raw emails + events into:
   3 urgent items, 3 drafts worth writing, and a ranked to-do list.
3. Give me a test plan: how to run it safely on my real inbox
   without it ever sending anything itself.
project three

A second brain you can chat with

Intermediate, about 5 to 7 hours. A chatbot grounded in your own notes, PDFs, and saved articles that answers with citations pointing back to your files. This is retrieval-augmented generation, the exact pattern behind "chat with our company docs", which is the most requested AI build in business right now.

  1. 📚 Gather the corpus. Export your Notion pages, PDFs, and notes into one folder. Quality in, quality out: cut anything outdated before you start.
  2. 🗄️ Store it as vectors. Supabase's free tier includes a vector store. Chunk your documents, embed them, and load them in. Claude can write this entire script for you.
  3. 💬 Chat with citations. A simple Streamlit page: your question goes in, the top matching chunks come back, Claude answers using only those chunks and cites which file each claim came from.

project four

A micro SaaS, actually shipped

Ambitious beginner, about 8 to 12 hours. A real web app with accounts, a database, and a Stripe payment link, live on a real URL, solving one niche problem you actually understand. A deployed product a stranger could pay for beats any certificate on a resume.

  1. 🎯 Pick a one-job problem. Something from your own work: a rate calculator, a brief generator, an audit checklist. If you can describe it in one sentence, it's the right size.
  2. 🏗️ Let Lovable build v1. Describe the app conversationally; Lovable generates the working app with Supabase behind it for auth and data. Free tier gets you a long way, Pro is about $25 if you run out of credits.
  3. 💳 Charge for it. A Stripe payment link is the whole monetization stack: no code, just a link on your pricing page. Ship it, post it, and see if one person pays.

project five

An AI data dashboard

Comfortable with a little code, about 6 to 10 hours. A live, clickable dashboard analyzing a dataset you actually care about: job postings in your field, local rent prices, your own spending. Claude writes the analysis app, you publish it free on Streamlit Community Cloud, and hiring managers get a link instead of a static screenshot.

  1. 📦 Get a real dataset. Scrape it with Claude's help or grab a public one. The analysis only lands if the question is real, so pick data you're genuinely curious about.
  2. 📈 Let Claude write the app. Hand it the CSV and the three questions you want answered. Ask for a Streamlit app with filters, charts, and a written "so what" section per chart.
  3. 🌐 Publish it. Streamlit Community Cloud hosts it free from a GitHub repo. The live URL goes on your LinkedIn, not in a drawer.

before you start

How to actually finish by Sunday

Pick Friday night Choose the project and set up accounts before the weekend starts. Setup friction on Saturday morning is where most weekend projects die.
Build ugly first Get the thing working end to end before making anything pretty. A working ugly demo Saturday night means Sunday is polish, not panic.
Let Claude drive Every project here assumes Claude writes the code and the configs while you make the decisions. Paste errors straight back into the chat; that's the workflow.
Ship with proof Record a 60-second screen demo the moment it works. The demo is what you post, show in interviews, and send to clients.
🌱 Honest expectation. "Complete this weekend" means a working v1 you can demo, not a polished product. That is genuinely enough: the person who shows up Monday with a working agent is in a different category from the person who watched tutorials.
