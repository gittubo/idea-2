One thing before you start. This is a styling and habits mood board, not a verdict on how you look now. Nothing about you needs fixing for this to be fun. The reason it works is that seeing a version of yourself with better sleep, better posture and clothes that actually fit is easier to aim at than a vague plan to "sort myself out".

The photo decides everything

Ninety percent of bad results come from the source photo, not the prompt. The model can only keep what it can see, so a dim, filtered, half-cropped selfie gives it permission to invent a face. Spend two minutes here and the render actually looks like you.

  1. 🌱 Use the back camera. Front cameras are wide-angle and quietly distort your nose and jaw at close range. Prop your phone up, step back, and use the timer or a volume-button shutter instead of holding it at arm's length.
  2. 💡 Get flat, even light. Face a window in daylight, or stand under bright even indoor light. No harsh overhead spotlight, no backlighting, no golden-hour drama. Even light lets the model read your actual skin tone and features instead of guessing at shadows.
  3. 🧠 No filters, no beauty mode. Turn off any smoothing your camera applies by default. If the input is already filtered, the render is a glow-up of a filter, which is nowhere to aim.
  4. 👩‍💻 Frame it plainly. Hair off your face, neutral expression, plain wall behind you, shoulders in frame. Add one full-body shot in a plain outfit if you also want wardrobe and posture ideas.

The exact prompt

Open ChatGPT, attach your photo, and paste this. Image generation is included on the free plan with a daily cap, so if you hit the limit you either wait for it to reset or run it on a paid plan.

🌱 the glow-up render 
Here is a photo of me. Generate an image of a healthier, more put-together version of the same person, keeping my face exactly as it is: same bone structure, same eye shape and spacing, same nose, same jawline, same skin tone, same hairline.

Change only the things that a few months of decent sleep, movement, grooming and styling would change. Clearer, more hydrated looking skin with texture still visible. Posture corrected, shoulders back, chin level, weight evenly on both feet. A fresh, well cut version of my current hair rather than a completely different style. Groomed brows. A simple, well fitted outfit in colours that suit my complexion.

Shoot it like a real photograph, not a beauty filter: soft even daylight, plain neutral background, relaxed natural expression, slight smile. Do not slim or reshape my face or body, do not lighten my skin, do not change my age, and do not smooth my skin into plastic. Keep it realistic enough that someone who knows me would still say that is me.
Two lines in there are doing quiet heavy lifting: "do not slim or reshape" and "do not lighten my skin". Image models drift toward a generic ideal unless you close that door explicitly, and a render that quietly reshapes you is useless as a goal.
when it goes wrong

The fix for "that isn't my face"

The most common failure is a good-looking stranger. When that happens, do not re-run the same prompt hoping for better luck. Send this instead, in the same chat, so it still has your photo in context.

💡 lock my face 
That render changed my face, so try again and treat my face as fixed. Match the photo I uploaded exactly on eye shape and spacing, nose shape and width, lip shape, jawline, chin, hairline, skin tone and undertone, and any freckles or marks you can see.

Only hair styling, clothing, posture, lighting and background are allowed to change. Before you generate anything, write me one short paragraph describing what you are keeping identical, so I can check you actually read the photo. Then generate the image.

Two more things that reliably help: attach two or three photos of yourself from different angles instead of one, and iterate in small steps ("keep everything, change only the hair") rather than rewriting the whole prompt each time. If it still drifts, a stronger image model handles likeness better. Reve is the one I reach for when a render has to actually look like the person, and it is paid rather than free, so treat it as the upgrade step rather than the starting point.

Three more prompts worth running

Same photo, different question. Each one answers something you would otherwise pay someone to tell you.

The haircut test, so you stop screenshotting styles that will not work on your hair:

🌱 the haircut test 
Using my photo, generate three separate images of me with three different haircuts that would genuinely suit my face shape, hair texture and hairline. Keep my face identical in all three and change nothing else: same lighting, same plain background, same top.

For each one, write two lines underneath: what the cut is actually called so I can ask for it in a salon, and how much upkeep it needs between appointments. Rule out anything that only works with daily heat styling if my hair texture will not hold it.

The wardrobe test, for the version of you that owns clothes that fit:

💡 the wardrobe test 
Here is a full body photo of me. Generate four images of me in four different outfits that suit my proportions and colouring, for these settings: everyday errands, work or study, a nice dinner, and something more dressed up. Keep my face and body exactly as they are and change only the clothes, shoes and posture.

Under each image, list the individual pieces in plain language so I could search for them, name the colours that are doing the work, and tell me which single piece I could buy first that would work across all four outfits.

The lock screen version, which is the whole point of the exercise:

🧠 the lock screen crop 
Take the glow-up render you just made and give me a version sized as a vertical phone wallpaper, 9 by 16, with my face and shoulders in the upper two thirds and calm empty space at the bottom so my clock and notifications do not sit on my face.

Keep the same person, the same lighting and the same styling as the render, just recomposed. Do not add any text, quotes or graphics to the image.
make it real

Turn the render into a 30 day plan

An image on your lock screen is a reminder, not a plan. This is the step most people skip, and it is the one that makes the whole thing worth doing. Run it in Claude or ChatGPT, whichever you already have open.

👩‍💻 close the gap 
I generated an AI image of a more put-together version of myself and I want to turn it into something real rather than something I stare at. Here is what is actually different in the render compared to my current photo: [list the differences, for example posture, haircut, skin looking calmer, clothes that fit].

Build me a 30 day plan in three lanes. Lane one: things that are free and start today, like posture, a consistent sleep window, and how I stand and angle myself in photos. Lane two: things under [your budget], like a haircut, brow shaping, two or three wardrobe pieces, and a basic skincare routine. Lane three: the things that honestly take longer than 30 days, so I stop expecting them in a fortnight.

For every item give me the specific action, roughly what it costs, and how long it realistically takes to show. Then tell me the three things that would make the biggest visible difference first, and keep the tone kind and matter of fact rather than motivational.
The lane three answer is the useful one. Most of what people hate about their own photos is lighting, posture and fit, and all three are free and fixable this week. The slow stuff is genuinely slow, and knowing which is which is what stops you quitting in a fortnight.
the honest bit

It is a render, not a photo

Do not post it as a before and after, do not use it on a dating profile, a CV or a professional headshot, and do not let anyone believe it is a real photo of you. Images from the big generators carry Content Credentials metadata that anyone can check at contentcredentials.org/verify, so passing one off as real is both unkind and easy to catch. Label it as AI if you share it at all.
Only your own face Do not generate glow-ups of other people from photos you have of them, even as a joke. It is their face, not your material.
Watch the drift If the render only looks good because it made you thinner, paler or younger, throw it out and re-run with the guardrail lines. That version is not you and it is not a goal.

Used properly it is a mood board with your face on it: haircut, posture, colours, fit. Used badly it is a machine for feeling worse about a face that was never the problem. Keep it in the first category. 🌱
