the basics

What a connector actually is

On its own, Claude cannot see live websites. A connector gives it permission to talk to one specific service on your behalf. Connect Indeed and Claude can pull real job listings, read the resume on your Indeed profile, and look up company data, all inside the chat you are already in.

Indeed's connector gives Claude exactly four abilities, and it is worth knowing them because your prompts land better when you ask for something it can actually do (connector listing, Indeed's own docs).

🔎 Job search Searches live listings by title, keywords, location and employment type. Returns titles, companies, locations, salaries where disclosed, and apply links.
📄 Job detail Pulls the full posting for a specific job: description, requirements, qualifications, benefits and company info.
🧾 Get resume Reads the experience on your own Indeed profile, so Claude can compare you against a listing without you pasting anything.
🏢 Company data Employer research from Indeed: satisfaction, compensation, culture, management and reviews.
There is no fifth ability, and that is on purpose. The connector is read-only. It searches and reads, it does not submit applications, and Claude will not press apply for you. You review and send every single application yourself. That is the version of this you want: an automated job hunt that fires off hundreds of applications is how people end up with their name on a role they cannot describe in the interview.
the setup

Setup, about 3 minutes

  1. 📄 Update your Indeed resume first. Make sure the resume on your Indeed profile is current. The "get resume" ability reads it from there, so an old profile means Claude is matching you against an old version of you.
  2. 🔌 Open your connectors. In Claude, open Customize, then Connectors. You can also reach the same list from the search and tools menu next to the chat box, under add connectors.
  3. ➕ Add Indeed. Find Indeed in the directory and click to add it. Sign in with your Indeed account when the authorisation window appears. If you do not have an account, create one first, since the resume ability needs a profile to read.
  4. 🔁 Switch it on in the chat. Connectors are toggled per conversation. Before you start a new job-hunt chat, open the search and tools menu and make sure Indeed is on, or Claude will answer from memory instead of live listings.
  5. ✅ Sanity check it. Ask "search Indeed for [your role] in [your city] and show me three listings with links". If you get real links you can click, you are connected. If you get vague descriptions with no links, the toggle is off.

Build a Job Hunt project

A Claude Project keeps your context in one place so you are not re-explaining your career in every new chat. Click Projects in the left sidebar, then New project, and name it "Job hunt". Projects are available on the free plan, capped at five (Anthropic's Projects article).

Paste this into the project's instructions box. Anything in square brackets is a placeholder, so swap it for your own details before you save.

🗂️ the project instructions 
You are helping me run my job search. Use the Indeed connector for anything involving live listings, and always link to the actual posting so I can check it myself.

My situation: target role [role], target locations [cities or remote], salary floor [number and currency], seniority I am aiming for [level], companies I am interested in [list], the three things I am genuinely best at [list], and things I will not accept [commute, shift work, industries, whatever applies].

Rules for this project. Never invent a listing, a salary or a company detail, and if the connector returns nothing, tell me it returned nothing instead of filling the gap from memory. Never write a resume bullet or a cover letter claim that is not already true in my experience, and if a listing wants something I do not have, say so plainly rather than dressing it up. I submit every application myself, so your job is to shortlist, compare and draft, never to apply.

Every chat you start inside that project already knows all of this, so the prompts below stay short.

1. The highest paying roles that actually match you

This is the one from the reel. The trick is asking for pay and fit together, because the top of the pay range is usually a role you are not a credible candidate for, and sorting by salary alone hands you a list of polite rejections.

💰 the match search 
Pull my Indeed resume, then search Indeed for [role type] roles in [city or remote] posted in the last 14 days. Find the highest paying ones where I am a genuine match, meaning my last 18 months of experience already cover most of what the listing asks for.

Return the top 5 ranked by pay, and for each one give me the title, company, disclosed salary (and flag clearly when a listing does not disclose pay rather than estimating it), the link to the posting, one line on why I am a credible candidate, and the single biggest gap between me and the requirements.

Then, separately, list any roles you found that pay more but where I am a stretch candidate, and tell me what I would need to add to my experience to become credible for them. Do not pad the main list with roles I cannot honestly get.

2. Tailor your resume for one specific role

The second half of the reel. Job descriptions repeat their real priorities, and matching that vocabulary matters both for the human skim and for whatever screening software sits in front of them. Rewording what you did is fair. Claiming what you did not do is not, and it falls apart in the interview.

✍️ the tailored resume 
Here is the Indeed listing: [paste the link or job ID]. Pull the full job details, then pull my Indeed resume.

Rewrite my experience bullets for this specific role. For each bullet, show the original and the tailored version side by side, and name which phrase from the job description you mirrored and why. Lead every bullet with the outcome and keep the numbers exactly as they are in my original, because inflating a metric is the fastest way to lose an offer at reference stage.

Do not add any skill, tool or responsibility that is not already somewhere in my experience. Where the listing asks for something I genuinely do not have, leave it out of the resume and list it separately at the end as a real gap, with an honest one-line suggestion for how I could address it in the interview.
Keep a master version. Tailor a copy for each application and never overwrite your original. Three weeks in, you will have eight tailored versions and no memory of which one you sent where, so put the company name in every filename. 💡

3. Skills gap audit before you apply

Run this before you decide whether a role is worth an hour of your evening. It tells you what is missing, which is usually a faster decision than reading the posting three times.

🧠 the skills gap audit 
Here is the Indeed job link or job ID: [paste]. Pull the full job details and compare them against my Indeed resume.

List the top 5 skills or keywords in the listing that are missing from my resume, ranked by how often each one appears in the posting, since repetition is the clearest signal of what they actually care about. For each one, tell me whether it is a hard requirement or a nice to have based on how it is phrased.

Then give me a verdict in one line: apply now, apply after I add [specific thing], or skip. Be willing to tell me to skip.

4. Salary research without anchoring low

Do not anchor to your current salary, and do not anchor to the median. Anchor to what the level above yours is advertised at, using listings that actually disclose pay.

📊 the salary research 
Search Indeed for [role title, one level above mine] in [city or remote]. From the listings that include disclosed pay, give me the range and the median, and tell me exactly how many listings the median is based on so I know how much weight to put on it.

Pull three specific listings as named data points with links, so I can open them myself. Where most listings hide pay, say that plainly rather than estimating a range from nowhere.

Then suggest a negotiation target band based on those data points and nothing else, and give me the one sentence I would say to justify the top of that band using my own experience.

5. Reverse-engineer the hiring manager

The strongest applications land before a recruiter has formed an opinion. Find the person who owns the role and give them a reason to remember your name.

🔦 the hiring manager dig 
Here is the Indeed listing: [paste]. Pull the company data from Indeed, then search the web for who currently holds this role or the manager likely hiring for it.

Find their public profile and anything recent they have written, posted or said publicly. From that, tell me the three things they appear to care about right now that are not written in the job description, and be explicit about which of those three is an inference rather than something they stated.

Then draft a four sentence message I could send them before I apply. It should reference one specific thing they said, connect it to one specific thing I have done, and ask a real question. No flattery, no "I would love to pick your brain".

6. Predict the interview from real listings

You can anticipate most of the questions if you read enough postings for the same role. Claude can read twenty of them in under a minute.

🎤 the interview predictor 
Pull the last 10 Indeed listings for [target role] in [city or remote]. Extract the skills, tools and phrasing that appear in three or more of them, since that overlap is what the market considers core to this role.

From that pattern, predict the 7 most likely interview questions. For each, give me a STAR-format answer skeleton mapped to a specific bullet in my Indeed resume, and leave the specifics as blanks for me to fill in rather than writing my stories for me.

Finish with the two questions I am least prepared for based on the gaps in my experience, and tell me honestly how I should handle each one.
the honest bit

Two rules that decide whether this works

🎯 Filter, do not spray Volume is the disease, not the cure. Three applications where you are the obvious fit beat thirty mediocre ones, and Claude is a filter before it is a factory.
🔗 Open the links Click through to every listing before you apply. Connectors return real data, but you are the one whose name goes on the application, so read the posting with your own eyes.
If this is your first time using a connector, the job hunt is a good one to start on. It is read-only, you can see every result, and nothing irreversible happens without you clicking it. 🌱
