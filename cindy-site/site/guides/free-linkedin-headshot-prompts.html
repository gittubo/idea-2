pick your route

Two ways to get your face in

📱 Route A: reference selfies, no training
Attach 3 to 5 clear selfies to every prompt in ChatGPT and tell it to keep your face exactly. Nothing to set up, and ChatGPT's free tier gives you a couple of looks a day. Expect a little drift around the jawline and eye spacing.
🧬 Route B: train a character once
Give a character-reference tool about 20 photos once and it builds a version of you that stays the same person across every prompt. Best likeness and best for a full set of ten. You can start this one for free: Krea's free plan trains on up to 50 images and gives you 100 credits a day.

Both routes run the same prompts below, because the prompts never describe the face. Tools worth knowing for route B: Krea (free to train, then US$5.25 a month), Freepik, Midjourney's character reference, and Higgsfield Soul. One catch on Krea's free plan is that it has no commercial licence, so if the photo is for a business or a client you need the paid plan. I used a trained character for these ten.

the craft

How to make it actually look like you

  1. 📸 Feed it recent, boring photos About 20 from the last few months, well lit, face clearly visible, different angles and expressions, including one full-length. No sunglasses, no hats, no filters, no group shots where it has to guess which person is you.
  2. 🚫 Never describe your own face in the prompt The moment a prompt says "almond eyes" or "sharp jawline", the AI starts drawing that description instead of you. Describe only the outfit, the pose, the light and the background.
  3. 🎨 Keep the hex palette under the prompt Each prompt below ends with a list of hex colours. That's what holds the whole set together visually, so the ten images look like one shoot instead of ten random photos.
  4. 🔁 Generate 4 at a time, keep 1 Run each prompt as a batch of four and pick the best. Likeness varies between generations of the exact same prompt, so the throwaways are part of the process, not a mistake.
  5. 👀 Check the hands, ears and text Hands and jewellery are where these models still fail, and any text in the image is usually gibberish. Look at the about-me collage below and you can read the nonsense words for yourself. Crop text out or regenerate rather than posting it.
  6. ™️ Watch for copied watermarks If your reference screenshot came from a magazine or a brand shoot, the model often copies the logo too. Three of my ten came back with magazine text in the corner. Either crop it, or add a line to the prompt saying the image has no text, logos or watermarks anywhere.

The 2-minute check

✅ Would a colleague recognise you?
Put the generated photo next to a real one on your phone. If a friend hesitates, the likeness isn't there yet, so regenerate rather than settling.
✅ Does it match your actual life?
A conference stage shot is great if you speak at conferences. Pick the looks that match the work you do, not the fantasy version.
💡 Use one, not ten. Pick the single strongest image for your LinkedIn profile photo and keep the rest for your banner, your about section, a slide deck or a speaker bio. A profile photo you change every week costs you recognisability.
