the trade

Why hidden text is bad maths

Researchers analysed 196,682 real resumes and found 2,030 with hidden prompt injections, about 1% and stated as a lower bound. More than 90% were not commands aimed at an AI at all, just concealed keyword lists and invented experience. Two things the paper is honest about: the authors never tested whether it works (their data had no hiring outcomes attached), and the detectors they built are already running in a live recruiting platform.

Then a 2026 study tested injections against real screening models and found the advantage collapses toward zero as more people do it. At around 80% adoption the benefit is nil, and it helps weak candidates most, which is exactly who cannot survive the interview after.

⚠️ The tactic is eating itself, and the record is permanent. Greenhouse found 41% of US job seekers claim to be doing this, against roughly 1% actually measured. Whether or not it lands, the hidden text sits in the parsed record for as long as the employer keeps your application.

And here is the mechanical irony: colour is a rendering property, but text extraction reads the text layer and never checks the colour. White-on-white text is invisible to a human and perfectly visible to the machine. As recruiter Mike Peditto puts it, "if you have to hide the keywords in white font, it means you didn't have them anywhere already in black font." It is catalogued as LLM01 in the OWASP GenAI Top 10: a vulnerability in someone else's system, not a career strategy.

the real mechanic

What actually rejects you automatically

In a 2025 study, 23 of 25 recruiters interviewed said their systems do not auto-reject resumes for formatting, content or design. But all 25 used knockout questions, and those genuinely do fire automatically. Oracle Taleo's docs say a candidate failing one "can be instantly exited from the application process". SAP SuccessFactors has a status literally called Auto-Disqualified.

Knockouts are binary because a law binding the employer makes them binary: work authorisation, licences, clearance, minimum ages. Nothing in your resume overrides those.

☑️ Spot one by its shape Vendors restrict knockout rules to yes/no and dropdown questions by design, because a rule has to evaluate cleanly. If it is a free text box, it is not a knockout.
🙋 A human wrote the rule Greenhouse tells candidates its applications are "reviewed by real people, one by one", that AI "doesn't score or rank applications", and that auto-rejection runs on rules "set and controlled directly by recruiters".

So the risk is not really rejection, it is burial. Where ranking is switched on, nothing rejects you. A human just never scrolls far enough down.

the thing you're reverse engineering

The scorecard exists before the job is posted

Before a role goes live, the recruiter and hiring manager hold a kickoff meeting, which Greenhouse says "should happen before a role is opened". They answer a fixed set of questions, and the answers fork into two places: the scorecard every interviewer is later scored against, and the job description you read. Same meeting, same answers. So reverse engineering one from the other is not a metaphor.

The questions they answer about you are: what will a superstar accomplish in a year, what would they need to do in the first 90 days, what is non-negotiable, and what is merely coachable.

🔎 Read for the 90-day outcome. Career advice says the first bullet matters most. What is actually documented is that the kickoff works backwards from the one-year goal to the 90-day plan, so the bullet describing an early outcome is the one the hiring manager answered a real question about. It can sit anywhere in the list.

A finished scorecard is roughly 15 to 30 attributes in four categories. That is the whole universe you are measured against:

CategoryThe question it answersReal example attributes
DetailsAre they even eligible?Able to work in the US, fits salary range, willing to travel
QualificationsDo they have the background?Engineering degree, HR certification, SaaS experience
SkillsCan they do the work?Event coordination, lead generation, budget management
TraitsWill they add to the culture?Entrepreneurial, likes to learn, passionate

The exercise: sort every line of the job description into those four. Bucket one is where the knockouts hide. Buckets two and three are what a resume can evidence. Bucket four is what the interview is for.

Then the detail that should change what you write. Interviewers score you on two different scales: five points per attribute, including a "Mixed or Neutral" middle, and four points overall with no middle at all. Evidence is allowed to be partial. The hire call is not. Your whole job is moving attributes out of that neutral column, because it does not survive the overall rating.

👥 It is five to ten people, not a crowd. That is how many interviewers a hiring manager expects ratings from, each scoring a handful of named attributes. And in the decision meeting Greenhouse coaches teams to challenge vague assessments with one question: "Can you provide an example?" Someone will be asked to produce yours. A keyword cannot survive that sentence.
stop disqualifying yourself

Required versus preferred is a manufactured split

That distinction is a deliberate instruction, not decoration. Greenhouse's kickoff worksheet tells recruiters to "stick to the need-to-haves" and, for anything else, "be sure to label it as such". Workable has them ask hiring managers outright: "should we immediately disqualify candidates who don't have X?"

The US federal government defines both precisely. A selective factor is "absolutely required because a person cannot perform successfully in the position without" it, and missing it makes you "ineligible for further consideration". A quality ranking factor could "significantly enhance performance, but are not essential", and candidates who have it "may be ranked above those who do not, but no one may be rated ineligible solely for failure to possess" it.

✅ One wall, one sort order. Required screens you out. Preferred ranks you lower but keeps you in. Someone already argued that item down on your behalf, so applying without a preferred qualification is doing what the posting invites.

On the line that men apply at 60% of qualifications and women at 100%: it traces to an unpublished internal report with no author, date or method, and a 2024 paper ran four studies and found "no gender difference emerged in participants' application intentions, neither at 60% nor at 100% qualification fit". The real finding from the original article is better anyway. Asked why they did not apply, 41% of women and 46% of men said they assumed they would not be hired and did not want to waste the effort. Nearly identical, and as its author put it, the barrier was "not a mistaken perception about themselves, but a mistaken perception about the hiring process".

the heart of it

Evidence versus keyword

An assertion names a quality. Evidence names something that happened which a third party could confirm. Laszlo Bock, who ran hiring at Google, published the conversion:

🧾 the formula, in his words 
Accomplished [X] as measured by [Y] by doing [Z]

Start with an active verb, numerically measure what you accomplished,
provide a baseline for comparison, and detail what you did.

The baseline is the part people drop and the part that does the work. Bock: "Adding ($1.2M) anticipates the reviewer's question about whether 12% is a big deal or not. If you improved investment results by 12%, but that meant going from $100 to $112, that's not too impressive." A percentage with no denominator is a keyword wearing a number costume.

💊 Other industries regulate this exact claim shape. The US Food and Drug Administration tells drug companies a relative figure alone is "harder to comprehend and more favorable", and they must give "the corresponding absolute probability measures" alongside. Its own example: "reduced the risk of stroke by 50% (1% of patients treated with Drug X had a stroke, compared to 2% in the control group)." That is structurally identical to a resume bullet.

His own rewrites, where the claim usually stays identical and only the proof changes:

BeforeAfter
Managed sorority budgetManaged $31,000 Spring 2014 budget and invested $10,000 in idle funds in high-yielding capital notes returning 5% over the year
Skills: Excellent customer service skills. Friendly and positive attitudeExcellent customer service skills and positive attitude as demonstrated by receiving employee of the month in four consecutive months in 2014
Achieved annual business plan commitments for volumes, model mix, wholesale revenue and brandAs a team member, contributed to 21% increase in advertiser spend by achieving 158% of target customer contacts (80 per week) and 192% of target interaction depth (20 minutes per customer)
Responsible for negotiating service contracts with XYZNegotiated 30% ($500k) reduction in costs with XYZ by designing and using results from an online auction of multiple vendors

Notice the third: Bock added the hedge "as a team member, contributed to". Shrinking a claim to the truth made it more credible. And this works without a corporate job. Here he is on waiting tables: "Exhibited the spirit of Hospitaliano by achieving 120% of dessert sales targets (compared to an average of 98%) and averaging 26% in tips per night."

🔬 A naked metric invites the probe Amazon's leadership principles tell its own people that leaders "are skeptical when metrics and anecdote differ". A number with no story attached is the thing that makes someone dig.
💰 Ownership is a pay grade Dropbox's public career ladder puts "I execute on defined projects" at IC2 and "I own and deliver projects" at IC3. A published pay framework prices that exact gap as a full level.
why truth wins

The padding targets the weakest signal there is

Industrial psychologists measure how well each hiring method predicts actual job performance:

How you're assessedPredicts performance at
Structured interview.42, the best predictor there is
Job knowledge test.40
Work sample.33
Unstructured interview.19
Years of experience.07
🎯 Read those two bold rows together. The thing people pad hardest is the weakest predictor on the list. The structured interview, where a padded claim has to survive live questioning, is the strongest. Padding optimises the .07. The decision gets made in the .42.

Deception research finds liars and truth tellers separate most sharply on unanticipated questions, the detail layer nobody rehearses. Worth being precise though: one study found follow-up questioning can actually increase polish. What probing reliably does is separate people with real specificity from people generating more of it, and the tell is not how much detail you give but how much verifiable detail. Names, dates, numbers somebody else also saw. You can invent detail. You cannot invent checkable detail.

⭐ Which is why STAR is worth using. The US Office of Personnel Management publishes it as guidance for the interviewer, on how to build and score questions. It is not a candidate trick, it is the shape of the scoresheet. Note the split: a resume bullet uses X, Y, Z and strips pronouns because it is a claim. A STAR answer uses "I" and tells the story, because it is the evidence hearing for that claim.
test it, don't guess

Parsing, proved in 10 seconds

Here is a two-column resume run through a standard text extractor. The sidebar and the main column interleave:

JANE CANDIDATE
CONTACT

EXPERIENCE

jane@example.com

Data Analyst, Acme Corp
Jan 2022 to Present

0400 000 000

"EXPERIENCE" is stranded next to "CONTACT" and the job title is severed from its heading, so a parser assigning content by proximity files the role under contact details. PDF has no guaranteed reading order, so extractors reconstruct it heuristically and a two-column grid defeats the heuristic.

Run this on your own resume:

pdftotext resume.pdf -          # raw stream, reveals column interleaving
pdffonts resume.pdf             # empty table means no text layer at all
pdftotext resume.pdf - | wc -c  # near zero means it is an image, not text

No terminal? Open the PDF in a browser, select all, copy, paste into a plain text editor. That is roughly what the machine gets.

💀 What genuinely breaks Scanned or image-only PDFs are fatal: a page yielding 329 characters as text yielded one when rasterised. Contact details in the header or footer, and tables that scramble reading order, are the other real failures.
✅ What no longer breaks Fancy bullet characters survived testing byte-perfect, and "never use two columns" is out of date. One controlled test found the loss shrinks to about three points once an AI model reads the text.
🧐 Notice who publishes the formatting rules. Parser vendors document almost nothing about what breaks extraction. The detailed "ATS hates tables" lists come from companies selling resume tools, which is the same incentive that produced the "75% of resumes are auto-rejected" myth. That traces to a 2012 sales pitch by a company that shut down in 2013 and never published a study. Test your own file instead of trusting any of us.

The ATS scorecard builder prompt

You need the complete job description, your resume, and a proof bank of projects, results, dates, tools, links and qualifications. Strip your home address and phone number first, since none of it improves the output.

📋 the ATS scorecard builder 
You are helping me prepare truthfully for an applicant screen. Do not invent experience, keywords, credentials, metrics, tools, dates, responsibilities, personality traits, or outcomes.

JOB DESCRIPTION
[PASTE THE COMPLETE JOB DESCRIPTION]

CURRENT RESUME
[PASTE RESUME]

MY PROOF BANK
[PASTE PROJECTS, RESULTS, LINKS, QUALIFICATIONS, AND REAL EXAMPLES]

STAGE 1: BUILD THE LIKELY SCORECARD
Sort every line of the job description into the four categories employers actually use:
1. DETAILS: eligibility and knockouts (work authorisation, location, licences, salary range, travel)
2. QUALIFICATIONS: credentials and background
3. SKILLS: work product and demonstrable ability
4. TRAITS: behavioural signals

Then extract:
- Knockout criteria, stated separately
- Required versus preferred, kept strictly apart
- The 90-day or first-year outcome the role exists to deliver
- Claims that would require proof

Quote the exact words from the job description for every criterion. Mark each as explicit, strongly implied, or uncertain. Do not pretend to know the employer's proprietary system.

STAGE 2: MAP MY EVIDENCE
Build a table with:
- criterion
- my exact supporting evidence
- source location in my resume or proof bank
- strength: strong, partial, missing, or contradictory
- what a recruiter may still need to verify

Do not treat a keyword as evidence. An assertion names a quality; evidence names something that happened which a third party could confirm.

STAGE 3: RUN THE INTEGRITY PASS
Flag:
- inflated or ambiguous claims
- any metric with no baseline or denominator
- experience that sounds more senior than the proof
- tool names unsupported by an example
- inconsistent titles or timelines

If evidence is missing, write [GAP: the exact proof I need]. Do not fill it.

STAGE 4: REWRITE FOR RECRUITER AND PARSER
Propose the smallest truthful edits that:
- use the employer's language where it accurately matches my experience
- follow "accomplished X as measured by Y by doing Z", always including the baseline
- put required evidence near the relevant role
- use standard headings and remove decorative tables, icons and columns that may parse poorly
- preserve my voice

For every changed bullet, show:
ORIGINAL
WHY IT MAY FAIL
REVISED
EVIDENCE USED

FINAL OUTPUT
1. Likely scorecard, in the four categories
2. Evidence map
3. Integrity risks
4. Revised resume
5. Five interview questions built from the remaining gaps, with the verifiable detail I would need to answer each
judging it

Four states, and one rule

✅ Strong Requirement and evidence clearly match, with a number and a baseline.
🟡 Partial Proof exists but scope or result is incomplete. This is the neutral column, and it is the one to attack.
⬜ Missing No honest evidence. Leave it missing and go get the proof.
🔴 Contradictory The resume says something the timeline does not support. Fix this first.
🚫 Never let Claude turn "missing" into polished fiction. A gap you can name is an interview answer you can prepare. A gap it papers over is one you walk into the room still carrying.

That matters because credential claims do not expire. MIT's dean of admissions resigned in 2007 over degrees claimed on a 1979 application. In R v Andrewes [2022] UKSC 24 the UK Supreme Court noted a man with faked degrees "did a good job" and was appraised "strong or outstanding", then ordered confiscation of his earnings uplift anyway. Closer to home, Tham v Hertz [2018] FWC 3967 upheld a dismissal for overstated resume dates, finding they were "not only intentional they were also misleading". Being good at the job is not a defence.

🧾 Being straight about the law. There is no reported case anywhere of an applicant prosecuted for prompt injection, and anyone saying otherwise is inventing it. The realistic risk is quieter and quite enough: rejection, a withdrawn offer, or a dismissal that survives challenge.
