the week at a glance

Six days, six badges

  1. Day 1 · Claude 101. 2.5 hr. Everyday Claude: projects, artifacts, skills, connectors. The only day that works fully on a free account.
  2. Day 2 · AI Fluency for small businesses. 4 hr. The 4D framework on real business work, plus the data and privacy side.
  3. Day 3 · AI Fluency for creative work. 5 hr. The long one, built with two universities.
  4. Day 4 · AI Fluency for builders. 3 hr. Problem to shipped product, with judgment built in.
  5. Day 5 · Claude Code 101. 1.5 hr. The densest 90 minutes here. Needs a paid plan to practise.
  6. Day 6 · Claude Code in action. 1 hr. The shortest day. Long hands-off sessions.
Two honest numbers before you start. The six add up to exactly 17 hours (2.5 + 4 + 5 + 3 + 1.5 + 1), so this is a real week rather than six quick evenings. And day 5 is the only one where you need a paid Claude plan or an API key to follow along hands on. Everything else works on a free account.
how it works

How the badge actually works

Every course on Claude Academy follows the same shape, so once you have done one you know the whole system. And the "free" claim is not mine, it is Anthropic's, printed on the Academy FAQ: "Academy certificates of completion (badges) are free and are earned by passing a course's quizzes on academy.claude.com. The Claude certification exams are a separate paid, proctored program."

  1. 🔑 Make a free account and sign in first. The course pages show a "Sign in to save progress" prompt. Work through signed out and your progress is not saved, so there is nothing to attach a badge to.
  2. 📖 Work through the lessons in order. Each course is a set of short video and reading lessons down the left-hand side.
  3. 🧠 Pass the course quiz. Every one of these six ends with a single quiz, and it is the last item in the lesson list.
  4. 🏅 The badge appears after the quiz. On several of these, "Completion badge" is literally the final item in the curriculum, sitting under "Course quiz". That is how you can tell before you start that a course actually issues one.
  5. 📌 Add it to LinkedIn. Licenses and certifications, issuer Anthropic, course name written exactly as the course page writes it.
These are badges, and the paid exams are a different thing entirely. Anthropic also runs Claude Certified Associate and Claude Certified Architect. Those are the "separate paid, proctored program" the FAQ is talking about, and they a separate programme rather than something you can simply sit from the course page. Nothing in this week gets you one, and nothing in this week costs you anything either. Do not let a listicle blur the two.
Go to academy.claude.com, not the old site. Anthropic's older learning site at anthropic.skilljar.com is still online, looks almost identical, and carries no notice that things have moved. If you land there you hit a registration flow for a course that is sitting free on the new site. If the address bar says skilljar, close it and type academy.claude.com instead.
all six links

Day 1: Claude 101

Open the course · 13 lessons · 2.5 hr · completion badge, works on any plan including Free

What it is. The everyday-Claude course, and the only one here that works start to finish on a free Claude account. Better prompts, Projects, artifacts, Skills, connectors and Research.

What it actually teaches. Not "what is AI". It teaches the parts of Claude most people never open: keeping context in a Project instead of re-explaining yourself every chat, getting a real artifact out instead of a wall of text, and connecting Claude to the tools you already use.

What you walk away with. A completion badge, and the reason every other day on this list makes sense.

The honest bit. The stated requirement is "a Claude account on any plan (Free, Pro, Max, Team, or Enterprise)" and "no prior experience with Claude or other AI assistants needed". A couple of lessons demonstrate features that live on paid or Team plans, but you can complete the course and earn the badge on the free tier.

Do this one first even if you already use Claude daily. It is where the vocabulary for the rest of the week comes from, and it is the shortest route to having a badge on your profile by Monday night.

Day 2: AI Fluency for small businesses

Open the course · 9 lessons + quiz · 4 hr · completion badge

What it is. The 4D framework applied to actual business work: research, customer data and day-to-day operations, plus the data hygiene and privacy side that most AI content skips entirely.

What it actually teaches. How to decide what to hand to AI and what to keep, using a repeatable framework rather than instinct. The customer-data lessons are the useful ones, because that is where most people quietly do something they should not.

What you walk away with. A completion badge, and a defensible answer when someone at work asks whether it is safe to put company information into Claude.

The honest bit. The page recommends doing "AI Fluency: Framework and Foundations" first for a deeper understanding. It is a recommendation, not a gate, and this course covers the foundations lightly on its own. It also suggests having an AI tool open for practice, which the free tier covers.

Day 3: AI Fluency for creative work

Open the course · 8 lessons + quiz · 5 hr · completion badge

What it is. The longest day of the week and the most academically serious thing on this list. It was written and developed by Anthropic in partnership with Professor Rick Dakan from Ringling College of Art and Design and Professor Joseph Feller from University College Cork.

What it actually teaches. The Creative Value Lens and the Production Lens: two ways of deciding where AI genuinely helps a creative process, and where handing it over costs you the thing that made the work yours. This is the course for anyone who makes things for a living and is nervous about the answer.

What you walk away with. A completion badge, and two named frameworks you can actually cite, from two real universities.

The honest bit. Five hours is a lot for one evening. Split it across two nights, or move it to a weekend day and shuffle a shorter course into the midweek slot. Like days 2 and 4, this one recommends "AI Fluency: Framework and Foundations" first.

Day 4: AI Fluency for builders

Open the course · 9 lessons · 3 hr · completion badge

What it is. The course about taking something from a customer problem to a shipped product with AI in the loop, without shipping something broken.

What it actually teaches. Decomposing a real problem, writing acceptance tests before any code exists, and judging AI output through a set of lenses rather than by whether it looks right. The lessons "Discernment for code", "Discernment for user experience" and "Stand behind what you build" are the spine of it.

What you walk away with. A completion badge, and the habit that separates people who ship AI-assisted work from people who ship AI-assisted bugs.

The honest bit. Same recommended prerequisite as day 2, and the same suggestion to have an AI tool open while you work through it. You do not need to be a developer, but the examples lean toward building software.

Day 5: Claude Code 101

Open the course · 12 lessons · 1.5 hr · completion badge, needs a paid plan or an API key to practise

What it is. The proper introduction to Claude Code, which is Claude running in your terminal with access to your actual files instead of in a chat window.

What it actually teaches. The explore, plan, code, commit workflow, the context window and how to stop wasting it, the CLAUDE.md file, subagents, Skills, MCP and hooks. This is the densest 90 minutes on the entire list.

What you walk away with. A completion badge, and the reason people who use Claude Code stop using the chat window for real work.

This is the one day that needs money to follow along. The course is free and the badge is free, but the prerequisites say you will need "a Claude account (Pro, Max, or Enterprise) or an API key", plus basic familiarity with a code editor and the command line. You can watch the whole course and earn the badge without any of that. You just cannot practise alongside it on a free plan, so do not start day 5 expecting to type along.

Day 6: Claude Code in action

Open the course · 9 lessons + quiz · 1 hr · completion badge

What it is. The shortest day of the week and the direct follow-on from day 5. It is about long, hands-off sessions: setting Claude Code running on something real and having it still be right when you come back.

What it actually teaches. Steering long sessions, writing a CLAUDE.md that actually gets followed, permission modes, routines and headless runs, GitHub Actions and code review, and plugins.

What you walk away with. A completion badge, and the part almost nobody learns, which is how to verify work you did not watch happen.

The honest bit. This is the hardest course here for a non-technical viewer, and the page says so in its own way: it assumes "you already use Claude Code for single prompts" and expects basic familiarity with Git and the command line. Do day 5 first and it is manageable. Skip day 5 and it will not be.

the bit other lists leave out

There is a prerequisite, and you may already have it

All three AI Fluency courses here, days 2, 3 and 4, recommend doing "AI Fluency: Framework and Foundations" first. I am not going to hide that, because it changes how you plan the week.

If you did my earlier 7 days, 7 certificates week, you have already done it. Framework and Foundations was day 3 of that one, so the prerequisite is behind you and days 2, 3 and 4 here land properly. If you have not done it, it is on Claude Academy like everything else, and it is worth an evening before day 2 rather than after. That earlier week is here.

It is a recommendation rather than a hard gate, so you can start at day 2 without it. You will just be picking up the 4D framework as you go instead of having it already.

after the badges

Making them count

  1. 📌 List them the day you earn them. LinkedIn, Licenses and certifications, issuer Anthropic, course name written exactly as the course page writes it. Six separate entries across one week reads as momentum in a way one entry never does.
  2. 🗣 Be accurate about what they are. Anthropic's own FAQ calls these certificates of completion (badges), so that is the phrase to use. They are not accredited qualifications, and they are not the separate paid, proctored exams. They do not expire, so there is nothing to renew. And they are not the Claude Certified Associate or Architect exams, which are paid and proctored. Saying all of that plainly costs you nothing, and it is the kind of precision that reads as competence.
  3. 🔨 Pair one badge with one thing you made. After day 1, build something small in a Project. After day 5, point Claude Code at a folder and let it do a real job. A badge next to a working example is a different conversation from a badge on its own.
