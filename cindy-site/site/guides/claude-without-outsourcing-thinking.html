Think with Claude, not for you: the prompt set that keeps you sharp

There is a real, slightly unsettling idea behind this reel. Researchers call it gradual disempowerment: the slow drift where we hand more and more of our reading, writing, and deciding to AI, until one day we quietly stop trusting our own judgment. It is not a robots-take-over story, it is a use-it-wrong story, and the fix is not deleting the app. The fix is changing HOW you prompt so the thinking stays yours.

Below is exactly how to do that: the mindset, the copy-paste prompts, and the one habit that turns Claude from a crutch into a sparring partner.

the research

Why this actually matters

This is not a vibe, there is real research behind it, so here is the honest version.

  1. 🧩 "Gradual disempowerment" is a real term. It comes from a 2025 paper by Jan Kulveit and co-authors arguing that AI can erode human agency slowly and without any dramatic takeover, just by us handing over more and more of our decisions. Worth knowing the actual source so you can cite it, not just vibe it. Source: arxiv.org/abs/2501.16946.
  2. 📉 Heavy AI use is linked to weaker critical thinking. A 2025 study of 666 people by Michael Gerlich found a significant negative link between frequent AI-tool use and critical-thinking scores, driven by "cognitive offloading" (letting the tool do the mental work), strongest in younger users. Source: Societies journal, Gerlich 2025.
  3. 🧠 Your brain literally engages less when the AI writes for you. An MIT Media Lab study ("Your Brain on ChatGPT") used EEG on people writing essays and found the AI-assisted group had the weakest brain connectivity and remembered less of what they "wrote." Source: brainonllm.com.
The takeaway is not "stop using AI." It is that the DEFAULT way most people prompt ("just give me the answer") is the one that dulls you. The prompts below flip that on purpose.
the one habit

The one habit behind all of it

Every prompt here does the same thing in a different shape: it keeps YOU in the reasoning loop instead of letting Claude replace it.

  1. 🔄 Make Claude a sparring partner, not a vending machine. A vending machine hands you a finished answer. A sparring partner makes you defend your thinking, spots your blind side, and hands the decision back to you.
  2. ✍️ Always leave a fingerprint. When Claude drafts something for you, rewrite at least one part in your own words before you use it. That single edit keeps your voice and judgment in the work.
  3. 🧭 Prompt for the reasoning, not the conclusion. Ask for trade-offs, questions, and counter-arguments first. The conclusion is more trustworthy when you watched it get built.
copy these

The prompt set (copy, paste, keep your brain )

This is the "think with Claude, not for you" set. Swap in your real situation, the more honest detail you give, the sharper it gets.

1. Argue the opposite of what I am leaning toward. Use this before any real decision, instead of "what should I do."

1 · argue the opposite 
I'm leaning toward [your current choice]. Before I commit, lay out the honest trade-offs of both options in a simple table, then argue as hard as you can for the OPPOSITE of what I'm leaning toward. Assume I'm about to make a mistake and you're the one friend willing to tell me. End by asking me the one question I'm avoiding.

2. Ask me five questions first. Use this when you want to keep the reasoning yours instead of outsourcing it.

2 · ask me five questions 
I want to work out [the decision or problem] myself, not have you decide for me. Before you give any opinion, ask me the 5 sharpest questions that would change the answer. Ask them one at a time, wait for my reply, and only after all 5 give me your read, clearly labelled as your opinion so I can push back.

3. Make me defend it (the sparring drill). Use this when you already have a plan and want to stress-test it.

3 · the sparring drill 
Here's my plan: [paste it]. Don't improve it for me. Instead, poke holes in it: what's the weakest assumption, what am I not seeing, and what would have to be true for this to fail? Then make me defend it by asking me to respond to your three strongest objections.

4. Draft it, but hand me back the pen. Use this for emails, captions, plans, anything Claude writes for you.

4 · hand me back the pen 
Draft [the email / caption / plan] for [context]. Then, underneath, point to the ONE part that most needs my real voice or my personal judgment, and leave it blank with a note on what only I can fill in. I'll rewrite that part myself before I use this.

5. Teach me the reasoning, don't just answer. Use this when you want to actually learn, not just ship.

5 · teach me the reasoning 
I could just take your answer to [the question], but I want to understand it well enough to decide without you next time. Walk me through how you'd reason about it step by step, flag where smart people disagree, and at the end give me a short checklist I can reuse on my own for problems like this.
go further

3 bonus prompts to run next

Same goal, different angles, for when you want to go deeper.

  1. 🪞 The blind-spot audit.
    🪞 the blind-spot audit 
    Based on everything I've told you in this chat, what's the blind spot in how I'm thinking about this that I clearly can't see myself? Be specific and a little uncomfortable.
  2. 🧪 The steelman-then-break.
    🧪 steelman then break 
    Steelman my position as strongly as you can, then immediately break your own steelman. I want to see the best version of my thinking and its real weak point back to back.
  3. 🗓️ The weekly sharpener.
    🗓️ the weekly sharpener 
    Every Friday I'll paste the biggest decision I made this week. Play it back to me: was my reasoning sound, what did I skip, and what's one thinking habit I should practice next week?
the craft

How to get the most out of it

  1. 🌱 If you're newer to AI. start with prompt 2 ("ask me five questions first"). It is the gentlest way to feel the difference between AI deciding FOR you and AI helping you decide. You will notice you actually understand your own choice at the end.
  2. 🚀 If you're a power user. chain them: run prompt 1 to pressure-test, then prompt 3 to defend, then prompt 4 to draft the output with your fingerprint on it. You get AI speed without the AI-brain-fade.
what to watch

An honest heads-up

This will feel slower at first, and that is the point. Handing over the whole task is faster today and more expensive later, when you realise you cannot make the call without the tool. Use these on the decisions and writing that are actually yours to own. For genuine busywork (formatting, boilerplate, a quick lookup), let Claude just do it, no guilt. The goal is keeping the muscle where it matters, not making every tiny task a workout.
