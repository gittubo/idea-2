6 Claude prompts I run every week

the idea

Six jobs, same six prompts, every week

Most people use Claude like a search bar: fresh chat, fresh explanation, fresh disappointment. A weekly stack works differently. You pick the six recurring jobs that eat your week, write one strong prompt for each, and then you only ever paste your raw material in. Same input shape, same output shape, no thinking required from you at the start of the task, which is the part that usually makes you avoid it.

The six below are the ones I actually run: a weekly reset, inbox triage, repurposing, a decision memo, meeting notes into actions, and a read on my numbers. Steal the set, then swap any one of them for whatever your week keeps throwing at you.

do this first

The 15 minute setup

This is the part that turns six prompts into a system. It takes one sitting and you never do it again.

  1. 👩‍💻 Open Claude and make a Project. Go to claude.ai or grab the desktop app, then click Projects in the left sidebar and create one called "My week". Projects keep files and instructions attached to every chat inside them, so Claude starts each conversation already knowing your context (what projects are).
  2. 📄 Feed it who you are. In the project's files or knowledge section, upload three things: a short note on your role and your current big goal, two or three pieces of writing that sound like you (emails, posts, anything), and your team or client names with what each one wants. This is what makes the drafts sound like a person instead of a template.
  3. 📌 Paste the six prompts somewhere you can reach. A pinned note, a Notion page, the project instructions, whatever you will actually open. Keep the placeholders in square brackets so you can see at a glance what each one needs from you.
  4. 🔁 Run them in one sitting the first week. Block 60 minutes, run all six on real material, and tell Claude what was wrong with each output. Fold those corrections back into the prompt text. After two weeks the prompts are yours, not mine.
  5. 🧠 Promote the winners into skills. Once a prompt has stopped changing, ask Claude to turn it into a skill so it triggers automatically. In the app: Customize, then Skills, then Add, then Upload a skill. It works on every plan including Free (creating custom skills).
You do not need a paid plan to start. Free accounts can run all six, you will just hit usage limits sooner on the long ones like inbox triage and repurposing. Run the heavy ones first while you have headroom.
prompt one

The weekly reset

This is the Sunday one. You paste in your messy notes from the week and next week's calendar, and you get back a clean picture of what actually happened plus a real top three. The thing that makes it work is the fourth section, the list of things to deliberately not do, because that is the decision most weekly reviews skip.

🌱 the weekly reset 
You are my chief of staff and we are doing my weekly reset. Below are my raw notes from this week and my calendar for the week ahead, exactly as they came out, messy and out of order.

Give me four things. First, my wins: what actually got finished, in plain language, no padding and no credit for things that only moved slightly. Second, my open loops: anything I started, promised someone, or am waiting on, with the name of whoever is blocking each one. Third, my top three for next week, chosen by what moves my main goal forward rather than what is loudest, and tell me in one line why each one made the cut. Fourth, what I should deliberately NOT do next week, and what actually happens if I drop it.

If I have not told you my main goal for this quarter, ask me for it before you start. Keep the whole thing under 400 words and write it to me, not about me.

prompt two

Inbox triage

Paste your unread threads in and get them sorted by what they actually cost you, with a full draft reply for every one that needs it. I do this Monday morning and again Thursday. The rule that matters is the last one: when a thread needs a real decision from you, Claude must say so instead of inventing a position on your behalf.

📥 inbox triage 
You are triaging my inbox. Below are my unread email threads pasted in full, including senders and the whole back and forth where I have it.

Sort every thread into four buckets: reply today, reply this week, delegate or forward, and archive with no reply needed. For each thread in the first three buckets give me one line on what the sender actually wants (not what they said), then write a complete draft reply in my voice: warm, direct, short sentences, no corporate filler, no em dashes, no "I hope this finds you well".

Two rules. If a thread needs a decision only I can make, do not guess: say so, lay out the two or three options with the trade-off of each, and give me your recommendation instead of a draft. And put the highest-stakes thread at the top so I start there rather than with the easy ones.

Even once you connect your email through a connector (about connectors), keep it at drafts and press send yourself, so a wrong tone or a wrong recipient gets caught by you rather than by the person receiving it.
prompt three

The repurposer

One long thing becomes a week of posts, rewritten for each place rather than pasted across all of them. The two details doing the work here are the voice samples and the instruction that each piece must lead with a different idea, which is what stops you saying the same sentence five times in five formats.

♻️ repurpose one post into a week 
Turn the long piece below into a week of content, properly rewritten per platform rather than copy-pasted across them.

Give me: one LinkedIn post (a hook line, short paragraphs, a real opinion, no hashtag spam), three Instagram captions with a different angle each, three short-form video scripts of 30 to 45 seconds where the first spoken line IS the hook, one X thread of five to seven posts, and one short email to my list. Every piece has to stand on its own for someone who never saw the original, and each one must lead with a DIFFERENT idea from the source so I am not repeating myself all week.

Match my voice using the samples below: copy my sentence length, my level of casualness and the words I actually use. At the end, tell me which single idea from the source has the most reach potential and why you think so.

prompt four

The decision memo

For the choice you have been circling for two weeks. Most AI answers on decisions are mush because nothing forces a conclusion, so this prompt makes Claude argue both sides at full strength, then commit, score its own confidence, and name the one fact that would change its mind.

🧭 the decision memo 
Write me a decision memo on the choice below. I want both sides argued at full strength and then an actual answer, not a balanced-sounding list that avoids committing.

Structure it like this. One, restate the decision in a single sentence so I know you understood it. Two, the strongest case FOR, including the assumptions it quietly depends on. Three, the strongest case AGAINST, argued just as hard, and include the failure mode I am most likely to be blind to given what I have told you. Four, commit: pick one option, give me a confidence score out of ten, and name the single piece of information that would flip your answer. Five, the smallest reversible step I can take this week to test it before betting properly.

Where you are missing something important, ask me for it rather than filling the gap with an assumption.

--- THE DECISION ---
[what you are choosing between, the money and time at stake, your deadline, what you have already tried, who else it affects]
prompt five

Meeting to actions

Paste a transcript, get owners, deadlines and the follow-up email. The UNASSIGNED rule is the important bit. A transcript summariser that guesses who owns what quietly creates work nobody agreed to, and you find out three weeks later.

✅ turn a meeting into actions 
Here is a raw meeting transcript. Turn it into something the room can act on.

Give me a three-line summary of what was actually DECIDED, then a table of action items with the owner, the deadline and the exact wording of what that person committed to. If a name was never said out loud for a task, mark the owner as UNASSIGNED rather than guessing, and list those separately at the end so I can chase them.

Then two more things: the open questions nobody answered, and any decision that got made without the person it affects being in the room. Finish by drafting the follow-up email I will send to everyone. Short, friendly, decisions first, actions in a list, and a closing line asking people to correct anything I got wrong.

prompt six

Explain my numbers

The one I run on a Friday. It refuses to just read your metrics back to you, it has to point at the rows that prove the trend, separate a real change from a data problem, and give you one thing to change. Attach the CSV rather than pasting it if the file is big, Claude can read it directly.

📈 explain my numbers 
You are my analyst. Attached (or pasted below) are my numbers for the last [week / month]. Context: [what the business or account is, and what I am trying to grow].

Do not summarise the numbers back to me. Instead: name the ONE trend that matters most and quote the specific rows or dates that prove it. Then tell me the most likely cause, clearly labelled as a hypothesis rather than a fact. Then give me the single change I should make next, and the exact number I should watch to know whether it worked.

Also flag anything that looks like a data problem rather than real behaviour, like a missing week, a duplicated row, or a metric that jumped in a way humans do not actually behave. Keep the whole thing under 300 words and write it like you are explaining it to me over coffee.

the rhythm

When I run each one

Spreading them out is what keeps it sustainable. Nothing here takes more than about ten minutes once the prompt is saved.

Sunday evening Weekly reset. Notes plus next week's calendar in, top three out. Sets the whole week.
Monday morning Inbox triage. Clears the pile before it turns into a background hum of guilt.
Tuesday Repurpose. One long thing you already made becomes the week of posts.
Whenever it comes up Decision memo. The moment you notice you have thought about something three times without moving.
Straight after any meeting Meeting to actions. Do it while the transcript is open or it never happens.
Friday Explain my numbers. Ends the week with one change to make, not a dashboard to stare at.
make them permanent

Turn the ones you love into skills

A pasted prompt is fine. A skill is better, because Claude reaches for it on its own the moment your request matches, so you stop even opening the note. Ask Claude to build it for you, no code involved.

🛠️ turn this prompt into a skill 
Turn the prompt below into a reusable Claude skill so I never have to paste it again.

Write me the SKILL.md. Give it a short lowercase-hyphenated name (do not put "claude" or "anthropic" in the name or the upload gets rejected), and a description line that says both what it does AND exactly when to use it, because that line is what makes you trigger it automatically. Then write the instructions as clear numbered steps, and include the rules about tone and format that are in my prompt.

Before you write it, ask me the three or four things you need to know about my format, my inputs and the mistakes you should avoid. When you are done, package it as a zipped folder I can upload.

--- MY PROMPT ---
[paste one of the six]
  1. 📦 Take the zip Claude hands you. Inside is a folder with a SKILL.md. That folder is the skill.
  2. ➕ Install it. In the Claude app, open Customize, then Skills, then Add, then Upload a skill, and pick the zip. In Claude Code, drop the folder into ~/.claude/skills/.
  3. 🧪 Test it on a real task. Ask for the thing in your own words and check that the skill fires. If it does not, the description line was too vague. Make it name the trigger words you actually use.
Skills are available on every plan from Free through Enterprise, so there is no upgrade gate here (what are skills). Only install skills from sources you trust, since a skill carries real instructions.
good to know

Two honest notes

Claude does not know what happened this week unless you tell it. Memory and chat search help it carry context between conversations (chat search and memory), but the raw material still has to go in. That is why every one of these prompts ends with a paste block.
Ten hours is my number, on my week, with my six jobs. Yours will be different. The honest way to check is to time the same task once by hand and once with the prompt in week one, then keep the ones where the gap was real and drop the ones where it was not.
