The AI user spectrum, and how to actually level up

Hey, it's Cindy 🌱 This guide maps the whole ladder from dinosaur to builder, tells you honestly which rung you're probably on.

💡 The whole idea. Nobody skips rungs. You don't go from "I ask ChatGPT stuff" to "I built an automation" in one leap. Each rung is a single new habit stacked on the last one. Find your rung, do the one move, come back for the next.
the map

The 5 rungs of the AI ladder

  1. 🦖 Rung 1, the dinosaur (~20%) Doesn't touch AI, or tried it once, got a mediocre answer, and decided it was overhyped. Not a dumb position, just an uninstalled one. Most people are here because nobody ever showed them a reason that mattered to their actual life or job. Your next move: run one real task through it this week, not a demo.
  2. 💬 Rung 2, the chatbot-only user (~40%) Opens Claude or ChatGPT and types a question like it's a smarter Google, reads the answer, leaves. This is where the huge majority live. It works, and it also means you're driving a car in first gear. Your next move: stop asking from memory, start bringing your real context in.
  3. ⚡ Rung 3, the average daily user (~25%) Uses AI most days for real work: drafting emails, summarizing docs, fixing code, thinking out loud. Has two or three prompts they reuse. This is already ahead of almost everyone you know. The ceiling: you still start every single chat from a blank slate. Your next move: stop repeating yourself, make Claude remember who you are.
  4. 🧠 Rung 4, the power user (~12%) Runs everything through Projects and custom instructions, so Claude already knows their voice, their rules, and their formats. Has built or installed Skills, so one line triggers a whole workflow. What used to take ten messages now takes one. Your next move: give Claude hands, connect it to your real tools.
  5. 🔧 Rung 5, the builder (~3%) Connects Claude to live apps with MCP and wires small automations that run whether they're at the keyboard or not. AI stops being a chat window and becomes a system that reads, decides, and acts. This is where the leverage and the income live. Your next move: ship one tiny build, then sell it or scale it.
🎯 The percentages are illustrative, not a real survey. I made them up to show the shape of the thing: most people are bunched at rungs 1 and 2, and the air gets thin fast near the top. That thin air at the top is exactly why the skills are worth money.
rungs 1 to 3

Get genuinely fluent, fast

The first three rungs are all about one shift: treating Claude like a colleague you brief, not a search box you query. Claude has a free tier to start, and Pro is around US$20 a month if you want the heavier features later. You don't need Pro for any of this.

🎯 Pick one real task Not "tell me about AI." A thing you actually owe someone this week: a client email, a messy spreadsheet, a first draft. Real stakes teach you faster than any tutorial.
📎 Bring your context Paste the actual email thread, the real brief, the transcript, the numbers. Generic input is why people get generic, useless answers. The context you give is 90% of the quality you get back.
🗣️ Brief it, don't command it Give the goal, the audience, and what "good" looks like, then react to the draft. "Make it warmer and cut the second paragraph" gets you further than starting over.
🔁 Keep what works When a prompt nails it, save it somewhere. That saved prompt is the raw material for a Skill later. Every power user's system started as a note full of prompts that worked.
rung 4

From user to power user

The jump to rung 4 is when you stop re-explaining yourself. Two features do almost all the work here, and both come with Claude directly.

  1. 📁 Set up a Project A Project is a workspace that holds standing context: who you are, your voice, your rules, your reference docs. Every chat inside it starts already knowing all of that. Give it your bio, a few examples of your writing, and your non-negotiables once, and never paste them again.
  2. ⚙️ Write custom instructions once The permanent "here's how I want you to talk to me" layer. Tone, format, length, things to never do. This is the difference between correcting the same thing daily and having it right by default.
  3. 🧩 Turn your best repeated task into a Skill Claude Skills are packaged instructions Claude loads automatically when a task calls for them. Anthropic ships some (Excel, PowerPoint, PDFs), and you can build your own from a workflow you do over and over. It's how "format this the way I always do" becomes a single trigger instead of a paragraph.
paste into Claude to build your first Skill 
I do this exact task over and over and I want to package it as a reusable
Claude Skill so I never re-explain it.

The task: [describe it, e.g. "turn my rough voice-note ideas into a
structured client update email in my voice"].

Here are 2 real examples of me doing it well:
[paste example 1]
[paste example 2]

Please:
1. Reverse-engineer the exact steps and rules I'm following (including my
   tone, structure, and what I always avoid).
2. Write it up as a clear SKILL.md: a name, a one-line description of when
   it should trigger, and step-by-step instructions Claude can follow.
3. Flag anything you had to guess so I can correct it.
💡 You don't need to code to do this. A Skill is just clearly written instructions in a text file. You're not programming, you're documenting how you already think, and letting Claude reuse it.
rung 5

From power user to builder

Rung 5 is the one everyone thinks is out of reach, and it's closer than it looks. The unlock is MCP (Model Context Protocol), the open standard Anthropic created that lets Claude plug into real apps like Gmail, Calendar, Notion, and Slack. Connectors are the ready-made plugs. Once Claude can read and act inside your actual tools, it stops talking and starts doing.

  1. 🔌 Connect one tool, not ten Pick the single app where your most annoying repeated job lives, and connect it. Gmail if your pain is your inbox, Calendar if it's scheduling, Notion if it's admin. One connector is a real build. Ten is a mess you'll abandon.
  2. 🎯 Pick one painful loop The read-decide-act job you do every day without thinking: triage new emails, draft replies to the same five questions, turn meeting notes into tasks. That loop is your first automation.
  3. 🧑‍⚖️ Keep a human approval step Have Claude read, decide, and draft, but let you approve before anything sends or posts. This is how real builders ship: automate the boring 90%, keep judgment on the last 10%.
  4. 🚀 Ship it small, then grow it Your first build should be almost embarrassingly simple and actually run. A working tiny thing beats a grand plan every time. Once it runs for you, the exact same build is something you can run for a business and get paid for.
paste into Claude to plan your first build 
I want to build my first small AI automation and I'm a beginner. Help me
scope something I can actually finish this week, not a fantasy.

My most repetitive, most dreaded task is: [describe the loop, e.g. "sorting
new emails and drafting replies to the same handful of questions"].

The tool that task lives in is: [e.g. Gmail].

Please:
1. Design the simplest possible version of this as a read, decide, act loop,
   with a human approval step before anything is sent.
2. Tell me exactly which connector or MCP integration I'd need and how to
   set it up, in plain steps.
3. List what could go wrong and how to keep it safe.
4. Give me a 30-minute first version I can test on real data today.
the honest bit

Keep it real

🚫 You can't skip rungs, and pretending you did shows. If you're on rung 2, trying to build an automation before you're fluent just means a broken thing you don't understand. Do the boring rung in front of you. It's faster than the shortcut.
💡 Every rung is one habit, not a personality. "Technical person" isn't a type you're born as. It's someone who did rung 4 last month and rung 5 this month. The people at the top just started climbing earlier. You can start today.
