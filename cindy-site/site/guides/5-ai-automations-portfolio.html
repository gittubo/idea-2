5 AI automations to build for your portfolio this week

Hey, it's Cindy 🌱 Five automations, one per evening, each starting from a real free template.

💡 Set up once, before Monday. Install n8n (the self-hosted Community Edition is genuinely free) and connect one AI model key. That's the only setup cost, and it covers all five builds. Every template below imports in one click after that.
evening one

A proposal generator

Intermediate, about 4 evenings if you polish it, 1 to get it working. A client fills in a form, and AI expands their answers into a full proposal in a Google Slides deck, then drafts the follow-up email. This is the most theatrical demo on the list: you submit a form on camera and a branded deck appears.

Stack: n8n + Tally (free forms) + Google Slides + Drive + Gmail. Template: Client proposal generator

Make it yours: change the form questions to your industry's real intake questions, and rewrite the deck template with your own sections. The generic version looks like a template; the one built around a real service reads like a product.

evening two

An invoice chaser

Intermediate, about 3 evenings. Runs daily, finds overdue invoices in a sheet or database, and sends escalating AI-written reminders: gentle at day three, firm at day fourteen. Payment confirmations update the status automatically.

Stack: n8n + Google Sheets (or Postgres) + Gmail, optional Slack alert. Template: AI invoice reminder and payment tracker

Why it demos well: put the day-3 and day-14 emails side by side. The tone escalation is what shows judgment, and judgment is what people are hiring for. Anyone can connect two apps.

evening three

A review responder

Beginner friendly, about 3 evenings. Checks a business's Google reviews every morning, classifies the sentiment, drafts a personalised reply to each one, and emails you the drafts for one-click posting. Angry reviews and delighted reviews get genuinely different treatment.

Stack: n8n + Google Places API + Gmail. Template: Monitor Google reviews and draft replies

Cost note: the Places API sits inside Google Maps Platform's monthly free credit at this volume, so a demo build costs nothing.

evening four

A weekly analytics digest

Beginner friendly, about 3 evenings. Every Monday it pulls last week's traffic numbers, has AI write the "so what" in plain English, and emails a formatted report. It automates the exact Monday-morning task every marketing team quietly hates.

Stack: n8n + Google Analytics (GA4) + Gmail. Template: Weekly analytics report with AI analysis

Make it yours: the AI commentary is what separates this from a dumb data dump. Tell the model what decisions the reader makes each week, and have it answer those, not just describe the numbers.

evening five

A no-show rescuer

Beginner friendly, about 2 evenings. Detects missed appointments on a schedule and automatically messages the no-show a rebooking nudge, while alerting whoever owns that client. It recovers revenue that most businesses simply write off, which reads as commercial thinking rather than tutorial-following.

Stack: n8n + Calendly + Telegram or email. Template: Recover no-show appointments

Nice detail: it polls on a schedule instead of using webhooks, so it works on Calendly's free plan. Worth mentioning when you show it: constraints handled well are a signal.

the honest bits

What this actually costs

n8n Free forever if you self-host the Community Edition. Cloud starts around €20/month, which you don't need for portfolio builds.
The AI model Cents per run. The templates ship with OpenAI nodes; swap in Claude (or any provider) by changing the model node, which is a two-minute edit.
Everything else Tally, Sheets, Gmail, GA4, and Calendly all work on free tiers for these builds.
Your time Two to four hours per automation, honestly. The first one takes longest because you're learning n8n, not the automation.
📌 Record every build. A 60-second screen recording of the thing working is worth more than the workflow file. Five clips by Friday is a portfolio; five JSON exports is a folder.
🌱 Honest expectation. Templates get you working software fast, and that's the point of a portfolio. What makes it yours is the customisation: your industry's language, your edge cases, your prompt. Anyone can import a template, so show what you changed and why.
