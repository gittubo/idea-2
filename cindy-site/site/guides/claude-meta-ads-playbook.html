1. The Audit Prompt

What it does. Takes your Meta ads data and returns a structured audit. What to kill, what to scale, what to iterate. What's working across the account and what's missing entirely. Three concrete actions for the next 7 days.

How to use it. Paste the prompt below into Claude. After the prompt, paste your campaign data. Claude returns the audit in 30 seconds.

Where to get the data. Meta Ads Manager → select last 30 days → click Export → CSV. Or screenshot the campaign view if there's not much going on.

🔍 the audit prompt 
Act as a senior performance marketer who has personally managed $1M+ in Meta ad spend.

I'll paste my campaign data below. Return a structured audit covering:

1. KILL list. Which ads are draining budget without ROAS justification? Frequency too high? CTR too low? CPM out of line? Be specific about why each one goes.

2. SCALE list. Which ads are showing strong ROAS with room to grow? What's the next 7-day budget recommendation for each (percentage increase, not flat number)?

3. ITERATE list. Which ads have signal but aren't winning yet? For each, name the single biggest variable to test next: hook, creative format, audience, or offer.

4. Pattern read. What hooks, angles, and offers are working across the account? What's not? What's missing entirely from my testing matrix?

5. Next 7 days. Three concrete actions I should take this week, ranked by impact.

Use these benchmarks as context (don't quote them in the output, just use them to judge):
- Strong ROAS: 3x+ for ecom, 5x+ for low-ticket digital, breakeven OK for high-ticket if pipeline is healthy
- Frequency: kill at 3.5+ unless retargeting
- CTR floor: 1.0% on cold, 2.0% on warm
- Strong CPC: under $1.50 cold, under $0.80 warm

If data is missing for any of these, flag it and tell me what I need to pull next.

Here's my data:

[paste your Ads Manager export below]
What to look for in the output. The KILL list is the most actionable. Most accounts have 30-50% of spend going to ads that should already be off. Cutting that first is often the single biggest ROAS lift you can do in a week.

2. The Creative Generator

What it does. Takes one ad that's working and generates 30 variants across 5 strategic categories: hook angle, format, length, CTA, and audience. You get a structured table you can paste into your testing spreadsheet.

How to use it. Paste the prompt into Claude, then paste your winning ad copy after it. Claude returns 30 variants organized by category.

⚡ the 30-variant generator 
Act as a senior performance marketer with $1M+ in Meta ad spend experience.

Below is my winning ad. Generate 30 variant ad copies I can test, organized into 5 categories of 6 variants each:

1. Hook angle variants. Six different hook angles: curiosity, fear of missing out, contrarian take, social proof, specificity, direct benefit.

2. Format variants. Six different formats: story, listicle, question, testimonial, before-after, data-led.

3. Length variants. Six different lengths: one-liner, two-line, short paragraph, mini-story, long-form, multi-line scannable.

4. CTA variants. Six different CTAs: soft, hard, question, urgency, social-proof close, no-CTA.

5. Audience variants. Six rewrites for different audience temperatures: skeptics, beginners, advanced operators, mid-funnel, cold, retargeted.

For every variant:
- Keep the brand voice from the original (study it before drafting)
- Match the same offer and value prop
- Don't invent claims I haven't made
- Don't use any of these cliches: "in today's world," "let's dive in," "the truth is," "unlock," "game-changer," "revolutionary," excessive exclamation points

Output as a markdown table with columns: Variant #, Category, Sub-angle, Copy.

Here's my winning ad:

[paste your winning ad copy below]
How to use the output. Don't test all 30 at once. You'll run out of statistical power and burn budget on noise. Pick 5 to 7 variants from across categories (one per category is a good starting structure). Test in pairs against your control. Kill losers in 3 to 5 days based on early signal, double down on winners with a budget bump.

3. The Viral Hook Swipe File

10 hook structures I run on live campaigns. For each one: the structure, a worked example, and when to use it.

1. The Specificity hook

  • Structure: "I [specific result] using [specific tactic]"
  • Example: "I scaled a $5k/mo client to $50k/mo using one ad and three audiences"
  • When to use: Case studies, performance proof, building authority. Specificity beats generality every time. Replace "a lot" with the actual number.

2. The Contrarian hook

  • Structure: "Stop [common advice]. Do this instead."
  • Example: "Stop A/B testing your creative. Do this instead."
  • When to use: Reaching experienced operators, breaking through ad fatigue. Works because it implies you know something the competition doesn't.

3. The Pattern Interrupt

  • Structure: "If you're [specific situation] and aren't [specific tactic], you're leaving money on the table."
  • Example: "If you're spending $5k+/mo on Meta ads and aren't using AI to generate variants weekly, you're leaving money on the table."
  • When to use: Pre-qualifying high-spend buyers. The qualifier filters out tire-kickers and stops the right people scrolling.

4. The Question hook

  • Structure: "Why does [counterintuitive thing] keep happening?"
  • Example: "Why do my best ads always die after 2 weeks?"
  • When to use: Nurturing problem-aware audiences. Opens a curiosity gap that demands the next line.

5. The Cost-of-Inaction hook

  • Structure: "Your competitors are [doing X]. You're [doing less]."
  • Example: "Your competitors are running 30 ad variants this week. You're running 3."
  • When to use: Competitive niches, urgency creation. Works because comparison is more visceral than abstract benefit.

6. The Bold Promise hook

  • Structure: "Every [thing] should follow this [number]-step formula."
  • Example: "Every Meta ad you write should follow this 3-line formula."
  • When to use: Educational content, lead magnets, building expert authority. Confident outcome statements work when you can back them up.

7. The Behind-the-Scenes hook

  • Structure: "Here's the exact [thing] I [verb] this week."
  • Example: "Here's the exact $4,200 ad test I ran this week and what it told me."
  • When to use: Building trust and authority. Transparency creates connection. Real numbers beat vague claims.

8. The Mistake hook

  • Structure: "I lost [specific amount] learning [lesson] so you don't have to."
  • Example: "I burned $8k learning this Meta targeting lesson so you don't have to."
  • When to use: Educational content. Pain plus benevolence is one of the most powerful framings in advertising.

9. The Stack hook

  • Structure: "[Number] things every [winner] has + [number] things every [loser] has."
  • Example: "3 things every winning Meta ad has + 2 things every loser has."
  • When to use: Carousel, reel, or thread formats. The paired structure is scannable and creates a built-in hook for the second half of the content.

10. The Industry Insider hook

  • Structure: "What every [authority figure] knows that [non-experts] don't."
  • Example: "What every $10k+/mo Meta advertiser knows that beginners don't."
  • When to use: Positioning content, premium offer pre-sell. Implies exclusive knowledge and signals you're inside the room.
