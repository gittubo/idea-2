What you're getting

5 ChatGPT prompts I use almost every single day to get way better answers out of it. Most people type a question and copy the first thing ChatGPT spits back. These 5 prompts unlock the other 90% (the part where it actually gets useful).

Each one is a copy-paste prompt. Use them solo, or stack them together for compounding results.

the method

How to use them

  1. Copy the prompt under each cheat code.
  2. Paste it at the top of your ChatGPT message, then add your actual question underneath.
  3. Tell ChatGPT it's in "[name] mode" so it keeps the rules for the rest of the chat.

(A "prompt" is just the message you type into ChatGPT. Think of it as a recipe: the better the recipe, the better the dish.)

for big calls

CODE 1: PREDICT

What it does: Turns ChatGPT into a personal forecaster. It uses everything it already knows about you (from past chats and saved memory) to predict the most likely outcome of a decision you're about to make.

Best for: Career moves, business decisions, "should I do X or Y," money calls. Works way better after you've talked to ChatGPT about your life and goals for a while.

The prompt:

🔮 Paste this, then add your decision 
Predict mode: Based on everything you know about me from our chat history and saved memory, predict the most likely outcome if I [insert your decision or situation here]. Use my actual patterns, my stated goals, and my known blind spots. Tell me:
1. What I'm most likely to do
2. What's most likely to happen as a result
3. The one thing I'd probably miss
4. The one change I should make before I commit

Don't soften it. I want the prediction, not the pep talk.

Example:

Pro tip: Turn on ChatGPT memory in Settings → Personalization → Memory. The more it knows about you, the sharper the prediction.
for honest feedback

CODE 2: KILL CRITIC

What it does: Stops ChatGPT from nodding along to everything you say. Forces it to push back, point out flaws, and tell you the truth instead of validating you into a bad decision.

Best for: Pressure-testing ideas, business plans, copy you've written, decisions you're emotionally attached to.

The prompt:

🪞 Switch off the yes-man for the whole chat 
Kill critic mode: Drop the sycophancy for the rest of this conversation. Treat me like a smart peer who needs honest feedback, not a fragile customer who needs validation. If my idea is weak, say so and tell me why. If I'm wrong, correct me. No "great question," no "you're so right," no hedging. Push back when you'd normally just agree. Tell me the truth.

Example:

What it looks like filled in 
Kill critic mode: Here's my Instagram bio and pinned reel idea. Rip them apart. [paste]
Pro tip: Run your idea through Kill Critic BEFORE you launch, post, or commit. It's like having a brutally honest advisor on call.
for learning fast

CODE 3: EXPLAIN LIKE I'M 10

What it does: Forces ChatGPT to strip out jargon, buzzwords, and corporate speak. Breaks down any topic so a 10-year-old could understand it.

Best for: Learning anything new (AI, finance, tech, marketing, legal stuff). Especially when you've Googled a term and the explanation just has more confusing terms inside it.

The prompt:

🧒 Drop any topic into the bracket 
Explain like I'm 10: Break down [insert topic] like I'm 10 years old. No jargon. No buzzwords. If you have to use a technical word, define it first using something a kid would understand. Use simple analogies from everyday life (school, food, sports, games). Short sentences. Make me actually understand it, not just nod along.

Example:

What it looks like filled in 
Explain like I'm 10: What's an LLM and how does ChatGPT actually work under the hood?
Pro tip: Once you "get" it at the 10-year-old level, ask a follow-up: "Now explain it like I'm 25 and want the next layer of depth." You'll learn faster than reading 5 articles.
for hard problems

CODE 4: 10X THINK

What it does: Forces ChatGPT to slow down and reason properly before answering. Most prompts get a fast surface-level answer. This makes it think the way Claude does in extended-thinking mode (deeper, more angles, less reactive).

Best for: Strategy questions, complex problems, anything where the first answer would be too obvious or too generic.

The prompt:

🐌 Make it slow down before it answers 
10x think mode: Before you answer, think 10x harder than you normally would. Slow down. Break the problem into smaller parts. Consider what's NOT obvious. Question your first instinct. Look at it from at least 3 angles. Then give me your final answer + a short note on what you almost got wrong on the first pass.

Example:

What it looks like filled in 
10x think mode: I run an AI marketing agency capped at 5 clients. What's the single highest-leverage offer I can build inside that cap, and why?
Pro tip: Pair 10x Think with Kill Critic for nuclear-level outputs. ChatGPT thinks deep AND tells you the truth.
for anything you publish

CODE 5: HUMAN

What it does: Strips the "AI tells" out of the output. No em dashes, no "delve," no "robust," no three-part lists when two work. Makes the writing sound like a real person.

Best for: Captions, emails, sales copy, anything that's going to be read by humans (so, basically everything).

The prompt:

🧑 Paste your draft at the bottom 
Human mode: Rewrite the output so it sounds like a real person wrote it. Specifically:
- No em dashes. Use commas, periods, or parentheses instead
- No "delve," "leverage," "robust," "in conclusion," "moreover," "elevate," "navigate the landscape"
- No three-part lists when two work
- Use contractions (I'm, you're, don't)
- Vary sentence length. Some short. Some longer with rhythm
- Cut filler intros like "Certainly!" or "Great question!"
- If a sentence sounds like AI wrote it, fix it
Now rewrite [paste your text here].

Example:

What it looks like filled in 
Human mode: Rewrite this Instagram caption so it sounds like @cindiezhu, casual + lowercase + 2 emojis max. [paste draft]
Pro tip: Save your own voice rules in ChatGPT memory ("I write in lowercase, no em dashes, 2 emojis max"). Then Human mode + your saved voice = caption written in your style every time.
go further

Bonus: stacking the cheat codes

The real unlock is combining these.

Predict + Kill Critic = sharpest decision-making tool you have. Predicts the outcome, then tears it apart.
Explain Like I'm 10 + 10x Think = best way to learn anything new. Deep understanding, zero overwhelm.
10x Think + Human = the perfect copywriting setup. Thinks hard, then writes like a person.
All 5 at once = nuclear mode. Use sparingly (it'll think for a while).

Thinking & decision-making (1–15)

1. Decision matrix: "List the top 3 options for [decision]. Score each one 1-10 across cost, time, risk, upside, and gut-feel. Show your reasoning. Recommend one."

2. Steel-man both sides: "Argue both sides of [decision] as if you genuinely believed each one. Make the strongest case for both. Then tell me which side wins on the merits."

3. 10-year regret check: "If I [insert decision], what's the most likely thing I'll regret in 10 years? What's the most likely thing I'll be grateful for? Be specific."

4. Reverse the question: "I'm trying to [achieve X]. Instead of telling me how to do it, tell me how to fail at it spectacularly. Then we'll work backwards."

5. Second-order effects: "If I [insert decision], what happens? Then what happens after that? Walk me through 3 layers of consequences."

6. Pre-mortem: "Pretend I made [decision] 1 year ago and it failed badly. Write the autopsy. What went wrong? Where did I miss the signs?"

7. Highest-leverage move: "Here's my current situation: [paste]. What's the single highest-leverage action I can take this week? Not a list. One thing."

8. Opportunity cost check: "If I say yes to [thing], what am I implicitly saying no to? List the trade-offs I'd be making."

9. 80/20 audit: "Here's my full to-do list: [paste]. Which 20% of these tasks will produce 80% of the results? Cut the rest."

10. Devil's advocate: "I believe [insert belief]. Argue the opposite. Show me the strongest case against my position."

11. Blind spot finder: "Based on what you know about me, what's the most important thing I'm probably ignoring right now? Be honest."

12. Risk-reward sizing: "If I do [X], what's the worst realistic outcome? What's the best realistic outcome? Which one is more likely?"

13. Should-I-quit test: "I'm thinking about quitting [project/job/situation]. Ask me 5 questions before giving an answer. Then tell me what your gut says."

14. Time vs. money trade: "I have $[X] and Y hours. What's the best ROI I can get on each? Show me 3 options for both."

15. The kill switch: "What's the one thing that would make me kill this idea/project? Write me a checklist. If any of these happen, I walk away."
