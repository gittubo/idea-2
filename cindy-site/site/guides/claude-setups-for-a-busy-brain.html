the basics

First, how to make a setup "stick"

A "setup" here just means a short set of instructions you give Claude once, then reuse. Two reliable ways to keep one around:

Keep it in one chat. Start a chat, paste the setup, and come back to that same chat. Everything you said is still in the thread, on every plan including free.
Make it a Project (if you want it tidy). A Claude Project holds one set of instructions plus files you reopen anytime.
Two honest notes: Claude does have memory, but asking it to search your past chats is a paid-plan feature, so the reliable free way is to keep a setup in one chat you reopen. And voice mode (talking to Claude out loud) is free on the app and web, which is lovely for the morning "can't start" one.
can't start

1. The can't-start button

For "I know exactly what to do, I just can't begin." Claude chops the first 90 seconds into steps so small they feel silly to skip.

🟢 the can't-start button 
You are my task-initiation coach. When I tell you what I'm
avoiding, don't give me the whole plan. Give me only the first
4 tiny physical steps, each doable in under 60 seconds, like
"close every tab but this one" and "type one sentence." Then
wait. When I say done, give me the next tiny step.
Tip: run this one in voice mode in the morning. It feels like a friend nudging you, not a to-do list yelling at you.
can't stay

2. The rabbit-hole brake

For the moment you look up and have 40 tabs open. You keep the task in one chat, so when you blank, the answer is right there in the thread.

🛑 the rabbit-hole brake 
For the next 25 minutes I am only working on: [the task].
Be my focus brake. If I message you about anything else,
first ask me "why are you switching, and does it have to be
now?" before you help. If I ask "what was I doing?", remind
me from what I've told you in this chat.
Tip: because it lives in one chat, when you drift back and ask "wait, what was I doing?", Claude just reads the thread and tells you. This is the one that quietly saves whole afternoons.
keep forgetting

3. A note from future you

For when your brain drops something the second you look away. You dump it in messy, Claude turns it into a note future-you can actually act on.

💌 a note from future you 
Turn this brain-dump into a clear note for future me.
Use these fields: What it is, Why it matters, Where to find it,
What to actually say, and end with one kind, calming line.
Here's the dump: [everything on your mind, messy is fine].
Tip: keep the note in a chat you can reopen (or a Project), so tomorrow the whole story is right there, not lost the second you looked away.
overthinking it

4. The "did that text even mean anything" check

For when a 4-word message lives in your head rent-free for 9 hours. Claude shows you what it actually said next to what your brain heard.

🧊 the spiral check 
I got this message and I'm spiralling: "[paste it]".
Show me two columns: what the words literally say, and what
my anxious brain is adding that isn't there. Rate how likely
it's actually bad. Then draft the calm reply I'd send tomorrow
morning, so I don't fire something off at 11pm.
Tip: usually "can we talk?" just means "quick question."
no gas in the tank

5. Pick by your battery

For "I have 4 hours and zero motivation." You tell Claude your energy level, it hands you tasks that fit what you can actually do right now.

🔋 pick by your battery 
Here's my task list: [paste it]. Here's what fills me up vs
drains me: [a line or two]. My battery right now is [3/10?].
Give me exactly 3 tasks for today at this energy level:
one easy 12-minute win to build momentum, one medium lift,
and one real thing. Don't give me the hardest job pretending
to be the right one.
Tip: start with the easy win on purpose. Momentum is the whole trick.
where to start

How to actually use these

You don't need all 5 today. Pick the one that matches the wall you keep hitting: can't start (1), can't stay (2), keep forgetting (3), overthinking a message (4), no gas in the tank (5). Set that one up in a chat, use it for a week, then add the next. These work with your brain, not against it.
