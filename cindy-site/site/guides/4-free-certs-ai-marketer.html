the short version

The whole thing in 4 steps

  1. 🔎 Start with Semrush's AI Search Operating System. Forty minutes, nine lessons, certificate included. It is the newest topic on this list and the shortest, so it is the one that gets you moving.
  2. 📬 Then Klaviyo's Digital Marketing Certificate. Two and a half hours across five parts, ending in a real exam. This is the one that gives you a full-funnel framework to hang everything else on.
  3. 🎨 Then the Google Ads Creative Certification. Just under four hours. Creative is where most ad accounts are actually losing, and it is the easier of the two Google exams.

why bother

What these are actually worth

PwC's 2026 Global AI Jobs Barometer, published 15 June 2026, found that "the average wage premium for workers with AI skills continued to surge higher, hitting 62%, up from 57% last year" (source). The same report found that jobs asking for specific AI skills are growing roughly eight times faster than the overall jobs market.

A certificate is not the skill, and nobody is getting a 62% raise for finishing a 40 minute course. What these four do is make the claim legible. When your manager is deciding who runs the AI piece of next quarter's plan, the difference between you and the next person is usually that one of you can point at something.

read this first

Free to take is not the same as free to certify

This is the trap on nearly every "free certificate" list going around, and it is worth knowing how to spot it. Plenty of platforms give the course away and then charge you at the end for the certificate itself, or hand you a completion report and reserve the actual credential for paying users.

All four here are free at both ends, and here is where each claim comes from, so you are not taking my word for it:

Semrush The course page says "Enroll for free" and "Earn Your Certificate from Semrush Academy", and the academy FAQ answers "Are Semrush Academy courses free?" directly.
Klaviyo The certificate page prints no price, so the claim comes from Klaviyo Academy's own landing page: "free courses, quick guides, live trainings, and certificates". Certificates is named explicitly.
Google, both Neither Skillshop course page prints a price. Google's own Skillshop site calls it "Free Google Ads training for every kind of advertiser".
What you do need A free account with each provider, and for the Google ones a Google account. No card details, no trial, and nothing that auto-renews into a subscription.
Two honest caveats. The Semrush course page has a "Try Semrush free for seven days" footer, which is about the Semrush product and has nothing to do with the course, so do not let it talk you into a trial you did not want. And Google Ads certifications are valid for one year from the date you certify, after which you retake the assessment to renew (Google Ads Help).
all four links

1. Semrush, AI Search Operating System

Open the course · 40 minutes · 9 lessons · free, certificate included

What it is. A short course on getting a brand to show up inside AI answers rather than only inside blue links. Semrush frames it for "in-house SEO leads, digital marketing managers, and agency leaders who need to align their organization around AI search". It is taught by Semrush's own Director of Organic Visibility and their Head of Academy.

What it actually covers. The four layers of brand visibility, which Semrush names as discoverability, clarity, authority and trust, then how to audit a brand across all four, then how to turn that audit into KPIs and a 90-day plan. The nine lessons run 3, 5, 5, 8, 6, 11, 1, 1 and 0 minutes, which is where the 40 comes from.

What you walk away with. A Semrush Academy certificate after the exam, plus a template the course hands you in lesson one to build the plan alongside the videos.

This is the only one of the four that covers a topic that barely existed two years ago, which makes it the most useful thing to be able to say out loud in a meeting. Forty minutes is a lunch break.

2. Klaviyo, Digital marketing certificate

Open the certificate · 2 hours 30 minutes · 5 parts · free, final exam

What it is. A full-funnel foundations certificate. Klaviyo describes it as "designed for those who are developing their digital marketing from scratch or looking to learn foundational marketing concepts", built around the marketing funnel as the organising framework.

What it actually covers. Five parts: understand the marketing funnel (19 minutes), create awareness and interest in your brand (39 minutes), turn customer interest into action (25 minutes), retain customers long-term (36 minutes), then the conclusion and final exam (31 minutes). That is 150 minutes in total. The retention part is the strongest, which makes sense given what Klaviyo actually sells.

What you walk away with. The certificate, earned by passing the final exam rather than by watching to the end. Klaviyo's own wording: "Earn your Digital marketing certificate by completing the exam and demonstrating your marketing expertise."

Be honest with yourself about whether you need this one. If you have been running campaigns for a few years, parts one to three will be revision. The exam still counts and the certificate still lists, but do it at 1.5x and spend the saved time on the Google ones.

3. Google Ads Creative Certification

Open the certification · 3 hours 55 minutes · 2 sections, 5 activities · free

What it is. Google's certification on making ad creative that performs, across Video, Display, App and Search. Structured as an assessment plus an optional study section, so the time on the page is the full path rather than the minimum.

What it actually covers. Google lists three things it recognises you for: making effective ad creative across those four campaign types based on their research-backed guidance, using their tools throughout the creative life cycle, and boosting creative performance with experimentation. The experimentation piece is the part most people skip and the part that changes results.

What you walk away with. A Google Ads certification, valid for one year, that shows on your Skillshop profile and can be added to LinkedIn. You need 80% to pass and you get 75 minutes for the assessment.

Look at the structure before you panic at the run time. Section one is "Pass the assessment and earn a certification" and section two is "Study Google Ads Creative (optional)". If you already work in ads daily, you can sit the assessment first and only go back for the study material if you miss.

4. Google, AI-Powered Performance Ads Certification

Open the certification · 3 hours 58 minutes · 2 sections, 20 activities · free

What it is. The one that actually names the thing this whole list is about. Google's description: "Understand the importance of Google's cross-channel AI solutions and learn how to create and optimize these campaigns to achieve business goals."

What it actually covers. Five stated objectives, and they are worth reading in full because they are the closest thing to a job description for an AI marketer that Google publishes: describe the importance of AI throughout Google Ads campaigns, prepare an AI automation strategy that aligns to your business goals, identify the benefits of cross-channel AI-powered campaigns and tools, adopt an agile mindset and flexible budgets to respond to demand, and use Google Ads reporting and insights to bring transparency to Google's AI.

What you walk away with. A second Google Ads certification, also valid for one year. Twenty activities against the Creative certification's five, so it is the meatier of the two despite the near-identical run time.

That second objective, preparing an AI automation strategy, is the one to hold on to. It is the difference between letting Google's automation run on defaults and being the person who can explain what it is doing and why. There is a prompt below that turns it into an actual document.

turn them into proof

Two prompts that do the next bit for you

Finishing four certificates and then leaving them in a browser tab is the most common way this goes wrong. These two prompts take the finished work and turn it into something other people can see. Paste either into Claude or ChatGPT.

The first one writes your profile. It takes whichever of the four you completed and produces the LinkedIn entries, a headline, an About paragraph and two interview answers, so you are not staring at a blank field trying to make a 40 minute course sound impressive without overclaiming.

prompt 1 · turn the certificates into profile copy 
You are helping me present marketing certifications accurately and confidently on LinkedIn, without overclaiming.

Here is what I completed: [list the certificates you finished from these four, with the month you finished each: Semrush AI Search Operating System, Klaviyo Digital Marketing Certificate, Google Ads Creative Certification, Google AI-Powered Performance Ads Certification].
Here is my current role and what I actually do day to day: [describe your job in three or four sentences, including which channels you own and roughly what budget or list size you work with].

Produce four things.

First, a Licenses and certifications entry for each one, with the exact credential name, the issuing organisation, and a two-line description written in plain sentence case that says what the credential covers rather than what I feel about it. For the two Google ones, note that they are valid for 12 months.

Second, three alternative LinkedIn headlines under 220 characters that position me as the person on my team who understands how AI runs paid and organic, phrased as capability rather than as a job title I do not hold.

Third, one About-section paragraph of no more than 120 words that connects these certifications to something specific I already do in my role, so it reads as a direction I am already moving in rather than a list of courses.

Fourth, two interview answers of about 90 seconds each: one for "what have you actually done with AI in marketing" and one for "how do you decide when to let Google's automation run and when to override it". Ground both answers in my real role above, and flag clearly any sentence where you had to assume something I did not tell you.

The second one does the job the fourth certification trains you for. Google's objective is to "prepare an AI automation strategy that aligns to your business goals", so this prompt produces a first draft of that strategy for your actual account, which you then take into the course and correct as you learn.

prompt 2 · draft the AI automation strategy 
Act as a paid search strategist. I want a first-draft AI automation strategy for my Google Ads account that I can take into a review and defend, not a generic best-practices list.

My account: [describe the business, what you sell, your monthly budget, your campaign types, your current bidding strategies, your main conversion action, and what your cost per conversion looks like now].
My constraint: [name the real one, for example a fixed monthly budget, a long sales cycle, low conversion volume, seasonal demand, or limited creative capacity].

Write the strategy in five sections.

One, which parts of this account should be handed to Google's automation now, which should stay manual for the next quarter, and the specific reason for each call. Low conversion volume is a real reason to hold back on some automated bidding, so say so if it applies to me.

Two, what signals the automation needs from me to work, covering conversion tracking quality, conversion values, audience signals and creative volume, and tell me which of those I am most likely missing based on what I described.

Three, a 90-day sequence of changes, one per fortnight, each with the single metric that tells me whether it worked and the point at which I should roll it back.

Four, the three reports or insight views I should be checking weekly to keep visibility over what the automation is doing, and the specific number in each that would make me intervene.

Five, the two sentences I would say to my manager to explain this plan, and the strongest objection someone could raise against it.

Where my inputs were not enough to make a call, ask me at most three follow-up questions before writing, then write the full strategy.
after the certificates

Making them count

  1. 📌 List all four, but list them accurately. LinkedIn's Licenses and certifications section. The two Google ones are certifications with an expiry date, so fill in the expiry field rather than leaving it blank. Semrush and Klaviyo are course certificates. That distinction costs you nothing to state and it is the kind of precision people notice.
  2. 📅 Put the Google renewal in your calendar the day you pass. Twelve months, then you retake the assessment. A lapsed certification sitting on a profile is worse than never having listed it.
  3. 🔨 Run the AI search audit on your own brand. The Semrush course hands you a template and walks you through auditing across its four layers. Do it for the brand you actually work on, not a practice one, and bring the finding to your next team meeting. A real audit of your own brand is worth more in a performance review than all four certificates combined.
  4. 🗣 Volunteer for the thing nobody owns. Most marketing teams have an AI question sitting unclaimed, usually about whether the brand shows up in AI answers or what to do with automated campaigns. Having these four behind you is the reason to put your hand up for it.
