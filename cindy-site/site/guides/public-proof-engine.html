Start with one skill

Choose the skill where you can produce evidence this month:

  • marketing
  • design
  • AI systems
  • product
  • writing
  • gaming
  • research
  • coding
  • operations

Do not choose a broad identity such as “entrepreneur.” Choose work you can show.

The proof engine

SKILL
  → POINT OF VIEW
  → BUILD OR DEMO
  → PUBLISH THE DECISION
  → LOG THE EVIDENCE
  → IMPROVE THE NEXT BUILD

Your content is the explanation layer around real work.

1. Build the point-of-view bank

For each idea, answer:

Field Question
Observation What did you notice while doing the work?
Common advice What does the category usually say?
Disagreement Where does that advice break?
Evidence What result, source, or artifact supports you?
Consequence Why should the audience care?
Next action What can they do or test?

If the evidence is only a feeling, label it as an opinion. Do not turn it into a fact because the post sounds better.

2. Pick a weekly artifact

One artifact is enough:

  • before-and-after creative
  • one-page teardown
  • small app
  • workflow diagram
  • decision log
  • experiment result
  • template
  • checklist
  • prototype
  • case-study excerpt with permission

The artifact should exist before the post claims it works.

3. Capture the receipts

Create a Proof Ledger:

Claim Evidence link Baseline Result Timeframe Permission Boundary

Useful proof includes:

  • working demo
  • input and output
  • method
  • date
  • baseline
  • result
  • honest limitation
  • third-party source
  • testimonial with permission

Blur contracts, private client data, emails, payment details, and brand contacts.

4. Turn the artifact into content

Use this structure:

HOOK
The viewer’s problem, question, or desired result.

PROOF
Show the artifact early.

DECISION
Explain the choice that changed the result.

METHOD
Give the repeatable step, not every implementation detail.

BOUNDARY
Say what the proof does not establish.

CTA
Offer the template, checklist, or build notes.

Ask Claude:

Turn this real artifact into three short-form content angles.

ARTIFACT
[PASTE OR DESCRIBE]

EVIDENCE
[PASTE PROOF LEDGER ROW]

AUDIENCE
[PASTE]

For each angle give:
- a searchable on-screen title
- a spoken opener that does not repeat the title
- the proof shown in the first ten seconds
- the one decision worth teaching
- an honest boundary
- a useful CTA

Do not invent performance, income, personal history, or a customer quote.

5. Build in public without oversharing

Share:

  • the artifact
  • the decision
  • the mistake
  • the trade-off
  • the next experiment

Keep private:

  • credentials and access tokens
  • client or sponsor contracts
  • private email and contact details
  • confidential analytics
  • unapproved customer stories
  • unfinished legal or commercial negotiations

Four-week public-proof calendar

Week Build Post 1 Post 2 Proof to capture
1 Small artifact Why I built it Demo Input and finished output
2 Improve one flaw The mistake Before and after Change and result
3 Apply it to a new case What transferred What did not Second example
4 Package the method The framework The template Repeated pattern and limits

The honest cost

Cindy’s personal brand has led to hundreds of thousands in signed brand deals and multiple job offers. The useful lesson is not “post and opportunities appear.” Those results sat behind constant research, content systems, an editing team, commercial negotiation, and consistent publishing.

When using personal commercial proof:

  • name whether a commercial result is signed, invoiced, or paid
  • use an exact number only when you are ready to publish it and can show the underlying record safely
  • never show private deal rows
  • do not imply the same result is typical

Beginner rule

Start with the work that gives you energy. Showcase it, tell the story, teach one useful thing, and keep the receipt.

Instead of waiting for somebody to notice your potential, publish the evidence of what you can already do and what you are learning to do next.
