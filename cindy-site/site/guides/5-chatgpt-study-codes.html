how it works

Give it the thing you want to learn, then name the format

  1. 📚 Open ChatGPT and paste in your notes, a chapter, an article, or just type the topic or book title.
  2. ✍️ Type the code, like /mind map, then the topic. ChatGPT reads the word as your request and makes the image.
  3. 🔁 Want it sharper? Paste the full prompt for that code from below instead. It tells ChatGPT the layout, so you get a clean study page every time.
/cheat sheet one page with all the main points
/mind map how all the ideas connect
/flashcards questions to quiz yourself
/timeline everything in order
/blueprint how all the parts fit together
Images are on every ChatGPT plan, including free, with usage limits that depend on your account. You can also ask it to fix any label in the same chat.
1 · the quick summary

1. /cheat sheet cheat sheet

A one-page summary with all the main points, so you can revise in two minutes.

💡 /cheat sheet prompt 
/cheat sheet [your topic, book or pasted notes]

Make a one-page visual cheat sheet of this. Include the 6 to 8 most important points, each with a short heading, one plain-English sentence and one quick example. Add a small "key terms" box. Clean layout, easy-to-read text, portrait.
2 · see the big picture

2. /mind map mind map

A mind map diagram with the main idea in the middle and every key topic branching out from it.

💡 /mind map prompt 
/mind map [your topic, book or pasted notes]

Make a mind map diagram of this. Put the main idea in the centre, the 5 to 7 key topics on the first branches, and 2 or 3 short details on each branch. Use a different colour for each branch and keep every label short.
3 · actually remember it

3. /flashcards flashcards

Simple flashcards you can quiz yourself with. Testing yourself is one of the best ways to make things stick.

💡 /flashcards prompt 
/flashcards [your topic, book or pasted notes]

Make a page of 8 flashcards on this. Each card has a question on the front and a short answer on the back, shown side by side. Go from easiest to hardest, and cover the most important points first.
Why flashcards work: in a well known 2006 study, students who tested themselves remembered more a week later than students who just reread the material.
4 · put it in order

4. /timeline timeline

Everything laid out in order, which is perfect for history, a biography, or anything that happened over time.

💡 /timeline prompt 
/timeline [your topic, book or pasted notes]

Make a visual timeline of this, from earliest to latest. Show 6 to 10 key moments, each with the date or step number, a short title and one sentence on why it matters. Keep it clean and easy to read.
5 · how it all fits together

5. /blueprint blueprint

A colourful blueprint page showing how all the parts of your topic fit together, like how the Taj Mahal was built.

💡 /blueprint prompt 
/blueprint [your topic, book or pasted notes]

Make a colourful blueprint-style diagram of this. Show the main parts, label each one, and draw arrows to show how they connect or build on each other, with a one-line note on each part. Blueprint look, clean labels, portrait.
