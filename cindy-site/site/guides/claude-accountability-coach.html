Make Claude your accountability coach

💡 Why this one is worth the twenty minutes. The reason to build a tracker and a weekly review is not that it feels organised. A meta-analysis of 138 studies covering 19,951 people found that interventions which increased how often people monitored their progress also increased goal attainment (d+ = 0.40), and that the effect was larger "when the outcomes were reported or made public, and when the information was physically recorded" (Harkin et al., Psychological Bulletin, 2016). Written down and reported to someone is the mechanism. Claude is the someone you report to, and the file is the record.
the shape of it

What you are actually building

1. A plan built backwards From your real deadline and your honest weekly hours, not from the week you wish you had.
2. A tracker that counts sessions Sessions done, not perfect days. The unit has to be something you can hit on a bad week.
3. A weekly review Ten minutes where you paste what actually happened and get one adjustment back.
4. A miss protocol The prompt you run when you drop three days, so the plan re-fits instead of restarting.

Most planning systems die in the gap between two and three. People build the tracker, feel productive, and never review it, so nothing ever adapts. The review is the part that compounds, and it is why the setup below spends more time on it than on the plan.

🌱 Honest limits before you start. This is a planning and reflection tool, not treatment and not a diagnosis. If what is stopping you is a health issue, ADHD, burnout or anything closer to a mental health barrier than a scheduling one, a prompt is one small tool among several and it is not a substitute for real support from a professional. Claude cannot see whether you actually studied, so it is honest reporting that makes it work, not surveillance.
the setup

Set it up once, in about ten minutes

  1. 🆓 Open Claude on any plan. Nothing here needs a paid tier. Plans and usage limits: claude.com/pricing.
  2. 📁 Make a project, not a chat. Call it "Accountability". A project keeps your goal, your tracker and every review in one place, so week six can see week one. Any account can create projects, with free accounts capped at five (projects guide).
  3. 📌 Put the coaching rules in the project instructions. Paste the standing instruction below into the project's custom instructions so you never have to re-explain the tone. That box also lives at the account level under your initials in the lower left, then Settings, then Instructions for Claude, if you want it applied everywhere (personalization features).
  4. 🗓️ Book the review before you need it. Put a recurring 15 minute slot in your calendar, same day and time every week. The system fails at the review, so protect the review.
📌 the standing instruction 
You are my accountability coach for the goal in this project. Your job is to keep me moving, not to make me feel good about a plan I am not following.

Always work from the plan and the tracker in this project rather than from memory, and ask for the current numbers if you do not have them. When I report a week, respond with one honest read of what the data shows, then exactly one adjustment for the coming week, never a list of five improvements, because a list is how I end up changing nothing. Count effort in sessions completed, never in whether the week was perfect, and never suggest I start again from scratch.

Be warm and direct at the same time. Do not lecture me about discipline, do not use motivational filler, and if I have missed the same thing three weeks running, say so plainly and tell me the plan is wrong rather than telling me to try harder. Keep every response short enough that I actually read it on a bad day.
prompt 1

The intake: goal, deadline, honest schedule

Run this first, inside the project. The output is the plan every later prompt reads from. The part people get wrong is the schedule: give it the week you actually have, including the evenings you know you will be useless.

🎯 build my plan backwards 
I want to build a plan for this goal: [the goal, stated as something finished, for example "pass the four exams in my semester" or "publish twelve reels" or "ship the first version of my app"]. My hard deadline is [date]. Here is my honest weekly availability, not my aspirational one: [days and rough hours, including the times you know you will not work].

Work backwards from the deadline. Break the goal into the specific pieces of work that have to exist by the end, put them in the order they actually depend on each other, and tell me which piece is the one that quietly determines whether the whole thing lands. Then convert that into a weekly session count: how many working sessions per week, how long each one is, and what a single session is supposed to produce. Define a session as something I can finish in one sitting and tick off, never as a vague block of time.

Two rules while you do this. Assume I will lose roughly one week in every four to life, and build that slack in rather than planning for a perfect run. And if the deadline and my real availability do not fit, say so directly in the first line and tell me what has to shrink: the scope, the quality bar, or the deadline. Do not quietly compress the plan to make the numbers work. Finish by asking me up to three questions if anything important is missing.
🧠 If it tells you the plan does not fit, believe it. That single sentence is the most valuable output of the entire setup. Cutting scope in week one costs you a decision. Discovering the same thing in week nine costs you the goal.
prompt 2

The tracker that counts sessions, not perfection

The unit matters more than the tool. A tracker that measures "was today good" gives you a broken streak and a reason to quit. A tracker that counts completed sessions against a weekly target survives a terrible week, because a terrible week is a three out of five, not a failure.

📊 build my tracker 
Build me a progress tracker for the plan above, as a single self-contained HTML file I can save and open in my browser, with the data stored in the file so I can edit it in a text editor.

It needs: a header showing my goal, the deadline, and how many weeks are left. A row per week from now until the deadline, with columns for sessions targeted, sessions completed, what was produced that week in a few words, and a one line note. A running total of sessions completed against sessions targeted for the whole goal. And a simple visual bar showing progress towards the total, so I can see the shape of it at a glance.

Design rules. It has to count completed sessions, never streaks and never day-by-day perfection, because a broken streak is the thing that makes people quit. Show the running total as "42 of 96 sessions" rather than a percentage of days survived. Keep it plain and readable rather than decorative, and add a short comment at the top of the file explaining exactly how to update it each week.

Also give me a markdown version of the same table, so I can paste it back to you in our weekly reviews.
  1. 💾 Save the file where you will see it. Download the HTML file from the artifact and keep it on your desktop or in the folder you already work in. Claude renders single page HTML directly in an artifact window on every plan (artifacts overview).
  2. 📋 Keep the markdown version too. That is the one you paste into the weekly review. Two formats, one source of truth, no app to sign up for.
  3. ✅ Tick after each session, not at the end of the week. Thirty seconds in the moment, because a tracker filled in from memory on Sunday is fiction, and fiction is what the review then works from.
  4. 📎 Add it to the project knowledge. Drop the current markdown table into your project so Claude reads it without you pasting it every time, and replace it when the numbers change.
prompt 3

The weekly review, where the compounding happens

This is the part most people skip, and it is the part that turns a plan into a system. Fifteen minutes, same time every week, and you leave with exactly one thing changed.

🔁 my weekly review 
Weekly review. Here is what actually happened: I targeted [X] sessions and completed [Y]. Here is what I produced: [a few words per session, or what got finished]. Here is what got in the way, honestly: [the real reasons, including the boring ones].

Do four things, in this order and briefly. One, tell me where I am against the plan in a single sentence, using the session numbers rather than a judgement about my effort. Two, name the pattern you can see across the weeks so far, especially if the same day, time or type of session keeps failing, and say if it is too early to call a pattern. Three, give me exactly one adjustment for next week, and make it a change to the plan or the schedule rather than a change to my willpower. Four, tell me my session target for next week and what the first session should produce.

Do not give me a list of improvements, do not praise me for showing up, and if my completed sessions have been under target three weeks running, tell me the target is wrong and propose a lower one I would actually hit.
🌱 Report the bad weeks too, especially the bad weeks. A review where you only show up when it went well is a highlight reel, and a coach with a highlight reel gives you useless advice. The two out of five weeks are the ones that reveal the structural problem.
prompt 4

The miss protocol: re-plan, never restart

Everyone drops a few days. The damage is almost never the lost sessions, it is the restart: throwing out the plan, rebuilding it from scratch on a Sunday, and treating four days off as a reason to start again in January. This prompt exists so that never happens.

🩹 re-fit the plan 
I missed [number] days or sessions. Do not rebuild the plan from scratch and do not reset anything, re-fit what is left.

Take the sessions I still have to complete and the days I actually have left before the deadline, and tell me the new weekly session count. If that number is now higher than what I have ever actually hit in a week, say so plainly and give me two options instead of a fantasy schedule: what to cut from the scope, or what a realistic new deadline looks like. Be specific about what gets cut, name the actual piece of work, do not tell me to "reduce scope".

Then give me one small change that makes the next three days easier to start, for example a shorter first session or a different time of day. Keep it to a few lines and skip the encouragement, I want the maths and the next action.
📌 Run this the same day you notice, not on Sunday. The gap between missing and re-planning is where the guilt spiral lives, and the spiral costs far more sessions than the days you missed.
the craft

Four rules that keep it alive past week three

⏱️ Make the minimum session tiny Define a fallback session of fifteen minutes that still counts. On a wrecked day, a fifteen minute tick beats a zero, and the number in the tracker stays honest.
📝 One adjustment a week Change one thing, see if it worked, change the next. Five changes at once means you never learn which one mattered.
🗓️ Protect the review slot Same day, same time, in the calendar. When the review slips, the whole system quietly stops being a system.
🤝 Report it to a person too Send your session count to one friend each week. Monitoring works harder when the outcome is reported, and a human raises the stakes in a way a chat window does not.
make it one word

Optional: turn the review into a /checkin skill

Once the weekly review has run a few times, stop pasting the prompt. A skill is a small folder with a SKILL.md inside holding a name, a description of when to use it, and your instructions. Claude pulls it in when your request matches, so the review becomes one word. Skills work on every plan, Free through Enterprise, and there is no code involved.

⚡ build the check-in skill 
Build me a reusable Claude skill called "checkin" that runs my weekly accountability review.

Use skill-creator to write it. The SKILL.md description should say to use it whenever I type /checkin or say it is time for my weekly review. The instructions should say: first ask me for three things and nothing else, my target sessions, my completed sessions, and what actually got in the way. Then respond in four short parts, where I am against the plan in one sentence using session numbers, the pattern across the weeks so far, exactly one adjustment to the plan or schedule rather than to my willpower, and next week's target with what the first session should produce.

Add these standing rules to the skill: never suggest starting over, never give a list of improvements, never open with praise, and if completed sessions have been under target three weeks running, say the target is wrong and propose a lower one. Keep every response short. Then package it as a zipped folder I can upload.
  1. 📦 Take the folder it hands you. skill-creator finishes with a folder containing a SKILL.md. That folder is the skill.
  2. ➕ Add it to Claude. In the app, open Customize, then Skills, click Add, and choose Upload a skill to add the zip. In Claude Code, drop the folder into ~/.claude/skills/ instead. Anthropic's creating custom skills article has the same steps.
  3. 🔁 Edit it after a month. By then you know which of its questions you never answer honestly. Tell Claude what to change and have it rewrite the SKILL.md.
be honest about it

Where this works, and where it does not

✅ Genuinely good at this Goals with a real deadline and a countable unit of work: exams, a portfolio, a content batch, a certification, an app you are shipping.
🌱 Weaker here Vague goals with no unit ("be more disciplined"), anything needing in-the-moment intervention, and anything where the real blocker is health rather than scheduling.

Claude cannot see your screen, cannot verify a session happened, and will not chase you. What it does well is hold the whole plan in one place, notice patterns in your own reported numbers faster than you will, and give you a re-plan in thirty seconds so a missed week never turns into a quit.
