the basics

What this actually does

There is a real feature underneath this. Claude can reason through a problem step by step before it gives you a final answer, not just spit out a conclusion. Anthropic calls this extended thinking, and it moved to general availability on 26 June 2026. On the newest models it runs as adaptive thinking (Claude decides when to reason deeper, with low, medium, high, and max effort levels). Source: Anthropic extended thinking docs.

The trick is to prompt in a way that pulls that reasoning out where you can see it, instead of asking for a verdict. You are not asking Claude to decide. You are asking it to lay out the logic so you can decide.

copy this

The core prompt: argue both sides

Instead of asking "should I raise my prices," hand Claude your real numbers and ask it to argue both sides. Paste this in:

⚖️ argue both sides 
Here is my situation and my real numbers: [paste your context, e.g. current price, cost, volume, churn, goal].

Do not tell me what to do yet. First, argue both sides of this decision as strongly as you can. Then tell me what I would need to believe for each side to be the right call. End with the 2-3 assumptions the whole decision actually hinges on.
Why this works: it stops Claude from collapsing a messy trade-off into a confident one-liner. You get the reasoning on screen, the conditions under which each choice wins, and the cruxes you should actually go verify.
the safety net

The two lines that catch its guesses

Claude will sometimes fill gaps with a confident guess. Anthropic's own prompt guidance is to give the model explicit permission to say when it is unsure rather than speculate, which measurably reduces made-up answers. Source: Anthropic prompt engineering best practices. Add these two lines to any high-stakes prompt:

🔍 the two guard lines 
Show your work: walk me through your reasoning before your conclusion.

Flag anything you are unsure about or are assuming, and if the information is not enough to answer well, say so instead of guessing.
Now you can see where it is standing on solid ground versus where it is filling in blanks, before you act on it.

3 bonus prompts to run next

Same idea, different decisions. Each one keeps Claude in partner mode.

🛡️ steelman the other side 
Steelman the option I am leaning against. I am currently leaning towards [X]. Make the strongest possible case for [the opposite], as if you were trying to change my mind. Then tell me the one piece of evidence that would flip my decision.
⚰️ the pre-mortem 
Pre-mortem this plan. Assume it is 6 months from now and this decision failed badly. Walk backwards and tell me the 3 most likely reasons it went wrong, and the earliest signal I could have caught each one.
🎤 interrogate me first 
Interrogate me first. Before you give any recommendation, ask me the 5 questions you most need answered to give good advice here. Wait for my answers, then reason it through.
the craft

How to get the most out of it

  • 🌱 If you are newer to this: always paste in real specifics (your actual numbers, names, constraints). A partner prompt on vague inputs gives you vague reasoning. Real context is what turns Claude from a generic advisor into one that reasons about your situation.
  • ⚡ If you are a power user: on the newest Claude models, watch the visible thinking / reasoning steps and turn up the effort level for genuinely hard calls, then read the thinking, not just the answer. The reasoning trace is where you catch a flawed assumption early. You can also chain these: argue both sides, then feed its own output back with "now pre-mortem the option you leaned towards."
skip it when

When not to bother

For simple, low-stakes, or factual lookups ("what's the formula for", "reword this"), skip all of this and just ask. Partner mode is for decisions with real trade-offs, where the reasoning matters more than a fast answer. Using it everywhere just slows you down.
