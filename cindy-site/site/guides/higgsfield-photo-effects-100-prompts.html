The short names are labels for the ideas. They are not verified built-in slash commands. Paste the full prompt so the image or video tool receives the actual direction, including what should stay unchanged.

Set up the generation route

  1. Open the Plugins area in ChatGPT, find Higgsfield and choose the available Add or Install option. If you do not see that area, start from Higgsfield's connection page and follow the instructions available for your account.
  2. Connect or sign in to your Higgsfield account when prompted, then start a new conversation. You can find the service at Higgsfield (my affiliate link). I may earn a commission if you purchase through this link, at no extra cost to you.
  3. Attach your source photograph and say whether you want an image or video. For a video, explicitly ask to generate through Higgsfield. For a still, choose the image-generation tool available in your chat, or the appropriate Higgsfield image tool if connected.
  4. Paste one full prompt. Check the selected model, supported duration, aspect ratio and credit cost before generation. A text description of a shot is not the generated clip, so confirm that the generation tool was actually used.

The setup labels can vary by account and rollout. OpenAI's plugin guidance and Higgsfield's connection instructions are the current setup references. A particular ChatGPT model choice is not listed as a requirement in Higgsfield's setup instructions. Product access and generation credits are separate from the wording of your prompt, and connecting a tool does not make every generation free.

Choose a source that supports the shot

Use a sharp image with an unobstructed subject, enough space around the edges and lighting that makes the important details visible. For a product, include the label and any surfaces you want to show. For a person, use a photograph you have permission to animate. Two or three clear references are more useful than asking a model to guess an unseen face angle, clasp or internal mechanism.

A request for an orbit or exploded arrangement produces a rendered visual. It does not automatically create a usable 3D asset, reconstruct accurate engineering or prove how the product is assembled. If you need exact component relationships, supply separate part photographs or a reliable assembly reference.

The prompts below describe the intended result, not a guarantee that a model will preserve every letter or movement. Start with one small generation. Check the subject, label, hands and contact points before extending the shot or spending credits on variations.

The four effects from the reel

Product explosion

Output: a still image showing a conceptual exploded arrangement. Supply: the complete product plus separate photographs or a reliable reference of the parts you want separated. You can animate the approved image afterwards if you want motion.

copy this prompt
Using the attached complete-product photo and component references, create one vertical still image of the product in an orderly exploded arrangement against a clean charcoal background. Separate only the documented components by small, clearly visible gaps, preserving their relative orientation, proportions, materials and existing label. Use soft studio lighting and restrained shadows to make the arrangement readable. Do not invent internal mechanisms, hidden layers or included accessories. If the photographs do not establish separable parts, keep the product intact and arrange only the supplied accessories around it. This is a conceptual rendered illustration, not an engineering reconstruction or a usable 3D model. No added text.

If it adds unfamiliar parts, simplify the arrangement and supply individual component images. A single exterior photo is not enough evidence for an accurate internal build. For video, animate the approved arrangement with a small separation movement and the camera fixed.

Man POV

Output: a first-person video from the viewpoint of the person in your supplied photo, with a smart-glasses-style camera position. Supply: the scene photo and any extra reference showing what is in front of that person.

copy this prompt
Using the attached photograph, generate a short vertical video through Higgsfield from the first-person viewpoint of the person shown, as if a small camera were mounted at their eye level on smart glasses. Carry forward the photographed setting, activity and visible clothing rather than moving them to an unrelated location. Show the space directly ahead with a subtle natural head movement and one simple action supported by the reference. Include hands only if that action needs them, matching supplied references. Do not show an invented face, add extra fingers or fabricate readable signs. If the person's forward view is not established, ask for that reference before generating. No cuts, captions or audio.

POV describes where the camera is. A photograph of someone does not necessarily show what they can see, so add a forward-facing scene reference when the view is missing. Keep the first action small enough to inspect.

Orbit shot

Output: a full 360-degree camera orbit around a stationary subject. Supply: clear front, side and rear photographs, ideally with consistent lighting and the intended background.

copy this prompt
Using the attached front, side and rear reference photographs, generate a vertical video through Higgsfield in which the camera completes one smooth full 360-degree orbit around the stationary subject and returns to its starting view. Choose a supported duration long enough to keep the movement readable. Maintain a consistent camera height, subject scale, lighting and coherent background throughout. Keep the subject anchored in place without rotating it independently. Match the supplied surfaces, proportions and identifying details as each angle appears. Do not invent hidden product mechanisms or additional accessories. Hold briefly at the start and end. Produce one continuous rendered shot without cuts, captions or audio.

This is a generated video, not a recovered 3D model. Even with several references, views between them are inferred and may contain errors. If you only have a front photo, use a smaller 20-degree arc as a fallback and keep it from revealing unsupported rear details. Check every side of a full orbit, not only its first frame.

Slow motion

Output: one simple action played slowly. Supply: a photo that clearly shows the object and the surface where the action will happen.

copy this prompt
Using the attached product photo, generate a short vertical video through Higgsfield of one narrow strip of lightweight tissue paper drifting slowly down beside the stationary product. Let it fold naturally in the air, touch the tabletop and settle. Keep the product completely unchanged and the camera locked at a gentle three-quarter angle. Use soft side lighting that makes the paper's movement visible without covering the label. Show a smooth slow-motion appearance with plausible gravity and contact, rather than suspending everything indefinitely. Do not add liquid, flying product parts or multiple competing actions. Finish with a brief still hold, without captions or audio.

Slow motion works best when the viewer can follow one action. If the model struggles, use a shorter drop and keep the camera still.

How to use the 100 additional prompts

These are 100 additional image and video prompts, separate from the four demos: 55 video prompts and 45 still-image prompts. Each entry names an image or video output. The categories help you find a job quickly: portraits, optical effects, constructed product sets, hands, food, fashion, spaces, layouts and transitions.

For entries that need a specific subject, supply it. The zipper prompt needs a visible zipper; the two-scene transition needs two photographs. If the input does not fit, choose another prompt rather than asking the tool to invent the missing evidence. Portrait treatments and mock layouts are editorial visuals, not records of real print publication or product performance.

For transitions 91–100, supply the two or three photographs named in the prompt and choose a model that accepts that reference workflow. Start-frame, end-frame and multi-image controls vary by model. If your model cannot use the required inputs, create separate shots and assemble the transition in your video editor instead.

For video, ask Higgsfield to use a short duration supported by your selected model. For still images, keep the request to one finished image. Most prompts use vertical framing; two editorial layouts explicitly use horizontal framing. You can change the requested framing to square or landscape when your destination requires it. Add only one revision at a time so you know what improved the result.

Make the next version more specific

If the result looks generic, change one concrete thing: the camera position, the motion, the light direction or the background. If the subject changes, shorten the movement and strengthen the source reference instead of adding more adjectives.

Before using a clip, inspect its beginning, middle and end. Look for drifting facial features, label changes, extra fingers, moving contact points and objects passing through each other. For stills, inspect at full size and at the size people will see on their phone. Add important text and logos in your design editor when exact reproduction matters.
