The template to steal

📋 the fill-in-the-blank template 
You are [role]. You are writing for [audience].
Here is my context: [voice, docs, past work].
Do this: [the task], following these rules: [length, format, tone, what to avoid].
Here is an example of good: [example].
Now write it, then think step by step.

What goes in each bracket:

  1. 🎭 [role] who Claude should be (a founder, a conversion copywriter, a researcher). This sets the altitude of the answer.
  2. 👥 [audience] who the output is for. Writing for early-stage founders reads completely differently to writing for beginners.
  3. 📁 [context] your voice, your docs, your past work. Paste real material, this is the single biggest quality jump.
  4. 📏 [rules] length, format, tone, and what to AVOID. Telling Claude what you do not want is a cheat code.
  5. ⭐ [example] show it one example of what good looks like. It will match the pattern.
  6. 📨 The ask end with the actual request, then say "think step by step".
the reason

Why this works: the 6 parts

Most great prompts have the same 6 parts: role and task, audience, context, rules, an example, and the ask. You do not need all 6 every time. The more of them that matter to your task, the better the answer. A bad prompt pulls the average of the whole internet. A great one pulls your angle.

The 3 examples from the carousel

1. A LinkedIn post

❌ Bad: Write a LinkedIn post about niching down.

👍 Good: Write a LinkedIn post on why founders should niche down early. Keep it practical and under 3,000 characters.

🏆 Great:

1 · the great LinkedIn post 
You are a founder writing for early-stage founders. Explain why niching down
early speeds up growth. Short paragraphs, practical, experience-led not
motivational. Here are two hook ideas to pick from: [hook 1] / [hook 2].
Write it under 3,000 characters, then think step by step.

2. A marketing plan

❌ Bad: Give me a marketing plan.

👍 Good: Create a marketing strategy for a startup selling [product].

🏆 Great:

2 · the great marketing plan 
You are a founder building a marketing strategy. Product: [product].
Audience: [audience]. Budget: [budget]. Design a plan to get the first
1,000 customers. Focus on the highest-leverage channels only. I want
practical first actions, not a generic full-funnel plan. Write it, then
think step by step.

3. Competitor analysis

❌ Bad: Analyze my competitors.

👍 Good: Analyze these competitors and explain their strengths and weaknesses.

🏆 Great:

3 · the great competitor analysis 
You are a founder analyzing competitors in your market. Company: [you].
Competitors: [list]. Compare their positioning, pricing, and marketing.
Find their biggest advantage and biggest weakness. I do not want
surface-level notes, I want where they are genuinely stronger and where
there is an opening. Write the analysis, then think step by step.
more examples

5 more worked examples (the ones I promised)

4. A welcome email sequence

❌ Bad: Write a welcome email.

👍 Good: Write a 3-email welcome sequence for people who downloaded my free guide about [topic].

🏆 Great:

4 · the great welcome sequence 
You are an email marketer writing for [audience] who just downloaded
[freebie]. Here is my voice: [paste 3 lines of your own writing]. Write a
3-email welcome sequence: email 1 delivers the freebie and one quick win,
email 2 tells my story in 150 words, email 3 makes one soft offer for
[product]. Rules: under 120 words each, plain text, one idea per email,
no hype words like "unlock" or "game-changer". Here is a subject line
style I like: [example]. Write all three, then think step by step.

5. Content ideas that actually fit your niche

❌ Bad: Give me content ideas.

👍 Good: Give me 10 Instagram reel ideas about [your niche] for beginners.

🏆 Great:

5 · the great content ideas 
You are a short-form content strategist for [your niche]. My audience:
[who]. Here are my 3 best-performing posts: [paste hooks or links]. Give
me 10 reel ideas ranked by likely watch time, each with a first-line hook
and the payoff moment. Rules: specific over generic, no motivational
filler, every idea must show something on screen. Match the pattern of my
winners. Write the list, then think step by step.

6. Landing page copy

❌ Bad: Write landing page copy for my product.

👍 Good: Write landing page copy for [product], aimed at [audience], with a headline and three benefits.

🏆 Great:

6 · the great landing page 
You are a conversion copywriter. Product: [product]. Audience: [audience]
whose main pain is [pain]. Structure: hero headline + subline, 3 benefit
blocks written as outcomes not features, one social-proof line, one CTA.
Rules: grade-6 reading level, no buzzwords, headline under 10 words. Here
is a headline style I love: [example]. Write the page, then think step by
step.

7. Making sense of customer feedback

❌ Bad: Summarize this feedback.

👍 Good: Summarize these survey answers and tell me the main themes.

🏆 Great:

7 · the great feedback report 
You are a customer researcher. Here are [number] raw survey answers:
[paste]. Cluster them into themes ranked by how often they appear, with a
verbatim quote for each theme. Then flag the 2 most surprising answers
and what they suggest I should change. I do not want vague sentiment like
"users want more features", I want specific, quotable findings. Write the
report, then think step by step.

8. Meeting notes into an action plan

❌ Bad: Summarize this meeting.

👍 Good: Summarize these meeting notes and list the action items.

🏆 Great:

8 · the great action plan 
You are my chief of staff. Here are the raw notes from [meeting]: [paste].
Split everything into three lists: decisions made, open questions, and
action items with an owner and a deadline for each. Table format. If an
action item has no owner, flag it in bold instead of guessing. Do not
write a prose recap. Build the lists, then think step by step.

Bonus tricks

🚫 The "do NOT want" cheat code. The fastest quality upgrade in any prompt is one line about what to avoid. Steal these: "I do not want a generic listicle." / "Do not pad the answer, shorter is better." / "No motivational filler, practical only."
🪄 Make Claude upgrade your prompt for you. Paste this: "Here is my prompt: [your lazy prompt]. Rewrite it as a great prompt using this structure: role, audience, context, rules, an example, then the ask ending with think step by step. Ask me for any bracket you cannot fill." Claude will interview you and hand back the great version.
The 5-second checklist before you hit enter: did I say who Claude is, who it's for, what context it has, what rules to follow, and what good looks like? Even hitting 3 of the 5 puts you ahead of almost everyone.
