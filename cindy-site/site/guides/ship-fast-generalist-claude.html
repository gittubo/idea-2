the reasoning

Why the generalist wins

The roles that hold up in an AI-heavy company are the ones where one person can do what used to take a whole team: think through the product, design it, build it, and ship it. AI collapsed the old walls between the engineer, the designer, and the PM, so the edge now belongs to the person who is comfortable using AI tools and happy to build, break, and ship fast.

You don't get there by memorising which jobs are "safe." You get there by proving you can go from idea to shipped thing. This guide gives you the muscle: describe what you want in plain words, let Claude write the spec, hand it to a builder, and fix it live when it breaks.

the loop

The core loop (Claude = brain, builder = hands)

🧠 Claude thinks and plans You describe the messy idea, Claude interviews you until it actually understands it, then writes a clear spec (what it does, the screens, the data, the steps).
⚙️ A builder does the hands-on part You paste that spec into a no-code AI app builder (Lovable, Bolt, or Replit Agent) and it generates a real, working web app you can click.
🔧 Claude fixes what breaks The app will break. You copy the error, paste it back into Claude, and it tells you exactly what to change. This "break and ship fast" step is the part most reels skip, and it's the whole skill.
🚀 You test and ship Open the result, try the one job it is meant to do, and give the builder one concrete correction at a time. A small working version teaches you more than another week of planning.
copy this

Step 1: turn your idea into a spec with Claude

Open a new Claude chat (claude.ai, free tier is fine to start) and paste this. The trick is making Claude interview you FIRST instead of guessing, so the plan is actually right.

📝 the spec interview 
I want to build a small web app but I'm not technical. Here's the messy idea in my head: [describe your idea in 2-3 sentences, e.g. "a simple tool where I paste a client's website and it spits out 5 content ideas"].

Before you write anything, ask me one question at a time until you fully understand what I want, who it's for, and what the single most important thing it needs to do. When you have enough, stop asking and write me a plain-English build spec: the app in one sentence, the screens, what data it stores, and the exact steps a no-code AI builder should follow to create the first version. Keep it simple enough to ship this week.
💡 Why the "one question at a time" line matters: it forces Claude into interview mode instead of dumping assumptions, so the spec matches what's actually in your head. This is the same spec-driven approach developers use with Claude Code, changing the plan costs nothing, changing the built code costs hours (code.claude.com/docs).
the hands

Step 2: hand the spec to a builder

Copy Claude's spec, open one of these, paste it in, and let it generate the first version. All three have a real free tier so you can build your tiny thing without paying.

  • 🟣 Lovable (lovable.dev) is the friendliest for total beginners. You describe the app in plain language and it builds a working React app with a database, login, and hosting. Free plan gives you 5 build credits a day (up to about 30 a month), enough to answer "can this build what I need?" before you pay. Pro is $25/mo.
  • ⚡ Bolt (bolt.new) is great when you want to build AND tweak in the browser. Free tier is 300K tokens a day (about 1M a month), which stretches to several small prototypes. Pro is $25/mo with token rollover.
  • 🟢 Replit Agent (replit.com) is the most "real developer" of the three and good if your idea needs a backend or scheduled jobs. The free Starter plan includes limited daily Agent credits, 1,200 dev minutes a month, and lets you publish one app. Core is $25/mo with $20 of usage credits.
  • 🎯 Which to pick: start with Lovable if you just want a clean app fast, Bolt if you like seeing and editing the build, Replit if your idea is more of a tool or automation. You can literally paste the same Claude spec into all three and keep the one you like.
the real skill

Step 3: break it and fix it live

This is the part that turns you into the generalist. When the app errors out or does the wrong thing, don't panic, feed it back to Claude:

🔧 the fix-it prompt 
I'm building this app in [Lovable / Bolt / Replit] using the spec you wrote. It broke. Here's exactly what happened and the error message: [paste the error or describe what went wrong].

Tell me in plain steps what to change and where. If it's a prompt I should give the builder, write that exact prompt for me. Assume I'm not technical, so be specific about what to click or paste.
🔁 Loop this until it works. Every time you fix one bug, you learn the mechanism, and that's the "build, break, ship fast" muscle nobody explains.
go further

3 bonus prompts to run next

🔍 Pressure-test the idea before you build:

1 · pressure-test the idea 
Here's my app idea: [idea]. Play skeptical product lead. Tell me the smallest possible version I could ship this week, what to cut, and the one feature that actually makes it useful. Then rewrite my idea as that smaller version.

🎨 Get a design brief so it doesn't look generic:

2 · the design brief 
Based on the spec you wrote, give me a short design brief I can paste into the builder: colour palette, font vibe, layout of the main screen, and the tone of the copy. Keep it clean and modern, not templated.

🚀 Plan your "ship it" moment:

3 · the ship-it checklist 
The first version works. Give me a 5-step checklist to actually share it: what to test, how to publish it from [Lovable/Bolt/Replit], and one line I could post to show what I built.
pick your level

How to get the most out of it

🌱 If you're brand new pick the tiniest idea you can (a one-screen tool), do steps 1 to 3 once, and ship something ugly. The goal this week is not a great app, it's proving to yourself you can go idea to shipped. That belief is the whole unlock.
⚡ If you already build use Claude as the spec-and-review layer, not just the generator. Have it write the spec, then after the builder creates the app, paste the code or a screenshot back and ask Claude "what would a senior engineer flag here?" You get a second brain that catches the AI slop before you ship it.
the honest bit

An honest heads-up

AI builders are amazing for a first working version, but they can get stuck in loops on anything complex, and credits burn fast when you iterate a lot. Keep your first build small, lean on Claude to un-stick the builder, and treat the free tiers as "prove the idea," not "run a business." The skill you're building is judgment, knowing what to ship, what to cut, and when the output is actually good.
