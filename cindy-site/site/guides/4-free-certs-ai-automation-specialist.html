4 free certificates to become an AI automation specialist

Hey, it's Cindy 🌱 Here are all four courses from the reel, with the direct link, the real time each one takes, and what you actually walk away holding.

the short version

The whole thing in 4 steps

  1. 🔑 Set up your accounts first. UiPath Academy and Workato Academy both need a free login before the courses will open, and Workato needs a workspace on top of that. Doing this before you sit down saves you stopping halfway through.
  2. 🧠 Start with UiPath for the concepts. It is the only one of the four that explains what agentic automation actually is before handing you a tool.
  3. 🛠 Build twice, in n8n and Make. This is where it becomes a real skill. Both end with a thing you built that runs.
  4. 🏢 Finish with Workato. The most enterprise-flavoured of the four, and the one that looks best next to a corporate job title.
all four links

1. UiPath, Introduction to agentic automation

Open the course · 1 hour 5 minutes · beginner

What it is. The concepts course. It covers what agentic automation means, how an agent differs from a fixed rule-based robot, and where each one belongs. Doing it first means the next three stop feeling like unrelated software tutorials and start feeling like one skill.

What you walk away with. A UiPath diploma, unlocked when you complete the course. The page reads "Complete the course to unlock Get your diploma!".

The honest bit. This is a diploma, not a UiPath certification. UiPath's proctored certification exams are a separate paid thing. Describe it as "UiPath Academy diploma" and you are on solid ground. The courses themselves are genuinely free: UiPath's FAQ says "All UiPath Academy courses and learning plans are currently free of charge" (source).

2. n8n Quickstart (QS101)

Open the course · n8n education hub · 3 to 5 hours · no coding needed to start

What it is. The first one where you build. You finish it holding a working AI agent that can look up customer records and answer questions about them, which is a genuinely demonstrable thing rather than a quiz score.

What you walk away with. A completion certificate and a verifiable badge. n8n's own wording: "Every course is free for everyone, and you earn a completion certificate and a verifiable badge for each one you finish."

The honest bit. n8n says plainly on the course page that Quickstart is not a certification, it is a badge plus a certificate of completion. The 3 to 5 hour figure is the course FAQ's own estimate, and it is the longest of the four, so give it a real afternoon. There are three more courses after it (N8N101, 102 and 103) at learn.n8n.io if you want to keep going.

This is the one to do properly rather than fast. The agent you build here is the single best thing on this list to screen-record and show someone, because it visibly works.

3. Make Foundation

Open the learning path · 3.5 hours · new to Make

What it is. Six short courses plus an assessment, ending with your first scenario built and running. Make's visual canvas is the friendliest of the four to look at, which is why it is worth doing straight after n8n rather than instead of it: you see the same ideas in two different shapes and they stick.

What you walk away with. The Make Foundation badge, issued through Credly. The page describes it as a "Make Foundation badge to post on socials", so it is built to be shared.

The honest bit. This is a badge rather than a certificate, and Make Academy is free with no catch: their FAQ says "Yes, 100% free. No subscriptions, no surprises" (source). Free Make accounts do have a monthly operations limit, which is plenty for learning but worth knowing before you build anything real on it.

4. Workato Foundations Level 1

Open Workato Academy · 3 hours, plus a free exam · enterprise flavoured

What it is. The most corporate of the four, and deliberately last. Workato is what larger companies actually run, so this is the one that reads best next to an operations or IT job title. It is video lessons, study materials, interactive labs and tests, then a separate exam.

What you walk away with. A completion certificate. The course and the Level 1 exam are both listed as free in the Academy catalog.

The honest bit. This is free but gated. You need a Workato workspace before you can access the courses at all. Workato's docs say "If you don't have a workspace, you can create a new workspace or request a free demo workspace" (source). Requesting the demo workspace is not instant, so start this one a few days before you plan to sit down with it.

Do this bit first. Request the free Workato demo workspace on day one, before you start UiPath. By the time you have worked through the other three, your workspace will be waiting instead of blocking you.
be accurate

What each one actually issues

These four are all free and all worth doing, but they are not the same kind of credential. Describing them accurately is a small thing that makes you look like you know what you are talking about.

UiPath A diploma from UiPath Academy. Their paid proctored exams are the separate thing called a certification.
n8n A certificate of completion plus a verifiable badge. n8n states on the page that it is not a certification.
Make The Make Foundation badge, issued through Credly, designed to be posted publicly.
Workato A completion certificate from Workato Academy, earned after the free Level 1 exam.
what it is for

Turning four badges into work

LinkedIn's 2026 Jobs on the Rise list for the US has AI consultants and strategists near the top, described as people who "help organizations plan and implement AI technologies to improve operations and reach their goals" (source). The badges are not what gets you that work. The thing you built in n8n is.

  1. 🎥 Record the agent you built. Thirty seconds of your n8n agent answering a question about a customer record does more than four badge images ever will.
  2. 📌 List the credentials properly. LinkedIn's Licenses and certifications section, using the credential URL from each badge, not a screenshot in a post.
  3. 🔁 Rebuild one automation for a real business. A friend's booking reminders, an invoice chaser, a lead router. One finished example beats a list of tools you have heard of.
