The one rule that decides whether this works. Do not try all five. The reason most people make nothing with AI is that they hop between all of these and go deep on none. Pick one this week, commit to it for 90 days, and ignore the other four until it is working.
5 paths

The 5 paths, lowest barrier to biggest ceiling

  1. 🛒 Sell tiny AI products. Prompt packs, Notion templates, small AI tools. The lowest barrier because you can ship one this week. Start free on Gumroad, which costs nothing until you actually make a sale, so there is no risk to launching.
  2. 💼 Freelance your AI skills. Do the AI task businesses do not have time for. On Upwork, demand for AI skills jumped 109% year over year, and AI video is one of the fastest-growing gigs on the platform (Upwork In-Demand Skills 2026). Set up a profile and pitch one narrow service.
  3. 🎬 Make faceless AI videos. Build a niche channel where AI does the heavy lifting and you never show your face. Adam Del Duca teaches the whole faceless YouTube playbook for free (search his name on YouTube), which is more than enough to start.
  4. 🏢 Automate local businesses. Sell simple automations to the gyms, clinics, and salons near you. Nick Saraev walks through the exact cold email and the build, free on YouTube at @nicksaraev. Higher ceiling because these clients pay real retainers.
  5. 🚀 Build an AI automation agency. The biggest ceiling: recurring clients, real systems, a team eventually. Liam Ottley's free Skool has a 57-video course and over 300,000 people doing exactly this (AI Automation Agency Hub).

4 first offers you could launch this week

If you picked selling products or freelancing, here are four things simple enough to put out in days, not months.

A prompt pack 20-30 genuinely useful prompts for one specific job (real estate agents, teachers, coaches). Sell it on Gumroad.
A Notion template An AI-powered tracker or content system. Templates sell well because buyers want the structure, not to build it.
A 15-minute AI audit Review a small business's workflow and hand them three automations to run. Easy first freelance offer.
One done-for-you automation Pick a single painful task (follow-ups, lead replies) and sell the setup. Narrow beats broad when you are starting.
Momentum comes from shipping one small thing, not from planning the perfect business. Launch the tiny version this week, get one real bit of feedback, and improve from there.
